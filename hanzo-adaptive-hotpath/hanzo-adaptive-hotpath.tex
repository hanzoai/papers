\begin{abstract}
End-to-end latency in multi-turn coding agents is governed jointly by CPU frontend hot paths (prompt tokenization, chat-template rendering, multimodal tensor preparation, and KV-block routing) and GPU speculative decode efficiency across alternating explanatory prose and grounded code edits. We describe a set of native Rust systems shipped in \texttt{hanzoai/engine} (commit \texttt{36cd0bb3c}) and \texttt{hanzoai/ml} (commit \texttt{c9bee7434}): (1) an incremental $\mathcal{O}(1)$ amortized \texttt{SuffixAutomaton} maintaining most-recent occurrence positions (\texttt{last\_end}) via lockstep 2-gram intersection and rollback checkpoints, accelerating lookup misses by $26.8\times$--$34.8\times$ across 1K--16K token contexts; (2) \texttt{SA\_SPEC} suffix bypass combined with an EWMA cooldown regression guard (\texttt{GuardedDraft} / \texttt{AdaptiveSpeculativeGuard}) that gates neural draft forwards and single-token CUDA decode-graph capture while unconditionally verifying every proposed token under target-model logits; (3) two speculative verification bugfixes resolving \texttt{top\_k == 1} argmax routing in \texttt{Sampler::is\_argmax} and nonzero \texttt{layout.start\_offset()} pointer indexing across CUDA top-$k$/top-$1$ kernels; (4) a clean-room pure-Rust \texttt{hanzo-tokenizer} crate with packed 2-bit Unicode class scanners, open-addressed BPE merges, and a self-bypassing \texttt{PromptTokenCache}; (5) SIMD image resize and planar normalization in \texttt{hanzo-vision} plus cached \texttt{minijinja} templates; and (6) an epoch-fenced KV directory and \texttt{HKV1} async TCP transport in \texttt{hanzo-kv-fabric}. Measured on an NVIDIA Grace-Blackwell GB10 node with a 4B ternary hybrid-attention model (\texttt{zen6-nano-4b-PQ2\_0-MTP-Q8\_0.gguf}) over a warm 20-turn coding-agent conversation, full adaptive speculation lifts draft acceptance from $21.3\%$ to $49.2\%$, reduces total wall-clock time from $75{,}036.1\,\text{ms}$ (unguarded MTP) and $65{,}648.7\,\text{ms}$ (uncached decode) to $38{,}190.3\,\text{ms}$ ($1.96\times$ and $1.72\times$ speedups, respectively), and lowers grounded edit inter-token latency from $27.57\,\text{ms}$ to $2.79\,\text{ms}$ ($2.59\,\text{ms}$ under standalone lookup). We report negative and neutral results explicitly, including unguarded MTP's $1.68\times$ slowdown against cached decode on prose-heavy turns and a $0.82\times$ regression on $3\times$ CPU upsampling.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Multi-turn coding assistants alternate between two distinct execution regimes within a single session. Explanatory turns synthesize novel prose tokens with low $n$-gram overlap against the context window, whereas diff and file-edit turns reproduce substantial spans of identifiers, signatures, and configuration keys already resident in prompt history. Running an unguarded neural Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) drafter uniformly across both regimes introduces unnecessary draft forward passes and target hidden-state transfers when acceptance falls below breakeven, slowing wall-clock delivery relative to plain unspeculated decoding~\cite{leviathan2023speculative,chen2023accelerating}. Meanwhile, re-tokenizing growing chat histories via backtracking regular expressions, repeatedly compiling Jinja chat templates, and executing scalar image preprocessors accumulate avoidable host latency before GPU execution even begins.

This paper documents the architecture and measured behavior of the native Rust hot-path stack implemented in \texttt{hanzoai/engine} (\texttt{36cd0bb3c}) and \texttt{hanzoai/ml} (\texttt{c9bee7434})~\cite{hanzo2026engine}. Every component runs without C/C++ FFI dependencies in its default configuration, preserves target-model output equivalence under verification, and guards fast paths against adverse workloads through automatic bypass and fallback policies. All measurements in this study come directly from the checked-in Rust harnesses \texttt{hotpath\_bench}, \texttt{kv\_fabric\_bench}, \texttt{upsample\_nearest2d\_bench}, and \texttt{zen6\_agent\_20turn\_bench} on hardware recorded in Section~\ref{sec:eval}.

\section{Incremental Suffix Automaton, \texttt{SA\_SPEC}, and \texttt{GuardedDraft}}

\subsection{Most-Recent \texttt{SuffixAutomaton} with Lockstep 2-Gram Intersection}
Standard prompt lookup scans backwards linearly through the token prefix $T_{0:L}$ on every decode step, costing $\mathcal{O}(L \cdot n_{\max})$ per probe and spiking whenever no long $n$-gram match exists. Textbook suffix automata~\cite{blumer1985suffix} support $\mathcal{O}(n_{\max})$ queries and $\mathcal{O}(1)$ amortized incremental updates, yet their standard construction tracks \texttt{first\_pos}, whereas speculative continuation from repetitive codebases prefers the \emph{most recent} occurrence (\texttt{last\_end}) of the longest matching context suffix.

Our native \texttt{SuffixAutomaton} (\texttt{suffix\_automaton.rs}) maintains exact most-recent occurrence retrieval over $T_{0:L-1}$ with incremental append and rollback checkpoints for rejected draft tokens:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Inline transitions (\texttt{SmallEdges})}: Each state stores up to four \texttt{(token\_id, next\_state)} pairs inline before spilling to an \texttt{AHashMap}, avoiding heap allocations on sparse internal states.
  \item \textbf{Exact \texttt{last\_end} maintenance}: Leaf extensions propagate directly through \texttt{last\_1gram} and \texttt{last\_2gram} maps backed by a 1-based exclusive end-position chain (\texttt{prev\_2gram}). For internal automaton states with match length $n \ge 3$, exact most-recent match location is resolved without false positives by intersecting backward 2-gram occurrence chains from the match tail and head in lockstep, preserving 100\% parity with brute-force reverse $n$-gram lookup.
  \item \textbf{Undo checkpoints}: Appending tokens pushes compact \texttt{Checkpoint} records tracking state count, link modifications (\texttt{link\_undo}), and edge updates (\texttt{edge\_undo}), allowing speculative verification rejections to roll back automaton state in $\mathcal{O}(\gamma)$ time rather than rebuilding across $L$ tokens.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{\texttt{SA\_SPEC} Neural Draft Bypass and \texttt{AdaptiveSpeculativeGuard}}
When an autoregressive model exposes both a context \texttt{SuffixAutomaton} and a neural draft head (such as an MTP head or block-diffusion drafter~\cite{cai2024medusa,fu2024lookahead}), running both unconditionally wastes GPU compute whenever context lookup already supplies a confident multi-token continuation. Conversely, running the neural drafter during unconstrained prose turns yields low acceptance ($\sim 21\%$) while inhibiting single-token CUDA decode graph capture (which cannot capture dynamic hidden-state extraction needed to feed MTP).

Our adaptive speculative controller couples two mechanisms in \texttt{hanzo-engine/src/speculative}:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{SA\_SPEC} (\texttt{sa\_bypass\_threshold})}: On each speculative step, the engine queries \texttt{SuffixAutomaton} first. Whenever the longest suffix match length satisfies $n_{\text{match}} \ge \tau_{\text{SA}}$ (set to $\tau_{\text{SA}} = 4$ in agent benchmarks), \texttt{SA\_SPEC} emits up to $\gamma = 6$ suffix-matched draft tokens and skips the neural draft forward pass completely (\texttt{record\_sa\_bypass()}).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{GuardedDraft} / \texttt{AdaptiveSpeculativeGuard}}: Tracks an exponentially weighted moving average (\texttt{ewma\_accepted}) of accepted tokens per draft round. When low-yield streaks exceed \texttt{max\_low\_streak}, the guard enters an exponentially backed-off cooldown window (\texttt{cooldown\_left}, up to \texttt{MAX\_BACKOFF\_SHIFT} $= 2$) carried across sequential turns in a conversation (\texttt{with\_cross\_seq\_carry(true)}). During cooldown, \texttt{GuardedDraft} skips neural draft proposals, disables target hidden-state extraction (\texttt{draft\_needs\_target\_hiddens()}), and allows single-token CUDA decode graphs to re-engage, periodically issuing single-step probes after cooldown to recover immediately when workload structure shifts back toward code edits.
  \item \textbf{Unconditional target verification}: Neither \texttt{SA\_SPEC} nor context lookup ever skips target-model logit evaluation. Every proposed draft token sequence is verified identically against target-model logits inside \texttt{verifier.rs}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Verification Correctness Fixes: \texttt{top\_k == 1} Argmax and CUDA \texttt{start\_offset()}}
Benchmarking greedy speculative verification uncovered two latent correctness defects resolved in commit \texttt{1a9d35400}:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{\texttt{Sampler::is\_argmax()} handling \texttt{top\_k == 1}}: Request construction translates deterministic configs (\texttt{temperature: None, top\_k: Some(1)}) into \texttt{Sampler} instances with \texttt{temperature = Some(1.0)} and \texttt{top\_k = 1}. Previously, \texttt{Sampler::is\_argmax()} checked only \texttt{self.temperature.is\_none()}, causing deterministic \texttt{top\_k == 1} requests to bypass \texttt{SA\_SPEC}, skip greedy lookup continuation, and take stochastic verification paths. Expanding the predicate to \texttt{self.temperature.is\_none() || self.top\_k == 1} aligns greedy routing without disturbing stochastic top-$k$ temperature scaling.
  \item \textbf{Nonzero \texttt{layout.start\_offset()} in CUDA top-$k$/top-$1$ kernels}: During multi-token verification over $M$ drafted logits \texttt{[1, M, V]}, row slicing produces a contiguous 1D view with \texttt{stride == [1]} and \texttt{layout.start\_offset() == idx * V}. All four CUDA sampling kernels in \texttt{ops.rs} (\texttt{cuda\_topk}, \texttt{cuda\_topk\_logits\_f32}, \texttt{cuda\_topk\_logits\_f32\_packed}, and \texttt{cuda\_top1\_logits\_f32\_cached}) previously dereferenced device base pointers directly without adding \texttt{layout.start\_offset()}, reading row $0$ on every verification step and producing duplicate-token stuttering. Adding explicit element pointer offsets across F16, BF16, and F32 paths eliminates verification stuttering completely (verified via \texttt{cuda\_sliced\_row\_topk\_respects\_start\_offset} across all four kernels and zero duplicate-word stutters across all 100 evaluation turns).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Pure-Rust \texttt{hanzo-tokenizer} and Self-Bypassing Prefix Caching}

Mainstream Hugging Face \texttt{tokenizers}~\cite{huggingface2024tokenizers} incurs substantial CPU overhead from regex-engine pre-tokenization backtracking and repeated BPE merge heap allocations~\cite{gage1994bpe}. We built \texttt{hanzo-tokenizer} as a clean-room, Apache-2.0 Rust crate integrated through \texttt{FastTokenizerPool}:
\begin{itemize}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Packed 2-bit Unicode class LUT and SWAR scanners}: Instead of executing general regex backtracking engines on common pre-tokenizer patterns, \texttt{unicode\_lut.rs} compiles character classes into compact lookup tables paired with byte-level word-at-a-time (SWAR) scanners for modern BPE regex families, while keeping safe fallback to \texttt{tokenizers} in \texttt{FastTokenizerPool} when unsupported lookarounds appear.
  \item \textbf{Compact \texttt{MergeMap} and \texttt{pretoken\_hash} memoization}: Open-addressed hashing over packed token-pair keys with \texttt{FxBuildHasher} reduces merge lookup cost and cache pressure on large vocabularies ($151\text{K}$--$248\text{K}$ tokens).
  \item \textbf{3-probe startup parity gate}: At initialization, \texttt{FastTokenizerPool} verifies exact token-ID agreement between \texttt{hanzo-tokenizer} and the reference Hugging Face tokenizer across structured probes before activating the fast path.
  \item \textbf{Self-bypassing \texttt{PromptTokenCache}}: Multi-turn chat prompts append assistant replies and user turns to an existing prefix. \texttt{PromptTokenCache} (\texttt{prompt\_cache.rs}) stores bounded LRU entries indexed by text hash alongside newline split checkpoints \texttt{(byte\_offset, token\_end\_index)} so appended turns re-encode only the suffix delta and pre-EOS boundaries remain exact. Because hashing and prefix scanning add overhead on workloads with disjoint prompts or non-matching structures, encountering \texttt{CONSECUTIVE\_MISS\_THRESHOLD = 2} consecutive misses activates a bypass cooldown for \texttt{COOLDOWN\_CALLS = 8} calls (bounded prefix scan depth \texttt{MAX\_PREFIX\_SCAN\_ENTRIES = 32}), automatically trading back to full caching if prefix locality returns.
\end{itemize}

\section{Vision Preprocessing, Chat Templates, and \texttt{HKV1} KV Fabric}

\subsection{SIMD Vision Pipeline (\texttt{hanzo-vision}) and CPU Upsampling (\texttt{hanzo-ml})}
For multimodal Vision-Language Models, scalar RGB resize followed by separate normalization passes introduces tens of milliseconds of host latency per high-resolution frame~\cite{hanzo2026engine}. We updated \texttt{hanzo-vision} to pair SIMD Catmull-Rom image resizing (\texttt{fast\_image\_resize} behind \texttt{simd\_resize\_rgb}) with a fused single-pass planar \texttt{u8 -> CHW f32} normalizer (\texttt{ToNormalizedTensor}), eliminating intermediate interleaved float tensor allocations while preserving $<10^{-5}$ numerical agreement (\texttt{max\_diff = 0.00e0} vs scalar normalization on identical resized buffers, bounded within $1/127.5 + 10^{-4}$ end-to-end against \texttt{image::FilterType::CatmullRom}).

In \texttt{hanzo-ml}, we vectorized CPU \texttt{Tensor::upsample\_nearest2d} for integer $2\times$ scaling and contiguous inner-row duplication, checking bit-exactness against a scalar reference (\texttt{max\_abs\_diff = 0.0}) and measuring both $2\times$ and non-power-of-two $3\times$ shapes explicitly.

\subsection{Cached Chat Templates and Epoch-Fenced \texttt{HKV1} KV Transfer}
Repeatedly compiling complex Jinja2 tool-calling chat templates on every turn wastes host microseconds; pinning \texttt{minijinja} $2.24.0$ and caching compiled environments per model avoids redundant parsing and bytecode generation. For disaggregated prefill-decode clusters~\cite{kwon2023pagedattention,dao2022flashattention}, \texttt{hanzo-kv-fabric} implements \texttt{EpochFencedKvDirectory} (epoch-fenced block ownership publication, lookup, and stale-epoch fencing to prevent split-brain KV reads) and an asynchronous framed TCP transport (\texttt{KvBlockTransport}, wire magic \texttt{HKV1}) supporting verified SHA-256 block pushes and pulls without external C++ transport libraries.

Four quantitative findings emerge from Table~\ref{tab:agent}:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
  \item \textbf{Prefix KV caching cuts time-to-first-token by $4.05\times$}: Caching prefix blocks (\texttt{prefix\_cache\_n = 16}, hitting $28{,}704$ of $33{,}215$ prompt tokens) drops p50 TTFT from $1{,}087.2\,\text{ms}$ to $268.7\,\text{ms}$ (p95 from $2{,}522.3\,\text{ms}$ to $463.7\,\text{ms}$) and total session wall time from $65{,}648.7\,\text{ms}$ to $44{,}602.2\,\text{ms}$ ($1.47\times$).
  \item \textbf{Unguarded neural MTP regresses $1.68\times$ on mixed turns}: Running MTP continuously on mixed prose and edit workloads accepts only $21.3\%$ of $2{,}442$ drafted tokens across $1{,}221$ verify rounds, inflating prose p50 ITL from $21.85\,\text{ms}$ to $45.22\,\text{ms}$ and total wall time to $75{,}036.1\,\text{ms}$.
  \item \textbf{Suffix lookup cuts edit ITL by $10.64\times$}: Grounded edit p50 ITL plunges from $27.57\,\text{ms}$ (\texttt{cached\_plain}) to $2.59\,\text{ms}$ (\texttt{cached\_prompt\_lookup}) and $2.79\,\text{ms}$ (\texttt{cached\_adaptive\_full}, $9.88\times$), while total wall time falls below unspeculated decoding.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{SA\_SPEC} + \texttt{GuardedDraft} achieves the lowest total wall time}: Combining \texttt{SuffixAutomaton} lookup, \texttt{SA\_SPEC} bypass ($160$ neural draft rounds bypassed), and \texttt{GuardedDraft} cooldown ($861$ low-yield skips) reduces total neural proposals from $2{,}442$ to $1{,}308$, lifts verified acceptance from $21.3\%$ to $49.2\%$, and completes the 20-turn conversation in \textbf{38,190.3\,ms} ($1.96\times$ faster than unguarded MTP, $1.72\times$ faster than uncached decode, and $1.17\times$ faster than \texttt{cached\_plain}). Note that under FP16/BF16 CUDA reductions, batched $M>1$ verification and cached vs.\ uncached prefill can break near-tied logit ordering slightly differently than serial $M=1$ decode ($5/20$ identical turn texts between \texttt{cached\_adaptive\_full} and \texttt{cached\_plain}, vs.\ $9/20$ with unguarded MTP), with zero token-repetition artifacts across all $100$ outputs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations and Safety}
Our warm 20-turn end-to-end CUDA numbers evaluate one 4B quantized hybrid-attention model (\texttt{zen6-nano-4b-PQ2\_0-MTP-Q8\_0.gguf}) on one NVIDIA GB10 workstation; scaling ratios will shift on larger models (where neural draft passes cost more relative to target compute), bandwidth-bound Metal or ROCm GPUs, or non-coding workloads lacking local context repetition. Additionally, while \texttt{hanzo-tokenizer} guarantees bit-identical output against \texttt{tokenizers 0.21.4} at startup and falls back on unsupported regex lookarounds, models requiring PCRE2 lookaround engines do not receive SWAR tokenization acceleration. Finally, speculative decoding preserves greedy or distribution equivalence up to floating-point reduction associativity across batch sizes $M>1$ versus $M=1$.
