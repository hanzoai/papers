\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

LoCoMo~\cite{locomo} established the long-horizon conversational memory task and
supplies the per-question evidence annotations that make an \textbf{all}-evidence
metric computable; without them only \textbf{any} is measurable, and
\S\ref{sec:baseline} is the argument that \textbf{any} alone is misleading.

Dense passage retrieval~\cite{dpr} is the substrate every policy here runs on.
Pseudo-relevance feedback long predates it --- Rocchio's method~\cite{rocchio}
reformulates a query toward its own top results, and our \textbf{prf} policy is
that idea in vector space. IRCoT~\cite{ircot} interleaves retrieval with a
model's chain of thought, which is the shape our \textbf{chain} policy imitates
with similarity substituted for the reasoning step; \S\ref{sec:limits} is
explicit that this makes our result a bound on cheap hops rather than a
refutation of IRCoT.

Entity-and-graph-structured memory~\cite{hipporag} is the family
\S\ref{sec:implications} points at: it constructs the cross-session linkage that
our diagnostic identifies as the missing signal, instead of trying to recover it
from similarity. This paper's contribution to that line is negative and
supporting --- evidence, on a standard benchmark, that the cheaper alternative
does not work and cannot be made to.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Dense retrieval over a long conversation does not misunderstand multi-hop
questions. It answers them partially: at $k{=}20$ it finds at least one required
turn \rManyTwenty{}\% of the time and all of them \rMallTwenty{}\% of the time.

Four retrieval policies over one fixed index --- feedback, neighbour expansion,
and a two-deep chain --- move that \rMallTwenty{}\% by at most \rBestDelta{}
percentage points, and two of the four move it backwards. The reason is
structural: \rSpanSessions{}\% of multi-hop evidence spans sessions, the median
worst-ranked gold turn sits at rank \rMedWorst{}, and \rBeyondHundred{}\% of
questions have evidence past rank 100. Every policy tested spends more
similarity, and similarity is precisely the axis along which the missing turns
are distant.

The fix is not a better search over the same space. It is a different relation
--- entity linkage across sessions --- built as the conversation arrives and
traversed at retrieval time. We have not built it. We have established, on a
standard benchmark with a reproducible script, that the cheap alternative does
not work, and we would rather publish that than a tuning result of two questions
out of \rQmulti{}.

\section*{Reproducing}

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
curl -sL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/snap-research/locomo/main/\
data/locomo10.json -o locomo10.json
node brain.mjs      # baseline; caches vectors to brain-vectors.json
node hops.mjs       # the four policies, from the cache
node evidence.mjs   # the structural diagnostic
\end{lstlisting}
