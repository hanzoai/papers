\section{Kai in the loop: Enso systems evaluation}
\label{sec:enso}

This section is a design \proposed: every cell prints \ph{} until its run
lands, under the same rules as Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.

\paragraph{Thesis.}
Kai does not make Zen generate faster; it makes Zen generate less. Enso is
Hanzo's adaptive model family, served by the Zen engine, and Kai is its
controller, in process: at each step of an agent task it decides which model
answers, what context it sees, which tools and skills it is shown, how long it
may reason, whether the task is done, and whether to escalate, each a Decision
Program under policy in shadow, advisory or enforced mode.

\paragraph{Cost.}
Per task,
\begin{equation}
  C = C_{\mathrm{Kai}} + C_{\mathrm{prefill}} + C_{\mathrm{decode}}
      + C_{\mathrm{retrieval}} + C_{\mathrm{tools}},
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where prefill and decode sum over the task's generative calls, each the chosen
model's per-token cost times its prompt or decode tokens, and $C_{\mathrm{Kai}}$
is one encoder pass per decision. Kai spends $C_{\mathrm{Kai}}$ to shrink the
prefill and decode terms with a smaller model, fewer prompt tokens, fewer decode
tokens and fewer steps; the thesis holds where that saving exceeds
$C_{\mathrm{Kai}}$ at equal success. Every metric is per successful task, so no
condition looks cheap by failing: success rate, GPU-seconds, prompt and decode
tokens, KV GB-seconds $\int \mathrm{KV}(t)\,dt$ over the bytes of KV cache the
task holds, peak VRAM, latency p50 and p95, and dollars per successful task.

\paragraph{Conditions.}
\textbf{B0} Zen only: the largest Zen model for every step. \textbf{B1} a
static router by task type. \textbf{R1} Zen as router: the same large Zen model
routes itself. \textbf{K1} Kai routes the model. \textbf{K2} K1 plus context
pruning. \textbf{K3} K2 plus a tool and skill shortlist. \textbf{K4} K3 plus
progress and early stop. \textbf{K5} the full decision plane: model, context,
tools, reasoning budget, completion and escalation.

successful model per step is known; public agent benchmarks; and real Hanzo
traces replayed from shadow records. Table~\ref{tab:enso} gives the synthetic

\paragraph{Studies.}
Each isolates one decision on the synthetic workload (Table~\ref{tab:ensostudies}).
\emph{Routing} against the oracle, the cheapest model that succeeds on the
step; regret is the chosen model's cost minus the oracle's. \emph{Context}:
tokens kept and success with pruning. \emph{Tools and skills}: a shortlist
from Hanzo Skills (900+) against listing them all. \emph{Early stop}: steps and
decode tokens saved, and stops made before the task was done. \emph{Reasoning
budget}: decode tokens and success under Kai's budget. \emph{KV retention}: Kai
predicts which contexts return, against LRU. The \emph{full system} runs B0 and
K5 on the replayed traces under 1 to 128 concurrent agents
(Table~\ref{tab:ensoload}). Keys are \texttt{enso/<study>/<condition>/<metric>};
for the condition ladder the study names the workload.
