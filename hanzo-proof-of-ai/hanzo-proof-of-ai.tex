% ABSTRACT

\begin{abstract}
A decentralized AI network must do two things a token network does not: it must
\emph{prove} that operators performed the AI work they are paid for, and it must
\emph{price} that work into a coin without a trusted oracle. We give a complete
treatment of both, joined at a single seam.

For the proof: a digital signature proves \emph{authorship} of a message; a
plurality vote proves \emph{agreement} among reporters; neither proves
\emph{computation} --- that the claimed model was run on the claimed input to
produce the claimed output. The cheapest robbery of such a network is therefore a
one-bit modal guess that joins the quorum and is minted alongside honest work. We
present Proof of AI (PoAI): the proof is not a separate artifact bolted onto
inference, it is emitted \emph{as a side-effect of execution by the Hanzo engine
itself}. Because $\sim$95\% of a forward pass is matrix multiplication
$C = A\cdot B$, each layer is verified in $O(n^2)$ instead of recomputed in
$O(n^3)$ using Freivalds' algorithm over a prime field $\Fp$, $p = 2^{61}-1$,
applied to the \emph{exact} \code{int8} integer accumulator. Integer arithmetic
is bit-exact across CPU, GPU, and backends, so the check has \emph{zero}
false-reject. PoAI proves that an entire computation \emph{graph} ran --- inference,
embedding, and training are all graphs, verified by one proof system --- and its
soundness is information-theoretic, hence post-quantum.

For the price: we develop the \textbf{Hamiltonian Market Maker (HMM)}, an
automated market maker for the one asset PoAI makes well-defined --- a unit of
\emph{cryptographically verified} compute, settled in AICoin. We define the market
state $(q,p)$ (inventory of proven-compute units and their marginal price), a
conserved quantity $H$ (the Hamiltonian, the maker's bonded value), and trades as
the canonical flow that holds $H$ on its level set. We prove the properties this
buys: \emph{path-independence} (the cost of a trade depends only on its endpoints),
\emph{bounded round-trip} (a closed loop costs zero before fees, strictly positive
after, so no in-curve arbitrage exists), a \emph{bounded worst-case
market-maker subsidy} (the LMSR bound $b\ln N$, recovered as the entropic special
case, and the divergence-loss bound for constant-function makers), and a
\emph{monotone price--supply law} that ties price to verified-compute supply. We
show LMSR (Hanson) and constant-function AMMs (Uniswap's $x\cdot y=k$ is a
degenerate conserved quantity) are two potentials of one framework, with HMM the
principled Legendre-duality generalization. The supply side is honest by
construction: only miners bonded in proportion to declared capacity
(\code{MinerStakeRegistry}) feed the curve, and a network-wide no-double-mint
ledger (\code{AIComputeRegistry}) guarantees each inventory increment is a distinct
verified unit.

We state plainly what is live: the verifier core, the typed graph-composition, and
the determinism prerequisites ship and are tested in Rust and Go; the chain
enforces no-proof-no-mint; end-to-end proof \emph{emission} from a running model is
designed and merged but not yet live. The HMM is a protocol-design contribution:
every claim is a theorem with a proof, and we report no empirical benchmark we did
not measure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: prove the work, then price it}
\label{sec:intro}

A network that pays operators to run AI faces two questions that are usually
answered badly. \emph{Did the operator do the work?} and \emph{what is the work
worth?} The first is a soundness question; the second is a market-design question.
This paper answers both, and the answers meet at a single object: the
\emph{proven-compute unit} (PCU), a quantum of work that has been verified to be
real AI computation. Part~I (\S\S\ref{sec:freivalds}--\ref{sec:status}) builds the
proof that makes a PCU well-defined. Part~II
(\S\S\ref{sec:hmm-intro}--\ref{sec:hmm-poai}) builds the market maker that prices
it.

\paragraph{The proof question.} Three properties are easy to confuse, and only one
is the thing that must be secured.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item A \textbf{signature} proves \emph{authorship}: this party committed to
        this message. It says nothing about whether any computation occurred.
  \item A \textbf{plurality} (a quorum, a majority vote over reported outputs)
        proves \emph{agreement}: $m$ reporters submitted the same answer. It is
        only as honest as the reporters, and it rewards consensus, not work.
  \item A \textbf{proof of computation} proves the load-bearing fact: this
        operator ran \emph{this} model on \emph{this} input and obtained
        \emph{this} output.
\end{itemize}

The gap between the first two and the third is an economic vulnerability. If
minting requires only a signed output that matches the quorum, the cheapest attack
is a $\sim$1-bit \emph{modal guess}: an operator that never loads the model,
predicts the most likely token (or copies a neighbor), signs it, joins the
plurality, and is paid. No matmul is performed; the marginal cost of the ``work''
is a coin flip. PoAI closes the gap by making the proof a \emph{property of
execution} rather than a property of reporting.

\paragraph{The price question.} Once work is provably real, a decentralized market
must convert it into coin. An order book needs matched counterparties and deep
liquidity that a young compute market lacks; an oracle reintroduces the trusted
third party the proof just removed. Automated market makers (AMMs) solve this for
fungible tokens, but a unit of compute is not a token: it is supplied
continuously, its supply is gated by a cryptographic proof, and the market maker
must subsidize price discovery with a \emph{bounded} worst-case loss the way Hanson's
LMSR does for prediction markets. We give the AMM that fits: the Hamiltonian
Market Maker, whose conserved quantity ties the AICoin price of compute directly to
the supply of \emph{verified} PCUs.

\paragraph{Why they belong in one paper.} The honesty of the price rests entirely
on the honesty of the supply. An AMM that prices unverified ``compute'' prices a
modal guess; the curve is only as sound as the unit feeding it. PoAI is what makes
the HMM's inventory axis $q$ mean something: every increment of $q$ is a distinct,
verified, non-replayable PCU. Conversely, the HMM is what gives PoAI an economic
endpoint: a proof that settles into a fairly-priced coin rather than a flat
subsidy. The seam between them is the \code{MinerStakeRegistry} (bonded supply) and
the \code{AIComputeRegistry} (no double mint), grounded in \S\ref{sec:hmm-poai}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{(Part I, proof.)} The framing signature $\neq$ plurality $\neq$
        computation and the modal-guess attack it exposes (\S\ref{sec:intro}); the
        Freivalds core over the exact \code{int8} accumulator in $\Fp$ with zero
        false-reject, grounded line-for-line in \code{poi.rs}
        (\S\ref{sec:freivalds}); the four proofs PoE/PoI/PoT/PoC over one matmul
        primitive (\S\ref{sec:four}); typed \emph{graph} composition that lifts
        per-matmul soundness to whole-forward-pass soundness
        (\S\ref{sec:graph}); the determinism prerequisites
        (\S\ref{sec:determinism}); the commit-now/verify-later activation-trace MMR
        with interactive bisection (\S\ref{sec:mmr}); the \code{reportData} binding
        (\S\ref{sec:binding}); the by-tier floating-point treatment
        (\S\ref{sec:fp}); the post-quantum argument (\S\ref{sec:pq}); and an honest
        implementation-status section (\S\ref{sec:status}).
  \item \textbf{(Part II, price.)} The Hamiltonian Market Maker:
        a full phase-space formulation of compute pricing

\section{The Freivalds core: verifying a forward pass}
\label{sec:freivalds}

A transformer forward pass is dominated by matrix multiplication: the
attention projections, the MLP/expert GEMMs, and the output head are all of the
form $C = A\cdot B$, and together they are $\sim$95\% of the floating (here,
integer) operations. This is the lever. To \emph{recompute} a layer
$A\in\mathbb{Z}^{t\times k}$, $B\in\mathbb{Z}^{k\times n}$, giving
$C\in\mathbb{Z}^{t\times n}$, costs $O(t\,k\,n)$ --- the same work the prover
did. To \emph{verify} it, we do not need to redo it.

\begin{theorem}[Freivalds, 1977, over $\Fp$]
\label{thm:freivalds}
Let $p$ be prime, $A,B,C$ integer matrices of conforming shape reduced into
$\Fp$, and let $r \in \Fp^{\,n}$ be drawn uniformly at random. If
$C = A\cdot B$ then $A\,(B\,r) = C\,r$ \emph{always}. If $C \neq A\cdot B$ then
\[
  \Pr_{r}\!\big[A\,(B\,r) = C\,r\big] \;\le\; \frac{1}{p}.
\]
Computing $B\,r$, then $A\,(B\,r)$, and $C\,r$ costs $O(t k + k n + t n) = O(n^2)$
matrix--vector work, one order below the $O(n^3)$ of the multiply.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $D = C - A\cdot B \neq 0$ over $\Fp$, so some row $D_i \neq 0$. The event
$D\,r = 0$ forces $\inner{D_i}{r} = 0$, a single non-trivial linear
constraint on $r$; its solution set is a hyperplane, an affine subspace of
codimension one, containing exactly $p^{\,n-1}$ of the $p^{\,n}$ points of
$\Fp^{\,n}$, i.e. a $1/p$ fraction. An honest $C$ gives $D = 0$ and passes for
every $r$, which is why there is no false-reject.
\end{proof}

Running $k$ independent challenge vectors multiplies the per-vector soundness
error, giving a tampered $C$ a survival probability of at most $(1/p)^k$.

\paragraph{Why integers, and why $p = 2^{61}-1$.}
The engine's \code{int8} quantized path multiplies signed 8-bit operands into a
32-bit accumulator ($i8\cdot i8 \to i32$, the \code{f8q8} path in
\code{hanzo-quant}). That accumulator is an \emph{exact integer}; it is identical
on every backend that respects the reduction, because integer addition and
multiplication carry no rounding. Verifying over $\Fp$ with the Mersenne prime
$p = 2^{61}-1$ keeps every intermediate inside 61 bits, so a single 128-bit
multiply (\code{fmul}) and a conditional subtract (\code{fadd}) suffice and never
overflow. The result is the property the rest of PoAI depends on:
\textbf{zero false-reject across CPU/GPU/backends}. Soundness error is
$1/p \approx 2^{-61}$ per challenge vector; $k = 2$ vectors give $\le 2^{-122}$.

\paragraph{The shipped API.}
The core lives in \code{hanzo-engine/src/poi.rs}: dependency-free, backend-agnostic,
unit-tested in isolation. The surface is small and is mirrored byte-for-byte by
the Go verifier the chain consumes (\code{luxfi/crypto/poi}, with
\code{NewMat}/\code{Verify}/\code{VerifyMulti}/\code{DeriveChallenges} and the
same $p = (1\ll 61)-1$).

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
pub const P: u64 = (1u64 << 61) - 1;       // Mersenne prime field modulus

pub struct Mat { rows, cols, data: Vec<i64> }  // exact i32/i64 accumulators

// Verify C == A*B for ONE challenge r:  A*(B*r) == C*r over F_p, O(tk+kn+tn).
pub fn freivalds_verify(a, b, c: &Mat, r: &[u64]) -> bool;

// Verify against k challenges; honest C passes ALL, tampered C fails w.p. >= 1-(1/p)^k.
pub fn freivalds_verify_multi(a, b, c: &Mat, challenges: &[Vec<u64>]) -> bool;

// Derive k challenge vectors of length n from a seed (prod: keccak(beacon || ...)).
pub fn derive_challenges_keccak(seed: &[u8], n: usize, k: usize) -> Vec<Vec<u64>>;
\end{lstlisting}

The reduction \code{to\_field} uses \code{rem\_euclid} so signed \code{int8}
weights and activations map correctly into $[0,p)$. The module ships seven unit
tests covering an honest $2\times 2$ product, a single tampered entry being
caught, negative-entry reduction, deterministic challenge derivation, a
fail-closed shape mismatch, a $16{\times}24\cdot 24{\times}16$ product where
flipping one deep entry off-by-one is still caught (the sparse-cheat case that
motivates challenging every layer), and a \emph{cross-language golden-parity}
test: \code{derive\_challenges\_keccak} is asserted against a pinned hex value the
Go verifier (\code{crypto/poi}) emits and asserts in turn, so a Rust-emitted
challenge is guaranteed to reproduce on the chain side. The Go mirror carries 22
test functions across its \code{freivalds}, \code{wire}, \code{transcript},
\code{scale}, and \code{adversarial} suites.

\paragraph{The challenge must come after the commitment.}
Soundness in Theorem~\ref{thm:freivalds} assumes $r$ is unpredictable to the
prover. If the prover knew $r$ in advance it could craft $A,B,C$ with
$D\,r = 0$ yet $C\neq A\cdot B$. The production derivation therefore draws the
seed from an on-chain beacon \emph{after} the prover has committed
(\S\ref{sec:mmr}); \code{derive\_challenges\_keccak} is the reproducible expansion
both sides apply to that seed, computing for each $(j,i)$ the field element
$\keccak(\text{seed}\,\|\,\mathrm{BE}_{32}(j)\,\|\,\mathrm{BE}_{64}(i))[0{:}8]
\bmod p$. The unit tests additionally exercise a \code{splitmix64} stream for the
same reproducibility --- identical soundness, only the unpredictability source
differs.

\section{Four proofs over one primitive}
\label{sec:four}

PoAI is not four mechanisms; it is one matmul-verification primitive applied to
four \code{workloadType}s. The operands $(A, B, C)$ and the I/O commitment differ
by workload, but the verifier, the field, and the soundness argument are shared.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,style=nextline]
  \item[Proof of Embedding (PoE).] The embedding tower (and any ViT/audio
        encoder feeding it) is a stack of GEMMs producing a vector. Freivalds
        verifies the projection layers; the input commitment fixes what was
        embedded. Settlement uses the embedding as the unit of paid work.
  \item[Proof of Inference (PoI).] A decode step is the attention and MLP/expert
        GEMMs plus the output head. The transcript commits the per-layer
        operands; a challenged layer is opened and Freivalds-checked. This is the
        canonical case and the one the modal-guess attacker cannot fake.
  \item[Proof of Training (PoT).] A training step is a forward pass plus a
        backward pass; the backward pass is again matmuls (the transposed
        GEMMs of the gradient). The same primitive verifies that a claimed
        gradient update corresponds to real backpropagation, not a fabricated
        delta.
  \item[Proof of Compute (PoC).] The general case: any committed GEMM workload
        (a kernel an operator claims to have run for hire) is verifiable by the
        identical $A\,(B\,r) = C\,r$ check. PoE/PoI/PoT are PoC specialized by
        what the operands mean and what I/O is bound.
\end{description}

This is deliberate orthogonal design: one primitive at one slot, four payoffs.
A network that wants to pay for embeddings, inference, training, or raw compute
does not need four verifiers --- it needs one, parameterized by
\code{workloadType}.

\paragraph{Settlement: closed-form expert weights from PoAI.}
Once work is \emph{verified}, it can be \emph{weighted}. For each expert
(operator) $m$, let $q_m \in [0,1]$ be the Bayesian reliability estimated from its
historical attestations. Under a product-of-experts combination the optimal
mixing weight is closed-form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poai-weights}
  \eta_m \;\propto\; \frac{q_m}{1 - q_m},
\end{equation}
i.e. precision-weighted aggregation, with no manual tuning. At decode time this
is the product-of-experts rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poe-main}
  \log \pi_*(y\mid x) \;=\; \eta_0 \log \pi_0(y\mid x)
   \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{K} \eta_i \log \phi_i(y\mid x) \;-\; \log Z,
\end{equation}
where $\pi_0 = p_\theta$ is the base model, $\{\phi_i\}$ are expert factors, and
$Z$ is the per-token partition function. PoAI supplies the reliabilities $q_m$
that make Eq.~\eqref{eq:poai-weights} trustworthy: a weight derived from
\emph{verified} work, not from self-reported agreement.

\section{From matmuls to a graph: typed composition}
\label{sec:graph}

Theorem~\ref{thm:freivalds} secures one matmul. A forward pass is many matmuls
glued by non-matmul operations (residual adds, activations, integer rescales, MoE
routing). A prover could commit \emph{correct} matmuls computed on \emph{unrelated}
inputs --- a valid-looking transcript that is not a real forward pass on the actual
prompt. PoAI closes this with a typed computation trace (the
\code{poi\_graph} module, nine tests), lifting per-op soundness to whole-graph
soundness.

\begin{definition}[Graph trace]
A \emph{graph trace} is a finite sequence of typed operations
$o_1,\dots,o_L$ over hash-identified activations. Each $o_\ell$ commits the tuple
$\big(\text{kind}\,\|\,\text{input commitments}\,\|\,\text{params}\,\|\,
\text{output commitment}\big)$, where every commitment is a \keccak{} digest of
the exact-integer activation tensor (with domain separation \code{DOM\_ACT} for
activations and \code{DOM\_OP} for op leaves).
\end{definition}

Two checks compose. A trace is \emph{well-formed} (chaining) if every op's input
commitment is the output commitment of a prior op or the trace's declared input; a
break --- an op reading an activation no prior op produced --- is visible from the
committed leaves alone. A trace is \emph{locally correct} if, when opened, each op
recomputes its output from its inputs (a \code{MatMul} via Freivalds; a
deterministic glue op --- residual add, activation, integer scale, MoE top-$k$
route, expert gather, weighted combine --- by direct exact recomputation) and the
result's commitment equals the committed output.

\begin{theorem}[Graph soundness]
\label{thm:graph}
If a graph trace is well-formed and every op is locally correct, then the
committed final output equals the deterministic forward pass applied to the
committed input.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Induct over a topological order of the ops. Base: the declared input commitment is
the true input by definition. Step: consider $o_\ell$ in order. By well-formedness
each of its input commitments is either the declared input or the output
commitment of some $o_{j}$, $j<\ell$; by the inductive hypothesis those equal the
true intermediate values of the deterministic forward pass. By local correctness
$o_\ell$'s committed output is the exact function of its committed inputs that the
op computes. Hence $o_\ell$'s committed output equals the true value of the
forward pass at that node. Taking $\ell = L$ gives the claim. Everything is exact
integer, so equality of commitments is equality of values with zero false-reject.
\end{proof}

The practical payoff: attention's $QK^\top/AV$ and a full MoE block (router
$\to$ top-$k$ $\to$ gather $\to$ expert matmuls $\to$ combine) are expressible in
the trace with \emph{no} floating-point reduction, so the glue is exactly
recomputable and the only probabilistic step is Freivalds on the GEMMs.

\section{Determinism is the load-bearing property}
\label{sec:determinism}

Exact Freivalds needs an exactly reproducible $C$. If two honest runs of the
same model on the same input could produce different outputs, there would be no
canonical $C$ to commit to, and an honest prover could be false-rejected. Exact
verification is therefore \emph{impossible} without bit-level determinism. Two
sources of nondeterminism are silently present in ordinary inference, and both
are closed in the shipped engine.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Argmax ties.} Greedy decode takes $\arg\max$ over logits; when
        two logits are equal, the winner is implementation-defined (it depends on
        reduction order on the device). The engine pins a \textbf{lowest-id
        tie-break}: among tied maxima the smallest token index wins, on every
        backend.
  \item \textbf{MoE expert selection.} A Mixture-of-Experts router picks the
        top-$k$ experts by score; ties and unstable sort order change \emph{which
        experts run}, changing $C$ entirely. The engine pins a \textbf{canonical
        top-$k$}: sort by score descending, breaking ties by expert index
        ascending (the \code{deterministic\_topk\_indices} selection).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}
Determinism is not a performance feature here; it is the precondition for
soundness. Only because the int8 accumulator and these two tie-breaks are
reproducible can the verifier assert that an honest operator's $C$ matches
bit-for-bit, and hence that \emph{any} Freivalds failure is real cheating rather
than hardware drift. Proof-bearing runs additionally set
\code{set\_moe\_proof\_deterministic(true)} and pin temperature-0 greedy decode.
\end{remark}

\section{Commit now, verify later}
\label{sec:mmr}

A forward pass over a large model at hundreds of tokens produces terabytes of
activations; retaining them is infeasible, and forcing synchronous verification
would serialize the network. PoAI instead has the operator \emph{commit} to its
activations cheaply during execution and be \emph{challenged} later on a small
random part --- the optimistic bargain: cheap in the happy path, partial
re-execution only under challenge.

\paragraph{ActivationTraceMMR.} A streaming \textbf{Merkle Mountain Range}
accumulates one leaf per $(\text{layer}, \text{token}, \text{tap})$ in execution
order and keeps only $O(\log N)$ peak hashes --- a frontier of roughly 768 bytes.
Activations are hashed as the kernel produces them and then dropped. The hash is
\code{keccak}, with leaf/node domain separation:
\begin{align*}
\text{leaf} &= \keccak(\text{\small DOMAIN\_POI\_ACT} \,\|\, \text{layer}
   \,\|\, \text{token} \,\|\, \text{tap} \,\|\, \text{dtype} \,\|\,
   \text{shape} \,\|\, \text{tensor bytes}),\\
\text{node} &= \keccak(\text{\small DOMAIN\_POI\_NODE} \,\|\, \text{left}
   \,\|\, \text{right}),
\end{align*}
where $\text{tap} \in \{\code{matmul\_in}, \code{matmul\_out},
\code{block\_out}\}$ --- exactly the operands a Freivalds check (or a non-matmul
recompute) will open, no more. Lone nodes on odd levels use \textbf{RFC-6962
promotion}, \emph{not} Bitcoin duplicate-last: the duplicate-last construction is
the CVE-2012-2459 malleability (a forged tree can collide a root), so it is
deliberately avoided. The MMR closes by binding the leaf count,
$\text{activationTraceRoot} = \keccak(\text{\small DOMAIN\_POI} \,\|\,
n_{\text{leaves}} \,\|\, \text{mmr root})$, so a subtree cannot be reinterpreted
as the whole tree.

\paragraph{Zero overhead when off.} The commitment is threaded as an
\code{Option<\&mut ProofTranscript>} through the forward context, not a boolean
checked in hot loops. When the option is \code{None}, each commit site is a
single not-taken branch --- no hashing, no allocation, no tensor copy. When it is
\code{Some}, the per-matmul hook commits the int8 activation view and the i32
accumulator by borrow (no copy); the weight $B$ is committed once at load into
the weights root, never re-hashed per token.

\paragraph{Challenge, open, bisect.} A verifier derives a layer index
$\ell$ and challenge $r$ from a beacon \emph{after} commitment, opens that
layer's $A$ (\code{matmul\_in}), $B$ (a reference under the weights root), and
$C$ (\code{matmul\_out}) with $O(\log N)$ MMR inclusion proofs, and runs
\emph{windowed} Freivalds over a random token sub-range to keep the opened
payload small. The verifier recomputes the leaf hashes, checks the inclusions
against the committed roots, re-derives $r$, and checks $A\,(B\,r) = C\,r$ ---
total cost $O(t k + k n + t n + \log N)$, about $\min(t,k,n)\times$ cheaper than
recomputing the layer. A mismatch slashes the operator. To localize a
\emph{sparse} cheat (a single bad layer among many) the protocol runs interactive
\textbf{bisection} to the first divergent layer in $O(\log L)$ rounds, so
soundness is independent of how many layers are sampled.

\section{Binding: a proof points at exactly one (model, input, output)}
\label{sec:binding}

A correct matmul check is worthless if the attestation can be replayed, spliced
across tasks, or pointed at a different model. PoAI binds the proof with two
hash chains whose constants match the chain side byte-for-byte. The challenge is
derived from immutable task context:
\begin{multline*}
\text{challenge} = \keccak\big(\text{\small DOMAIN\_CHALLENGE} \,\|\,
  \text{taskId} \,\|\, \text{intentID} \,\|\, \text{modelSpecHash} \\
  \|\; \text{promptHash} \,\|\, \text{openBlockHash} \,\|\, \text{operator}\big),
\end{multline*}
and the attested result commits to that challenge:
\begin{multline*}
\text{reportData} = \keccak\big(\text{\small DOMAIN\_REPORT} \,\|\,
  \text{challenge} \,\|\, \text{modelSpecHash} \\
  \|\; \text{promptHash} \,\|\, \text{outputHash}
  \,\|\, \text{runtimeMeasurement}\big).
\end{multline*}
The chain consumes \code{reportData}; because it folds in the challenge (which
already folds in \code{taskId}, \code{intentID}, and \code{openBlockHash}), a
transcript is task-scoped and cannot be reused. This \code{reportData} is
recomputed on-chain from the receipt's own merkle-proven fields by
\code{ComputeProofLib.expectedReportData} and re-checked by the verifier in
\code{AIMiner.mine} (\S\ref{sec:hmm-poai}), so the binding is enforced at the
mint, not merely asserted in prose.

\paragraph{\code{modelSpecHash} is measured, not labelled.} The model identity
is a \keccak{} Merkle root over the \emph{quantized weight bytes the engine
actually loaded} (the per-layer \code{QuantizedSerde} serialization, with the
quant-type discriminant in each leaf). It is not a name string. Re-quantizing the
same weights to a different scheme yields a different root; a genuinely different
model yields a different root. Combined with a \code{runtimeMeasurement} that
folds in the kernel build id, reduction mode, and sampler/seed, the decoding
regime becomes part of identity: the same weights decoded at temperature 2.0, or
under a different reduction order, are rejected. The full transcript root chains
these together,
\begin{multline*}
\text{transcriptRoot} = \keccak\big(\text{\small DOMAIN\_POI} \,\|\,
  \text{modelID} \,\|\, \text{inputCommitment} \,\|\,
  \text{quantizationSpec} \\
  \|\; \text{kernelVersion} \,\|\,
  \text{activationTraceRoot} \,\|\, \text{outputCommitment}\big),
\end{multline*}
so the binding runs challenge $\to$ model $\to$ input $\to$ output and an
attestation cannot be detached from any link in the chain.

\section{Floating point: by tier, not by tolerance}
\label{sec:fp}

The natural temptation is to run Freivalds directly on floating-point GEMMs with
a tolerance: accept if $\lVert A(Br) - Cr\rVert < \varepsilon$. This is both
\textbf{unsound} and \textbf{false-rejecting}, and PoAI rejects it.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \emph{Unsound:} a tolerance corridor of width $\varepsilon$ is an
        attacker's budget. A cheater can perturb $C$ anywhere inside the corridor
        --- a non-trivial space of fabricated outputs --- and pass. The clean
        $1/p$ soundness of Theorem~\ref{thm:freivalds} holds only in an exact
        field.
  \item \emph{False-rejecting:} floating-point reduction order is not fixed
        across hardware, so two honest runs differ in the low bits. Any
        $\varepsilon$ small enough to be sound is small enough to reject honest
        operators on a different GPU.
\end{itemize}

PoAI therefore selects the verification regime by numeric tier:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Verification regime by numeric tier.}
\label{tab:fp-tiers}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Tier} & \textbf{Mechanism} & \textbf{Soundness basis} \\
\midrule
\code{int8} / quantized & exact Freivalds over $\Fp$ & software-only, zero false-reject \\
fp16 / bf16 / fp8       & deterministic-fp (RepOps): & bit-reproducible by pinned \\
                        & pin reduction order        & reduction; or TEE/CC attestation \\
small / niche           & zkML circuit               & succinct cryptographic proof \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For the quantized path the check is exact and software-only --- no special
hardware, the common case for the deployed zen models. For native low-precision
floating point, the fix is not a tolerance but \textbf{determinism}: pin the
reduction order (RepOps / deterministic-fp) so the GEMM is bit-reproducible, then
the exact equality check applies again; where that is impractical, fall back to a
trusted-execution / confidential-compute attestation of the kernel. For small or
unusual workloads a zkML circuit gives a succinct proof directly.

\begin{remark}
A floating-point value \emph{is} an integer --- a sign bit, an exponent, and a
mantissa. A GEMM executed in a \emph{pinned} reduction order is therefore an
exact, reproducible computation over those integers; ``deterministic-fp'' is not
an approximation but a commitment to a specific, replayable order of exact
operations. The unsoundness of naive fp-Freivalds comes entirely from
\emph{unpinned} order, not from floating point being inherently fuzzy.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Coverage across the zen families.} The matmul primitive plus two
extensions --- a \emph{routing proof} (commit router logits and selected expert
indices, verify the canonical top-$k$ chose the same experts, then Freivalds the
selected experts only) and \emph{sequential state-chaining} (bind step $t$'s
output to step $t{+}1$'s input in the commitment) --- covers dense transformers,
Mixture-of-Experts, linear-recurrent/SSM/Gated-DeltaNet, multimodal ViT+audio
towers, and diffusion/Transfusion. The recurrent chaining serves both linear
recurrence and diffusion, so there is no separate diffusion verifier: one
primitive, two extensions, full coverage.

\section{Post-quantum by construction}
\label{sec:pq}

PoAI's soundness does not rest on any computational hardness assumption, so it
does not degrade under a quantum adversary.

\begin{proposition}[Information-theoretic soundness]
\label{prop:itsound}
Conditioned on the challenge $r$ being drawn uniformly and independently of the
prover's committed $(A,B,C)$, the bound $\Pr_r[\,A(Br)=Cr \mid C\neq AB\,]\le 1/p$
of Theorem~\ref{thm:freivalds} holds against a computationally unbounded prover.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:freivalds} is a counting argument over the finite
set $\Fp^{\,n}$: the bad event is confinement of $r$ to a fixed hyperplane, of
density $1/p$, regardless of the prover's running time. No problem is assumed
hard.
\end{proof}

The only cryptographic ingredient is the commitment/transcript hash. We instantiate
it with \keccak{} (modelled as a random oracle for the domain-separated inputs of
\S\ref{sec:mmr}--\ref{sec:binding}); its collision and preimage resistance against
a quantum adversary degrade only by the generic Grover/BHT factors
($2^{128}$ preimage, $2^{85}$ collision at a 256-bit output), which is why the
transcript binds at a 256-bit width. The unpredictability of $r$ comes from an
on-chain beacon, not from a trapdoor. Consequently the part of the system an
attacker would target with a quantum computer --- forging a proof of work never
done --- is governed by Proposition~\ref{prop:itsound}, which a quantum computer
does not help with.

\paragraph{Post-quantum at the signing and key-exchange layers.} Where the
surrounding protocol does use public-key cryptography --- authenticating operators,
sealing prompts, carrying attestations across chains --- it uses post-quantum
primitives, so the \emph{envelope} matches the information-theoretic \emph{core}:
ML-DSA (FIPS~204, parameter sets 44/65/87) and SLH-DSA (FIPS~205) for signatures
exposed as precompiles; an X-Wing hybrid KEM (X25519 + ML-KEM-768) for prompt
sealing, implemented as a pure-Go HPKE library rather than a precompile; the P3Q
verifier precompile at slot \code{0x012205}; and \code{luxfi/warp} carrying an
\code{MLDSACertSet} so cross-chain attestations are quantum-safe end to end. The
network's security claim is thus uniform: \emph{public, leaderless, decentralized,
operator-safe, and nation-state-proof}, with no classical-only link in the chain
from execution to settlement.

\section{The market for verified compute}
\label{sec:hmm-intro}

Part~I makes a \emph{proven-compute unit} (PCU) a well-defined good: one unit of AI
work that has been Freivalds-verified, bound to a measured model and input, and
not double-claimed. Part~II prices it. We want a market maker --- an always-on
counterparty that quotes a price for PCUs against AICoin --- with four properties:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{No oracle.} The price is endogenous, a function of the maker's own
        state, not of an external feed (which would reintroduce a trusted party).
  \item \textbf{Path independence.} The cost of acquiring or releasing inventory
        depends only on the start and end states, not on the order or
        fragmentation of the trades; otherwise the maker leaks value to order
        manipulation.
  \item \textbf{Bounded subsidy.} Like Hanson's Logarithmic Market Scoring Rule
        (LMSR), the maker may run at a loss to bootstrap a thin market, but that
        loss is bounded by a constant the maker chooses in advance.
  \item \textbf{Supply coupling.} The price must rise with the supply of
        \emph{verified} compute and be insensitive to unverified claims --- the
        link to PoAI.
\end{enumerate}

We obtain all four from a single structural choice: the market's price one-form is
\emph{exact}, i.e. it is the differential of a scalar potential we call the
Hamiltonian. This is the abstraction that contains LMSR and constant-function AMMs
as special cases and that yields the bounds above as theorems rather than
parameters.

% ============================================================================
\section{The Hamiltonian formulation}
\label{sec:hmm-formulation}

\subsection{State, potential, price}

We begin scalar; \S\ref{sec:hmm-multi} lifts to a vector of heterogeneous compute
classes. The market state is a point $(q,p)$ in phase space
$\Gamma = \R \times \Rpos$, where $q\in\R$ is the maker's net inventory of PCUs
(positive: the maker has sold PCUs into the market and holds the AICoin; we let
$q$ range over $\R$ so the maker can be long or short) and $p\in\Rpos$ is the
marginal AICoin price of one PCU. We equip $\Gamma$ with the canonical symplectic
form $\omega = \dd p \wedge \dd q$ and the Poisson bracket
\begin{equation}
\{F,G\} \;=\; \frac{\partial F}{\partial q}\frac{\partial G}{\partial p}
            - \frac{\partial F}{\partial p}\frac{\partial G}{\partial q}.
\label{eq:bracket}
\end{equation}

The maker is specified by a single object: a \emph{cost potential}.

\begin{definition}[Cost potential]
\label{def:cost}
A \emph{cost potential} is a function $C:\R\to\R$ that is $C^2$, strictly convex
($C''(q)>0$ for all $q$), with $C(0)=0$, and superlinear
($C(q)/|q|\to\infty$ as $|q|\to\infty$, so the price range $C'(\R)$ is all of $\R$
and the conjugate below is finite everywhere with $(\Cstar)'=(C')^{-1}$). The
\emph{marginal price} the maker quotes at inventory $q$ is
\begin{equation}
p(q) \;=\; C'(q),
\label{eq:price}
\end{equation}
and the \emph{cash a trader pays} to move the maker's inventory from $q_0$ to $q_1$
is the path integral of the price one-form,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cost}(q_0\!\to q_1) \;=\; \int_{q_0}^{q_1} p\,\dd q
   \;=\; C(q_1) - C(q_0).
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Strict convexity makes $p(q)=C'(q)$ strictly increasing, hence invertible; write
its inverse (the \emph{inventory at price $p$}) as $q(p) = (C')^{-1}(p)$.
Buying PCUs from the maker decreases the maker's inventory and (since $C''>0$)
\emph{raises} the marginal price; selling does the reverse. This is the AMM
slippage law, here derived from convexity.

\subsection{The Hamiltonian and the equations of motion}

The conserved quantity --- the ``energy'' invariant along a trade --- is the maker's
\emph{bonded value}: the AICoin it must escrow to back its quotes. We define it as
the Legendre (convex) conjugate of the cost potential.

\begin{definition}[HMM Hamiltonian]
\label{def:ham}
Let $C$ be a cost potential with conjugate
$\Cstar(p) = \sup_{q\in\R}\{pq - C(q)\}$. The \emph{Hamiltonian} is the bonded
value as a function of state,
\begin{equation}
H(q,p) \;=\; p\,q - C(q).
\label{eq:ham}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

On the \emph{quote manifold} $\Sigma = \{(q,p): p = C'(q)\}$ --- the states the
maker actually occupies --- the supremum in the conjugate is attained at the
operating $q$, so $H$ restricted to $\Sigma$ equals the conjugate evaluated at the
quoted price:
\begin{equation}
H\big(q, C'(q)\big) \;=\; C'(q)\,q - C(q) \;=\; \Cstar\big(C'(q)\big),
\qquad q(p) = (\Cstar)'(p).
\label{eq:conj}
\end{equation}
The second identity is the standard Legendre relation: the inventory is the
derivative of the conjugate potential. Equation~\eqref{eq:conj} is the heart of the
construction --- it is what makes $H$ a clean function of price alone on $\Sigma$.

Two distinct motions live on $\Gamma$, and it is worth separating them cleanly. A
\emph{trade} is a controlled displacement \emph{along} the quote manifold $\Sigma$:
the trader names a new inventory and pays the potential difference
\eqref{eq:cost}; the maker stays on its own curve $p=C'(q)$ throughout, so a trade
is parametrized by inventory, not by a clock. \emph{Arbitrage relaxation} is the
off-$\Sigma$ motion that restores the quote when an external price diverges from
it, and \emph{this} is what the Hamiltonian flow describes. Hamilton's equations
with respect to \eqref{eq:bracket} are
\begin{equation}
\dot q \;=\; \{q,H\} \;=\; \frac{\partial H}{\partial p} \;=\; q,
\qquad
\dot p \;=\; \{p,H\} \;=\; -\frac{\partial H}{\partial q}
          \;=\; -\big(p - C'(q)\big).
\label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
The $\dot p$ equation is the meaningful one: it is a \emph{restoring force toward
the quote manifold}. Off $\Sigma$ (where $p\neq C'(q)$) it pushes the price back to
the quoted value $C'(q)$; on $\Sigma$ the restoring term vanishes and the maker
rests. (The $\dot q=q$ equation is a parametrization artifact of the bonded-value
generator and carries no economic content --- inventory is moved by trades along
$\Sigma$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:cost}, not by this flow time; this is why we never integrate
$\dot q$ to draw an economic conclusion.) The arbitrage gap
\begin{equation}
\Phi(q,p) \;=\; p - C'(q)
\label{eq:gap}
\end{equation}
is therefore the natural ``displacement'' coordinate, with $\Sigma = \{\Phi = 0\}$
its equilibrium. The invariant of the relaxation that matters is the bonded value
$H$ itself: along $\Sigma$ it equals the collateral $\Cstar(p)$
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:conj}), a state function, so no closed trade cycle changes it
(Thm.~\ref{thm:roundtrip}). We will see that $\Sigma$ is exactly the no-arbitrage
manifold.

\begin{remark}[Why this $H$, and not a hand-built energy]
\label{rem:why}
One could write down many Hamiltonians; only one earns the name here. The bonded
value $H=pq-C(q)$ is forced by two requirements: (i) its $q$-gradient must vanish
on the quote manifold (so the maker is at rest exactly when it quotes its own cost
curve), which forces $\partial_q H = -(p-C'(q))$ and hence $H = pq - C(q) + h(p)$;
and (ii) on $\Sigma$ it must equal the collateral the maker has to hold, the
conjugate $\Cstar(p)$, which by \eqref{eq:conj} forces $h\equiv 0$. There is no
free functional choice left. This is the discipline the earlier ``harmonic
potential'' formulations lacked: a term added to $H$ for stability that has no
collateral interpretation also has no conservation theorem behind it.
\end{remark}

% ============================================================================
\section{Conservation laws and the four guarantees}
\label{sec:hmm-conservation}

\subsection{Path independence}

\begin{theorem}[Path independence]
\label{thm:path}
For any cost potential $C$, the cash a trader pays to move the maker between two
inventory levels depends only on the endpoints:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cost}(q_0\!\to q_1) \;=\; C(q_1) - C(q_0),
\end{equation}
independently of how the move is decomposed into intermediate trades. Equivalently,
the price one-form $p\,\dd q = C'(q)\,\dd q = \dd C$ is exact.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Eq.~\eqref{eq:price}, $p\,\dd q = C'(q)\,\dd q = \dd C(q)$, an exact one-form.
For any partition $q_0 = x_0, x_1, \dots, x_m = q_1$ the total cost telescopes:
$\sum_{j=1}^m \big(C(x_j)-C(x_{j-1})\big) = C(q_1)-C(q_0)$. Because the integrand
is a total differential, the line integral \eqref{eq:cost} is independent of the
intermediate points and of any subdivision.
\end{proof}

Path independence is the property that defeats trade-splitting and ordering games:
a trader who breaks one large order into many, or interleaves buys and sells, pays
exactly what a single net trade would cost. It is the discrete-market analogue of a
\emph{conservative force field} in mechanics, and it holds for \emph{every} HMM
because every HMM is built from a potential.

\subsection{Bounded round-trip and no in-curve arbitrage}

\begin{theorem}[Zero-cost round-trip; no arbitrage]
\label{thm:roundtrip}
Let the maker charge a proportional fee at rate $\gamma\ge 0$ on the gross cash of
each trade. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,label=(\alph*)]
  \item With $\gamma=0$, any closed trajectory (one returning the maker to its
        starting inventory $q_0$) has net trader cost exactly $0$.
  \item With $\gamma>0$, any closed trajectory that involves at least one trade of
        nonzero size has net trader cost strictly positive; equivalently, no trader
        can extract value by a round trip against the curve.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(a) By Theorem~\ref{thm:path} the fee-free cost of $q_0\!\to\cdots\to q_0$ is
$C(q_0)-C(q_0)=0$; the conservative one-form integrates to zero around any loop.
(b) The fee on a single trade $x_{j-1}\!\to x_j$ is
$\gamma\,|C(x_j)-C(x_{j-1})|\ge 0$, and is strictly positive whenever
$x_j\neq x_{j-1}$ because $C$ is strictly monotone in price and hence injective.
The net cost of the loop is the fee-free part (zero, by (a)) plus
$\gamma\sum_j |C(x_j)-C(x_{j-1})| > 0$ as soon as one step is nontrivial.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[$\Sigma$ is the no-arbitrage manifold]
\label{cor:noarb}
A price $p$ admits no instantaneous arbitrage against the maker if and only if
$p = C'(q)$, i.e. $(q,p)\in\Sigma$ (the gap $\Phi=0$). Off $\Sigma$, buying at the
quoted $C'(q)<p$ and reselling at $p$ (or the reverse) yields a strictly positive
profit equal to $|p - C'(q)|$ per unit; on $\Sigma$ that profit is zero.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The marginal price the maker quotes is $C'(q)$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:price}. If an
external price $p\neq C'(q)$ exists, the gap $\Phi = p-C'(q)\neq 0$ is exactly the
per-unit profit of trading with the maker and against the external price; arbitrage
exists. If $p=C'(q)$ the gap is zero and no costless profit exists. The dynamics
\eqref{eq:eom} drive $\Phi\to 0$, i.e. arbitrage closes the gap and restores
$\Sigma$, consistent with the restoring force interpretation.
\end{proof}

This is the economic content of energy conservation: the Hamiltonian flow has no
``free energy'' to give away around a cycle, so the only way the maker pays out is
through deliberate subsidy (next) and the only way a trader profits from a cycle is
by supplying fees.

\subsection{Bounded market-maker subsidy}

A market maker that quotes a finite price for the first unit of a brand-new market
necessarily subsidizes price discovery: early trades move inventory cheaply, and if
the ``true'' price later settles far away the maker realizes a loss. Hanson's LMSR
famously bounds this loss by a constant. The HMM bounds it by the \emph{range of
the conjugate potential}, and LMSR's bound falls out as the entropic instance.

\begin{theorem}[Bounded subsidy]
\label{thm:bounded-loss}
Suppose the market opens at inventory $q_0$ with $C(q_0)=0$ (the maker has taken in
no cash yet) and that admissible inventories are confined to a set
$\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\R$ on which $C$ is bounded below by $C_{\min} =
\inf_{q\in\mathcal{Q}} C(q)$. Then over \emph{any} sequence of trades that ends at
some terminal inventory $q_T\in\mathcal{Q}$, the maker's net payout (cash out minus
cash in) is at most
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Subsidy} \;=\; C(q_0) - C(q_T) \;\le\; -\,C_{\min} \;=\; C(q_0) - C_{\min}.
\label{eq:subsidy}
\end{equation}
In particular the worst-case subsidy is the constant $C(q_0)-C_{\min}$, independent
of the path and of the number of trades.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:path} the total cash the maker has \emph{received} from traders
between open and close is $C(q_T)-C(q_0)$ (negative when it has paid out on net).
The maker's net payout is the negative of its receipts, $C(q_0)-C(q_T)$. Since
$q_T\in\mathcal{Q}$, $C(q_T)\ge C_{\min}$, hence
$C(q_0)-C(q_T)\le C(q_0)-C_{\min}$. With $C(q_0)=0$ this is $-C_{\min}$. The bound
references only endpoints, so it is path- and count-independent.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[LMSR is the entropic HMM, with subsidy $b\ln N$]
\label{cor:lmsr}
Let the market have $N$ exclusive outcomes with inventory vector
$\bm{q}\in\R^N$ and take the entropic cost potential (the log-partition function)
\begin{equation}
C(\bm{q}) \;=\; b\,\ln\!\sum_{i=1}^N e^{q_i/b}, \qquad b>0,
\label{eq:lmsr}
\end{equation}
whose gradient $p_i = \partial C/\partial q_i = e^{q_i/b}/\sum_j e^{q_j/b}$ is the
LMSR price (a probability on the simplex). Open at $\bm{q}_0=\bm 0$, so
$C(\bm{q}_0)=b\ln N$. At resolution to outcome $i^\star$ the maker pays out
$q_{i^\star}\le\max_i q_i$ per share held against the cash $C(\bm q)-C(\bm 0)$ it
took in. Then the maker's worst-case net loss is the constant
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Subsidy}_{\max}
   \;=\; \sup_{\bm q}\Big(\max_i q_i - \big(C(\bm q)-C(\bm 0)\big)\Big)
   \;=\; b\ln N,
\end{equation}
recovering Hanson's bound exactly.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$C(\bm 0)=b\ln\sum_i e^0 = b\ln N$. The maker's net loss at state $\bm q$ is the
payout minus the receipts, $\max_i q_i - \big(C(\bm q)-C(\bm 0)\big)$. Since
$C(\bm q)=b\ln\sum_i e^{q_i/b}\ge b\ln e^{\max_i q_i/b}=\max_i q_i$, we have
$\max_i q_i - C(\bm q)\le 0$, with the bound approached ($\to 0$) as one coordinate
dominates. Hence the supremum of the net loss equals $C(\bm 0)=b\ln N$. This is the
standard LMSR worst-case-loss computation, here read off the general HMM bound
\eqref{eq:subsidy}: $C(\bm 0)=b\ln N$ is the open value and $\sup(\max_i q_i - C)=0$
plays the role of $-C_{\min}$.
\end{proof}

Corollary~\ref{cor:lmsr} is the payoff of putting LMSR inside the Hamiltonian
frame: its celebrated bounded loss is not special to the logarithm, it is the
general HMM subsidy bound \eqref{eq:subsidy} instantiated at the entropic potential.
A compute market chooses the liquidity parameter $b$ (or, in the scalar compute
case, the curvature of $C$) to trade off depth against the constant subsidy it is
willing to spend to bootstrap price discovery.

\subsection{The monotone price--supply law (the PoAI coupling)}

\begin{theorem}[Monotone price--supply law]
\label{thm:monotone}
Let $s\ge 0$ denote the cumulative supply of \emph{verified} PCUs the maker has
absorbed (each a distinct, Freivalds-verified, non-double-claimed unit; see
\S\ref{sec:hmm-poai}), and let inventory track supply, $q = q_0 - s$ (absorbing
supply lowers the maker's net short position). Then the quoted price
$p(s) = C'(q_0 - s)$ is strictly decreasing in $s$ on the buy side and, dually, the
price a \emph{consumer} pays per PCU is strictly increasing in demand pressure; in
both cases the sensitivity is governed by the curvature:
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd p}{\dd s} \;=\; -\,C''(q_0 - s) \;<\; 0,
\qquad
\Big|\frac{\dd p}{\dd s}\Big| \;=\; C''(q) \;>\; 0.
\label{eq:supplylaw}
\end{equation}
Consequently the price moves \emph{only} in response to changes in \emph{verified}
supply: an unverified claim never reaches $q$ (it is rejected before the inventory
update, \S\ref{sec:hmm-poai}), so $\dd p/\dd s = 0$ along it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$p(s)=C'(q_0-s)$; differentiate, $\dd p/\dd s = -C''(q_0-s)<0$ by strict
convexity, with magnitude $C''(q)$. The second clause is structural rather than
analytic: the inventory update $q\mapsto q-1$ that drives the price is executed by
the mint path \emph{only after} the PoAI compute proof and the no-double-mint claim
succeed (Eq.~\eqref{eq:mintgate} below), so a claim that fails verification leaves
$q$, and hence $p$, unchanged: $\dd p/\dd s\big|_{\text{unverified}}=0$.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone} is the formal statement of ``the market prices
\emph{verified} useful work, not raw tokens.'' The curvature $C''$ is the single
knob: large $C''$ means a deep, slow-moving price (much verified supply needed to
move it); small $C''$ means a shallow, responsive price.

\subsection{Slippage is convex}

\begin{proposition}[Convex slippage / quote-width]
\label{prop:slippage}
The average price paid for a block trade of size $\delta$ starting at inventory
$q$,
\begin{equation}
\bar p(q,\delta) \;=\; \frac{C(q+\delta)-C(q)}{\delta},
\end{equation}
is increasing in $\delta$ for $\delta>0$, and
$\bar p(q,\delta)\to C'(q)$ as $\delta\to 0$. The realized slippage
$\bar p(q,\delta)-C'(q)$ is nonnegative and convex in $\delta$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\bar p$ is the average of $C'$ over $[q,q+\delta]$; since $C'$ is increasing
(strict convexity of $C$), this average increases with $\delta$ and tends to the
left endpoint value $C'(q)$ as $\delta\to0$. Slippage
$\bar p - C'(q) = \tfrac1\delta\int_q^{q+\delta}(C'(u)-C'(q))\,\dd u\ge 0$;
its convexity in $\delta$ follows from $C'$ increasing (the second difference of
$C$ is nonnegative).
\end{proof}

This is the standard depth guarantee: large trades pay strictly more per unit, by
an amount the maker controls through $C''$, and the marginal quote is recovered in
the small-trade limit.

\section{LMSR and Uniswap are two potentials of one framework}
\label{sec:hmm-unify}

The Hamiltonian frame is not a new market maker bolted next to the known ones; it
is the genus of which they are species. A market maker is an HMM precisely when its
price one-form is exact --- when it has a potential. We make the two canonical cases
explicit.

\paragraph{LMSR (Hanson): the entropic potential.} Take
$C(\bm q)=b\ln\sum_i e^{q_i/b}$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:lmsr}). The price is the softmax,
$p_i = \mathrm{softmax}(\bm q/b)_i$, a point on the probability simplex; the
Hamiltonian $H=\inner{\bm p}{\bm q}-C(\bm q)$ restricted to the quote manifold is
the negative Shannon entropy scaled by $b$ (the conjugate of log-sum-exp is the
negative entropy on the simplex). Bounded subsidy $b\ln N$ is
Corollary~\ref{cor:lmsr}. This is the right maker for an \emph{outcome} market ---
which model wins, which answer is correct --- where prices must be a probability
distribution.

\paragraph{Uniswap (constant function): the logarithmic-reserve potential.} A
constant-function market maker holds reserves $(x,y)\in\Rpos^2$ and keeps an
invariant $\varphi(x,y)=\kappa$ fixed across every trade. For Uniswap,
$\varphi(x,y)=x\,y$, so $H_{\mathrm{cf}}(x,y) = x\,y = \kappa$ is \emph{literally}
the conserved Hamiltonian --- the constant-function invariant \emph{is} an energy.
The marginal price of $x$ in units of $y$ along the curve is
$p = -\dd y/\dd x = y/x$ (differentiate $xy=\kappa$). One can recover these quotes
from a scalar generator: parametrizing by $u=\ln x$ and setting
$g(u) = -\kappa e^{-u} = -y$ gives $g'(u)=\kappa e^{-u}=y$, so the quoted price is
$g'(u)/x = y/x$. We call $x\cdot y=k$ a \emph{degenerate} conserved quantity for two
reasons. First, the generator $g$ is concave rather than strictly convex, so it is
not a cost potential in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:cost}; the
constant-function maker conserves the invariant $\varphi=xy$ directly, and it is
this invariant (not a convex cost) that plays the Hamiltonian role. Second, unlike
LMSR's potential, the constant-product invariant is a single hypersurface with no
liquidity parameter $b$ separating depth from subsidy --- depth and price-impact are
locked together by $\kappa$ alone. The shared structure that makes both HMMs is
still the same: the price one-form is exact (here $p\,\dd x = -\dd y$ along
$\varphi=\kappa$). This is the right maker for a \emph{fungible-pair} market with
two-sided reserves.

\begin{proposition}[Constant-function divergence-loss bound]
\label{prop:uniloss}
For the Uniswap potential, an LP whose position experiences a price ratio
$r = p_1/p_0$ between deposit and withdrawal suffers value ratio
\begin{equation}
\frac{V_{\text{pool}}}{V_{\text{hold}}} \;=\; \frac{2\sqrt r}{1+r} \;\le\; 1,
\end{equation}
with equality iff $r=1$; the loss $1 - 2\sqrt r/(1+r)$ is the constant-function
analogue of the bounded subsidy and is itself bounded on any bounded price range.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Along $xy=\kappa$ with price $p=y/x$ we have $x=\sqrt{\kappa/p}$ and
$y=\sqrt{\kappa p}$. The pool value (in the numéraire $y$) is
$V_{\text{pool}}=p\,x+y = \sqrt{\kappa p}+\sqrt{\kappa p}=2\sqrt{\kappa p}
= 2\sqrt{\kappa p_0}\,\sqrt r$, using $p=r p_0$. Holding the initial basket
$(x_0,y_0)=(\sqrt{\kappa/p_0},\sqrt{\kappa p_0})$ gives
$V_{\text{hold}} = p\,x_0+y_0 = r p_0\sqrt{\kappa/p_0}+\sqrt{\kappa p_0}
= \sqrt{\kappa p_0}\,(r+1)$. Hence
$V_{\text{pool}}/V_{\text{hold}} = 2\sqrt r/(1+r)$. By AM--GM $1+r\ge 2\sqrt r$, so
the ratio is $\le 1$ with equality iff $r=1$.
\end{proof}

The unification is the point: \emph{path independence, no-arbitrage, and a bounded
maker loss are not features one bolts onto a market maker; they are automatic
consequences of the price one-form being exact.} LMSR and Uniswap are the two
potentials practitioners already use; the HMM names the structure they share and
lets a compute market choose a potential fit to its own good --- which for PCUs is a
strictly convex scalar (or weighted multi-asset) $C$ with a tunable curvature.

\section{Multi-asset HMM over heterogeneous compute}
\label{sec:hmm-multi}

Compute is not one good. A PCU on an H100 at \code{int8}, a PCU on consumer
silicon at \code{fp16}, a long-context decode, and a training step are different
resources with different scarcity. We lift the scalar HMM to a vector of $n$
compute classes with a \emph{separable weighted} potential, which keeps every
theorem of \S\ref{sec:hmm-conservation} and adds cross-class structure only where
wanted.

\begin{definition}[Weighted multi-asset HMM]
\label{def:multi}
Let $\bm q=(q_1,\dots,q_n)\in\R^n$ be inventories of $n$ compute classes with
weights $\bm w$, $w_i>0$, $\sum_i w_i=1$, and per-class strictly-convex potentials
$c_i$. The pool potential is
\begin{equation}
C(\bm q) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^n w_i\, c_i(q_i),
\qquad
p_i \;=\; \frac{\partial C}{\partial q_i} \;=\; w_i\, c_i'(q_i),
\label{eq:multic}
\end{equation}
and the Hamiltonian is $H(\bm q,\bm p)=\inner{\bm p}{\bm q}-C(\bm q)$ with the
canonical bracket $\{F,G\}=\sum_i(\partial_{q_i}F\,\partial_{p_i}G -
\partial_{p_i}F\,\partial_{q_i}G)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Multi-asset guarantees]
\label{prop:multi}
For the weighted multi-asset HMM of Definition~\ref{def:multi}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,label=(\alph*)]
  \item (Path independence) $\sum_i p_i\,\dd q_i = \dd C$ is exact; the cost of
        moving $\bm q_0\to\bm q_1$ is $C(\bm q_1)-C(\bm q_0)$, route-independent.
  \item (No arbitrage) The no-arbitrage manifold is
        $\{\bm p = \nabla C(\bm q)\}$; off it, the gap $\bm p - \nabla C(\bm q)$ is
        the per-unit arbitrage profit vector.
  \item (Bounded subsidy) The worst-case subsidy is
        $C(\bm q_0)-\inf_{\bm q\in\mathcal Q}C(\bm q)$, the multi-asset form of
        \eqref{eq:subsidy}.
  \item (Permutation symmetry / no preferred class) If $w_i=w_j$ and $c_i=c_j$,
        the potential $C$ is invariant under the inventory swap
        $\sigma:(q_i,q_j)\mapsto(q_j,q_i)$, so the maker quotes the two classes by
        the \emph{same} curve: $p_i = p_j$ whenever $q_i=q_j$, and the equal-inventory
        diagonal $\{q_i=q_j\}$ is invariant under the symmetric two-class flow. No
        class is systematically cheaper to drain than its identical twin.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a)--(c) are the gradient-field versions of Theorems~\ref{thm:path},
\ref{thm:roundtrip}, and~\ref{thm:bounded-loss}: a separable sum of exact
one-forms is exact, and the endpoint accounting is unchanged. For (d): with
$w_i=w_j=:w$ and $c_i=c_j=:c$ the summand $w_i c_i(q_i)+w_j c_j(q_j)
=w(c(q_i)+c(q_j))$ is symmetric in $(q_i,q_j)$, and the other summands do not
involve these two coordinates, so $C\circ\sigma=C$. Hence the quoted prices
$p_i=w\,c'(q_i)$ and $p_j=w\,c'(q_j)$ are the \emph{same} function of the
respective inventory, giving $p_i=p_j$ exactly when $q_i=q_j$. By Noether's theorem
the discrete symmetry $\sigma$ maps trajectories to trajectories; in particular the
fixed-point set $\{q_i=q_j\}$ of $\sigma$ is invariant under the
$\sigma$-symmetric flow generated by $H$ (a trajectory starting on the diagonal
maps to itself under $\sigma$ and so cannot leave it). Thus the maker has no
curve-level bias between identical classes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why not an ``angular momentum'']
\label{rem:noL}
One is tempted to claim a conserved $L_{ij}=q_i p_j-q_j p_i$ by analogy with
rotational symmetry. For the cost-function maker this is \emph{false} in general:
a direct computation gives $\{L_{ij},H\}=w\,(q_i\,c'(q_j)-q_j\,c'(q_i))$, which
vanishes only on the diagonal $q_i=q_j$, not identically. We therefore claim only
the genuine discrete permutation symmetry above. A continuous rotational symmetry
would require a rotationally invariant $C$ (e.g. $C$ a function of $\lVert\bm
q\rVert$ alone), which is not the separable potential a heterogeneous compute
market wants; importing the conserved $L_{ij}$ without that hypothesis is exactly
the kind of unforced ``physics'' the construction is built to avoid
(Remark~\ref{rem:why}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[No spurious dynamics]
\label{rem:nokinetic}
We deliberately do \emph{not} add a kinetic term $\frac12\sum_i\mu_i p_i^2$ to
$H$. Such a term makes ``mass'' nonzero and yields oscillatory price dynamics, but
those oscillations have no market meaning --- a market maker is not a pendulum, and
a momentum that carries price past its quote is precisely the value leak
path-independence is meant to forbid. The clean conservation laws above hold
because $H$ is the bonded value and nothing else (Remark~\ref{rem:why}). Heterogeneity
is expressed through the weights $\bm w$ and the per-class curvatures $c_i''$,
which is where it belongs.
\end{remark}

\section{Binding the curve to verified supply}
\label{sec:hmm-poai}

The HMM's guarantees are only as meaningful as the inventory axis $q$. If $q$ could
be incremented by an unverified claim --- a modal guess --- the maker would price
fiction. PoAI is precisely what makes each unit of $q$ a distinct, verified,
non-replayable PCU. Three on-chain mechanisms, all read in
\S\S\ref{sec:freivalds}--\ref{sec:status}, enforce this, and we state the
composite mint gate.

\paragraph{(1) Bonded supply: \code{MinerStakeRegistry}.} A miner that supplies
compute must publicly bond capital in proportion to the capacity it declares. The
contract holds, for each miner, \code{bonded} wei and \code{capacity} units, and
exposes the single read the mint path needs:
\begin{equation}
\code{eligible}(m) \;\Longleftrightarrow\;
\neg\code{blacklisted}(m)\ \wedge\ \code{unbondAt}(m)=0\ \wedge\
\code{bonded}(m)\ \ge\ \max\!\big(\code{minBond},\ \code{capacity}(m)\cdot
\code{bondPerUnit}\big).
\label{eq:eligible}
\end{equation}
Three authorities are kept orthogonal (one per concern): the \emph{miner} controls
its own bond with a withdrawal cooldown; the \emph{slasher} --- the compute-proof
fraud verifier --- slashes the bond on a \emph{proven} discrepancy, no vote; and
\emph{governance} may blacklist for off-protocol harm. The cooldown is the fraud
window: a miner cannot dodge a fraud proof by exiting first, because \code{slash}
works during the cooldown. Economically, the bond is the supply-side honesty
guarantee feeding the curve: producing a PCU puts real capital at risk against the
Freivalds check of \S\ref{sec:freivalds}, so the expected value of a fabricated
unit is negative.

\paragraph{(2) No-proof-no-mint: \code{AIMiner}.} The increment of $q$ (the
mint of the subsidy that pays for one PCU) is gated, in pure Solidity that runs on
any EVM, on a valid \code{ComputeProof}. The contract fails \emph{closed}: if no
verifier is wired it reverts \code{ProofGateUnavailable}; when wired, it recomputes
the expected \code{reportData} from the receipt's own merkle-proven fields and
requires
\begin{equation}
\code{verifier.verify}\big(\code{computeProof},\
  \code{ComputeProofLib.expectedReportData}(\dots),\ \code{runtimeMeasurement}\big)
  \;=\;\texttt{true}.
\label{eq:mintgate}
\end{equation}
A forged receipt root alone (the ``C3'' hole) no longer mints: inclusion under a
root proves the chain committed the receipt, and the compute proof proves the
output is real computation. This is the on-chain face of the
signature/plurality/computation distinction of \S\ref{sec:intro}.

\paragraph{(3) Network-wide no double mint: \code{AIComputeRegistry}.} A prover
could do the work once and submit distinct, chain-bound proofs of the \emph{same}
computation to many chains, minting $N$ times for one unit. The registry keys on
the \emph{chain-independent} commitment
\begin{equation}
\code{computationKey} \;=\;
\keccak\big(\text{\small DOMAIN}\,\|\,\code{modelSpec}\,\|\,\code{promptHash}
  \,\|\,\code{outputHash}\big),
\end{equation}
records the first claimant, and reverts \code{AlreadyClaimed} for any second claim
from any miner on any chain. First-correct-proof wins; redundant recomputation
earns nothing. This guarantees the inventory increments are \emph{distinct} units,
so $q$ counts each PCU once.

\paragraph{The composite supply axiom.} Together, \eqref{eq:eligible},
\eqref{eq:mintgate}, and the registry give the property
Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone} assumes:

\begin{proposition}[Verified-supply integrity]
\label{prop:supply}
Every increment $q\mapsto q-1$ on the buy side of the HMM corresponds to a unit of
compute that is (i) produced by a miner satisfying \eqref{eq:eligible}, hence bonded
and slashable; (ii) accompanied by a \code{ComputeProof} satisfying
\eqref{eq:mintgate}, hence Freivalds-verified and bound to a measured
$(\code{modelSpec},\code{promptHash},\code{outputHash})$; and (iii) claimed once
network-wide via a unique \code{computationKey}. No other transition increments
$q$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The only path that mints the subsidy (and thereby moves inventory) is
\code{AIMiner.mine}, which in sequence: checks the receipt shape and finality;
proves merkle inclusion under an accepted root; requires \eqref{eq:mintgate} or
reverts (fail-closed); claims the \code{computationKey} on the registry, reverting
on a duplicate; and only then calls \code{mintSubsidy}, which is itself restricted
to contract minters (the god-key defense) and clamped to the halving allowance.
Each conjunct of the claim is one of these gates; the absence of any other minter
of the coin (enforced by \code{setMinter} requiring contract code and the
single \code{mintSubsidy} entry point) gives the ``no other transition'' clause.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Coupling to issuance.} The coin the HMM denominates is itself bounded:
\code{AICoin} is a capped, halving, fair-launch subsidy with
$\code{MAX\_SUBSIDY}=10^9$ and a four-year halving period, vesting
\begin{equation}
\code{cumulativeAllowance}(t) \;=\; \mathrm{MAX}\big(1-2^{-k}\big)
   + \big(\mathrm{MAX}\cdot 2^{-(k+1)}\big)\cdot\frac{t \bmod T}{T},
   \qquad k=\Big\lfloor \tfrac{t}{T}\Big\rfloor,\ T = 4\ \text{years},
\label{eq:emission}
\end{equation}
with \code{emissionAllowance}$(t)=\code{cumulativeAllowance}(t)-\code{mintedSubsidy}$
clamped at zero, so cumulative mint can never exceed the cap. There is a clean
duality here: the \emph{issuance} side bounds how much coin can ever enter
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:emission}, a geometric sum to $\mathrm{MAX}$), and the \emph{market}
side bounds how much the maker can ever subsidize price discovery
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:subsidy}, the conjugate range). Both are constants chosen in
advance; neither is an oracle. The HMM prices PoAI-verified compute into this
fixed-supply coin, and every quantity in the loop --- the proof, the bond, the
double-mint guard, the curve, the cap --- is a mechanism we read in the shipped
code, not a parameter we wished for.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A network that pays for AI must do two hard things, and we have done both with one
honest seam. It must \emph{verify computation}, not authorship or agreement: the
decisive observation is that a forward pass is almost entirely matmuls, that a
matmul can be \emph{verified} an order of magnitude below the cost of
\emph{performing} it, and that on the engine's exact integer accumulator this
verification is bit-exact with zero false-reject and information-theoretically
sound, hence post-quantum. PoAI builds directly on the Hanzo engine: one Freivalds
primitive over $\Fp$, specialized into four proofs, lifted to whole-graph
soundness by typed composition, made sound by determinism, made asynchronous by a
streaming Merkle Mountain Range with interactive bisection, and made unforgeable by
a measured-weights binding into \code{reportData}. And it must \emph{price} that
verified work without an oracle: the Hamiltonian Market Maker is the principled
generalization of LMSR and constant-function AMMs --- a market maker whose conserved
bonded value gives path independence, no in-curve arbitrage, and a bounded
subsidy as \emph{theorems}, and whose monotone price--supply law ties the AICoin
price of compute to the supply of \emph{verified} PCUs. The bond and the
no-double-mint registry are what make that supply axis honest; the capped halving
coin is what the curve denominates. The proof core, the graph composition, the
determinism, and all four supply-side contracts are proven, tested, and enforced
today; emitting the proof end-to-end from a running zen model is the next, designed
slice. The economics are complete on paper because they are theorems; the systems
are complete in code because we cite where they live.
