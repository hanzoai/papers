\begin{abstract}
We report an observational study of a single multi-week campaign to close the inference-throughput gap
against \code{llama.cpp} on three accelerators (AMD gfx1151 RDNA3.5 APU, NVIDIA GB10 Blackwell, Apple M4
Max), conducted inside a one-source GPU kernel DSL. The campaign's primary artifact is not a kernel but a
\emph{refutation log}: twenty-five numbered hypotheses, most of them plausible and several of them
our own, each killed by a cheap decisive experiment. We formalize the practice behind it. First, the
quantified outcomes: a Vulkan-prefill \emph{win ladder} of $212\to1734$ tok/s ($8.2\x$ of the gap closed in
a single $\sim$36-hour release cadence, from $11.6\x$ behind the reference to $1.40\x$), decomposed per
lever; a CUDA decode gap closed $1.23\x\to1.003\x$ by a single model-eligibility list entry with
\emph{zero} kernel work; and an instrument fix that collapsed
prefill benchmark noise from $\pm42\%$ to $\pm0.8\%$ ($52\x$) and thereby made the second half of the win
ladder measurable at all. Second, three algorithms that recur across the log: a multi-fidelity
\emph{Gate Ladder} that rejects candidates cheapest-first (free static rejection $\to$ bit-exact oracle
$\to$ sustained per-op timing $\to$ in-engine wall $\to$ coherence); a \emph{decisive-experiment}
selection rule (the cheapest single-variable control that can falsify a live hypothesis); and
\emph{refutation-log-as-prior}, negative constraints injected into later work, which provably prevented at
least nine documented re-chases. Third, the measurement doctrine as testable claims (same-run ratios only;
sustained-vs-sustained; contended-timing refusal; warm-microbench distrust). We close by describing how the
discipline was operationalized as a 22-case language-model evaluation whose distractors are the campaign's
own refuted theories. We do not overclaim the standing: across the measured fronts the campaign holds two
wins (ROCm decode $1.07\x$; a $470\x$ margin over the fork's upstream on Metal), one parity (CUDA decode),
and three open gaps (Vulkan prefill/decode, CUDA prefill) --- ``beat everywhere'' is the objective, not the
achieved state. This is an $N{=}1$ study with no control group; we state that boundary explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\section{Thesis: the refutation is the deliverable}
\label{sec:thesis}

Conventional performance write-ups report wins. This campaign's ledger is dominated by \emph{kills}: of the
levers seriously proposed and evaluated, the majority were refuted, and refuting them was the value
produced --- because a refuted lever, recorded with its mechanism, removes an entire branch of future work.
The campaign ran against a fixed external baseline (\code{llama.cpp}, the reference LLM inference engine)
on the same silicon and the same model files, so every claim reduces to a measured ratio on shared
hardware. The engineering question was never ``is our kernel fast?'' but ``\emph{which} of these competing
stories about the bottleneck survives contact with an instrument?''

The stakes are concrete. On the AMD gfx1151 APU (evo), our Vulkan prefill began at $212$ tok/s against a
same-box \code{llama.cpp} figure of $2459$ --- an $11.6\x$ deficit. Twenty-five refutations later it stood
at $1739.6\,{\pm}\,7.4$ vs $2441.9\,{\pm}\,22.2$ same-run, $1.40\x$ behind. The distance between those endpoints is a
sequence of decisions, and most of the decisions were \emph{not to do the attractive thing}. This paper is
about how those decisions were made and what they cost.

\paragraph{Environment.} Three self-hosted boxes, each its own backend: \textbf{evo} --- AMD Ryzen AI
Max+~395 / Radeon 8060S, gfx1151 (RDNA3.5 APU, unified memory), bare-metal Linux 6.17, ROCm 7.2.4, Vulkan
via RADV; \textbf{spark} --- NVIDIA GB10, compute capability 12.1 (sm\_121, Blackwell), aarch64;
\textbf{dbc} --- Apple M4 Max, 40-core GPU, macOS~26. Models: \code{zen-eco-4b} (a Qwen2-3B derivative)
Q4\_K\_M, and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct Q4\_K\_M (18.5\,GB). Reference: \code{llama.cpp} (Vulkan/CUDA/Metal/HIP
backends) and its \code{llama-bench}, run on the identical box and GGUF file. All comparisons in this paper
are same-box, same-model; cross-box numbers are labelled as such.

\section{The refutation dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

Table~\ref{tab:refutations} is the study's core observation: a census of the campaign's hypotheses,
classified by the \emph{kind} of story each told and annotated with the decisive experiment that settled
it, its approximate cost to kill, and what killing it saved. We use four hypothesis classes:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{mechanistic-kernel} --- a claim that some change to a compute kernel (fuse these ops, cut this
dispatch count, use tensor cores, enlarge the tile) will move the wall clock.
\item \textbf{configuration-gap} --- the bottleneck is not the kernel but a build flag, an eligibility
list, a harness setting, or a dispatch-orchestration pattern.
\item \textbf{measurement-artifact} --- the ``finding'' was an instrument lie: a warm microbenchmark, a
warmup-shape mismatch, contended timing, or mixed-phase telemetry sampling.
\item \textbf{hardware-story} --- a narrative about the silicon: thermal throttling, a DVFS
utilization spiral, a sustained power limit.
\end{itemize}

The ladder's shape is itself evidence for the thesis. The first rung ($3.3\x$, Q6\_K tiling) was found not
by intuition but by a per-op profiler that named \code{mul\_mat\_q6k} as $76\%$ of prefill GPU time --- a
quant type that had no tiled path because it appears in only $\sim$2 tensors per layer (\code{attn\_v},

rung was a small, measured, single-variable change read out of the reference implementation's source and
A/B'd in-engine. No rung came from a redesign.

\subsection{The CUDA gap decomposition: configuration vs kernel}
\label{sec:cuda}

The CUDA front is the cleanest natural experiment separating configuration from kernel work, because its two
phases resolved differently. On spark (GB10, \code{zen-eco-4b} Q4\_K\_M, same-run):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Decode} began at $63.3\,{\pm}\,0.2$ vs \code{llama} $77.6\,{\pm}\,0.9$ ($1.23\x$). The
profiler (\code{nsys}) showed $\sim$1{,}110 kernel launches per token against \code{llama}'s $\sim$1 graph
launch. The engine already had a decode command-graph path, fail-closed behind a bit-exact gate and a
\emph{model-eligibility list}. \code{Model::Qwen} was simply not in the list (and decode RoPE read a
host-side offset that freezes under graph capture --- fixed by the device-positions pattern the eligible
models already used). Adding the one arm collapsed launches to $\sim$1 per token and took decode to the
CTO-frozen $81.9\,{\pm}\,0.6$ vs \code{llama} $82.15\,{\pm}\,1.76$ --- $1.23\x\to1.003\x$ \textbf{parity,
with zero kernel rewrites}.
\item \textbf{Prefill} remains open at $5003.4\,{\pm}\,23.0$ vs \code{llama} $6079\,{\pm}\,326$ ($0.82\x$)
and is glue-bound: naive attention \code{softmax\_f32} $\sim$13--15\%, \code{badd\_bf16} $10.5\%$, dtype
casts $6.2\%$, \code{ucopy} $4.2\%$. Here the attractive lever was itself refuted (\#25): building with the
\code{flash-attn} feature made prefill $3.6\%$ \emph{slower} ($4820.8\,{\pm}\,166.3$), because the paged
bench's gather path forces naive attention regardless and Ampere-era flash-v2 kernels do not win on
Blackwell. The residual is genuine kernel/dtype work (route \code{flashinfer} prefill, tighten
bf16$\leftrightarrow$f32 discipline) --- in progress.
\end{itemize}

The decomposition is stark: the \emph{entire} CUDA decode deficit was configuration (a graph-eligibility
list entry plus a device-placement fix), closed to statistical parity without touching a compute kernel;
the CUDA prefill deficit is what is actually left for kernel engineering. When a fresh front is opened, the
prior that ``the gap is plumbing'' pays before the prior that ``the gap is a slow kernel.''

\subsection{Instrument variance collapse: $\pm42\% \to \pm0.8\%$}
\label{sec:variance}

For much of the campaign the prefill benchmark was unusable for A/B testing: repeated runs of the same
configuration reported $728\,{\pm}\,306$ tok/s ($\pm42\%$), and in the worst observations $\pm48$--$62\%$.
The cause was a harness defect, not the GPU: warmup ran a fixed 32-token prefill while the measured batch
was 512 tokens, so the first measured forward paid a one-time Vulkan pipeline compile and the warmup
touched a different shape than the measurement. The fix (engine v1.7.60) warms the \emph{measured} shapes;
prefill went to $946.5\,{\pm}\,7.6$ ($\pm0.8\%$) --- a $\mathbf{52\x}$ variance collapse.

The downstream effect is what makes this a headline rather than a footnote. A change worth $+5\%$ is
invisible under $\pm42\%$ noise (the ratio of signal to noise is $0.12$); it is unambiguous under
$\pm0.8\%$. Decode was always tight ($\pm1.3\%$), so decode A/Bs were resolvable throughout --- which is
exactly why the decode wins landed earlier. Prefill A/Bs became resolvable \emph{only after} the harness
fix, and the entire second half of the win ladder ($946\to1734$, the packed-LDS, Q6\_K-coopmat, and
single-buffer rungs, each a $1.2$--$1.7\x$ op-level change) was A/B-decided on the honest bench. The
instrument fix did not speed up a kernel; it made three subsequent kernel wins \emph{legible}.

\subsection{The 32-token warmup phantom: one artifact, three dead theories}
\label{sec:phantom}

The most expensive single artifact of the campaign was the same warmup-shape bug viewed from the other
side. A profiler labelled every prefill batch \code{dispatch=1949} --- because dispatch count is
per-layer-ops $\times$ 36 layers, hence \emph{token-count-independent}. The 32-token warmup batches
therefore printed an identical dispatch count to the 512-token measured batches while doing $16\x$ less
work (byte counters: $334.6$ vs $8364$ MB). The visible symptom --- ``the first $\sim$5 forwards run
$\sim$120 ms, then collapse to $\sim$523 ms'' --- looked like accumulating degradation, and spawned three
competing hardware/leak stories:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item a \emph{DVFS utilization death-spiral} (mixed-phase telemetry read $29\%$ busy @ 713 MHz for us vs
$77\%$ @ 2431 MHz for \code{llama} on the same silicon);
\item \emph{accumulating engine state} / a per-forward memory leak;
\item a \emph{sustained power throttle}.
\end{enumerate}

All three were killed by two single-variable controls plus one counter read. Pinning the clock at 2900 MHz
left the 512-token forward at $540$ ms (throttle and downclock require the clock to move --- it did not).
Running \code{warmup=0} made forward \#1 $573$ ms and every subsequent forward $\sim$545--561 ms flat (no
accumulation; stable from the first forward). And the byte counters showed the ``fast'' forwards were
simply smaller. The tell, in hindsight, was that \emph{the fast-window length always equalled the warmup
count}. One mislabelled invariant --- treating a shape-independent dispatch count as workload identity ---
manufactured three plausible mechanisms and consumed multi-agent effort until the counters ended it. The
lesson entered the log as a rule: \emph{dispatch count is not workload identity; check tokens and bytes
before comparing two batches.}

\subsection{Optimization velocity: the campaign clock}
\label{sec:velocity}

If the tooling of \S\ref{sec:algorithms} has a reason to exist, it is velocity: the loop must close gaps
faster than unaided hand-tuning, or it is ceremony. The campaign's release history is the direct evidence,
and its timestamps are real (Table~\ref{tab:velocity}, from the \code{ml} and \code{engine} git logs). The

($946.5\to1734.4$) landed inside a single window of $\sim$5.5 hours (11:28--14:58 on 2026-07-18). The CUDA
decode close to parity (\S\ref{sec:cuda}) shipped the same evening. Twenty-five numbered refutations gate
this cadence, each with a cost-to-kill of minutes to hours (Table~\ref{tab:refutations}).

\paragraph{The honest contrast.} It is tempting to set this cadence against the maturity of the baseline,
and the comparison must be made carefully. \code{llama.cpp}'s Vulkan matmul lead was built incrementally by
many contributors over roughly two years: its Vulkan shader path dates to mid-2024, cooperative-matrix
support to December 2024 (PRs \#10206/\#10713), the DP4A MMQ + Q8\_1 quantization shader to March 2025
(PR \#12135), and a \code{mul\_mm} shader cleanup to September 2025 (PR \#15987), among others --- dates read
directly from the local \code{llama.cpp} checkout's git history. We closed $8.2\x$ of the gap to that
artifact in $\sim$36 hours. This is \textbf{not} a claim that our method is hundreds of times more
productive than theirs: the comparison is method-versus-method \emph{on different starting points}. They
pioneered the kernels with no map; we chased, using their shipped source as the map (the BK, tile, and
buffering constants were read out of their code, then A/B'd on our silicon). \textbf{Chasing a known-good
target is strictly easier than pioneering it}, and the velocity figure reflects that asymmetry as much as it
reflects the loop.

\paragraph{The falsification path.} The velocity claim --- that the loop out-paces hand-tuning --- is
falsifiable, and two controlled experiments are the honest way to settle it, both listed here rather than
assumed. (a)~The in-flight automated schedule search~\cite{tune} is machine-search against our own
hand-search on the \emph{same} kernels: if the machine cannot match or beat the hand-picked schedules per
unit compute, the loop's marginal value over an expert is refuted. (b)~A from-zero replication on an
untouched front (Metal, currently blocked by an offline box) measures time-to-parity with no prior map on
that backend; a slow replication would show the $36$-hour figure was mostly borrowed from the reference,
not generated by the loop. Until those run, the honest statement is narrow: \emph{on this campaign, the
measured cadence out-paced this team's prior hand-tuning}, and the mechanism (cheap refutation plus a map)
is legible in the timestamps.

\section{Three algorithms}
\label{sec:algorithms}

The practices above are not ad hoc; they instantiate three procedures that recur throughout the log.

\subsection{The Gate Ladder}
\label{sec:gateladder}

Verification is multi-fidelity: each candidate passes through gates ordered by increasing cost, and is
rejected at the cheapest gate that can reject it. Free static analysis (compile + shader statistics) filters
occupancy-killers and register spills before any GPU time is spent; the bit-exact oracle enforces
correctness as \emph{viability} (an incorrect kernel is not a slow candidate, it is a non-candidate);
sustained per-op timing on the honest instrument decides ``is it a win''; the in-engine wall A/B and a
coherence check confirm the win survives to the deployed regime.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{\textsc{GateLadder} --- multi-fidelity acceptance, cheapest rejection first}
\label{alg:gate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require candidate schedule $C$, shape $s$, device $d$, incumbent time $t_\star$
\State $(\mathit{spill}, \mathit{lds}, \mathit{occ}) \gets \textsc{Compile+ShaderStats}(C, d)$ \Comment{free; no GPU}
\If{$\mathit{spill} > 0$ \textbf{or} $\mathit{lds} > \textsc{OccBudget}(d)$} \State \Return \textsc{Reject}(``static'') \EndIf
\State $y_C \gets \textsc{Run}(C, s, d)$; \; $y_{\mathrm{ref}} \gets \textsc{CpuOracle}(s)$
\If{$\textsc{ScaleRel}(y_C, y_{\mathrm{ref}}) > \tau$} \State \Return \textsc{Reject}(``not bit-exact'') \Comment{viability, not penalty} \EndIf
\State $t_C \gets \textsc{SustainedPerOp}(C, s, d)$ \Comment{median of 25, $\pm0.4\%$, in-engine} \label{ln:sustained}
\If{$t_C \ge t_\star$} \State \Return \textsc{Reject}(``no op-level win'') \EndIf
\State $(w_C, w_\star, w_{\mathrm{ref}}) \gets \textsc{SameRunWall}(C, \text{incumbent}, \text{llama})$ \Comment{$\pm0.8\%$}
\If{$w_C \not> w_\star$} \State \Return \textsc{Reject}(``no wall win'') \EndIf
\If{\textbf{not} \textsc{Coherent}(C)} \State \Return \textsc{Reject}(``incoherent output'') \EndIf
\State \Return \textsc{Accept}(C) \Comment{ship: bit-exact $\wedge$ op-win $\wedge$ wall-win $\wedge$ coherent}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The ladder's economic point is the ordering. Static rejection is free and, empirically, disqualified a
large fraction of hand-tried variants (register spills, LDS over the occupancy budget) before they ever
reached the GPU. Line~\ref{ln:sustained} is deliberately \emph{not} a cache-warm microbenchmark
(\S\ref{sec:doctrine}); the honest per-op timer is the first gate that costs real GPU seconds, and it is
placed after the two free/cheap gates for that reason.

\subsection{Decisive-experiment selection}
\label{sec:decisive}

Given a set of live hypotheses, the campaign repeatedly chose the \emph{cheapest single-variable
experiment that could falsify the most of them at once}. The pinned-clock control (\S\ref{sec:phantom})
is the exemplar: one knob, minutes, and it bears on both the throttle and the downclock stories
simultaneously. The rule prefers controls that change exactly one variable, because a confounded
experiment cannot cleanly kill anything.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{\textsc{DecisiveExperiment} --- maximize hypotheses-killed per unit cost}
\label{alg:decisive}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require live hypotheses $H$, available experiments $X$
\State $\mathit{best} \gets \varnothing$; \; $\mathit{score}_\star \gets \infty$
\For{$x \in X$ \textbf{such that} $x$ varies exactly one factor} \Comment{single-variable controls only}
  \State $K_x \gets \{\, h \in H : x \text{ can falsify } h \,\}$ \Comment{the kill set}
  \If{$|K_x| = 0$} \State \textbf{continue} \EndIf
  \State $\mathit{score} \gets \textsc{Cost}(x) \,/\, |K_x|$ \Comment{cost per hypothesis killed}
  \If{$\mathit{score} < \mathit{score}_\star$} \State $\mathit{score}_\star \gets \mathit{score}$; \; $\mathit{best} \gets x$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\mathit{best}$ \Comment{e.g.\ pin-clock kills throttle \emph{and} downclock in one run}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Refutation-log-as-prior}
\label{sec:logprior}

A refutation is only worth its cost if it is not re-purchased. Every kill was written to a durable log with
its mechanism, and subsequent task briefs were seeded with the log's negative constraints: forbidden
re-chases and masked dead operators. This converts a one-time experiment into a permanent search-space
restriction.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{\textsc{SeedBrief} --- inject refutations as negative priors}
\label{alg:seed}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require task $T$, refutation log $L$
\State $\mathit{forbid} \gets \varnothing$; \; $\mathit{deadOps} \gets \varnothing$
\For{$r \in L$}
  \If{$\textsc{Class}(r) \in \{\text{mechanistic-kernel}, \text{hardware-story}\}$}
     \State $\mathit{forbid} \gets \mathit{forbid} \cup \{(\,r.\text{lever},\ r.\text{mechanism}\,)\}$
  \EndIf
  \If{$r.\text{outcome} = \text{regress}$} \State $\mathit{deadOps} \gets \mathit{deadOps} \cup \{r.\text{operator}\}$ \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return \textsc{Brief}$(T,\ \mathbf{forbid}{=}\mathit{forbid},\ \mathbf{mask}{=}\mathit{deadOps})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The log records at least nine re-chases this prior provably prevented, each carrying an explicit ``do NOT
re-chase'' marker traceable to a prior measurement: coopmat on gfx1151 (killed, then partially un-refuted
only when the access pattern --- not the tensor cores --- was identified as the cause); \code{BM}${\ge}128$
tiles ($2.4\x$ slower, occupancy collapse); \code{BK}${=}64$ on the coopmat path (llama's override read
correctly); the RN4 / BM=64 tile family (best occupancy, worst time); f16 accumulators (LDS-limited, not
register-limited); the per-shape tile family (skinny projections are $1.9\%$ of Q4\_K time); the Q4\_K
coalesced-cooperative rewrite (already at $83\%$ of peak); dispatch/instruction-count reduction as a
\emph{perf} lever (``10 for 10 --- counts never predict wall-clock on this decode path''); and Ampere-era
\code{flash-attn} v2 on Blackwell ($3.6\%$ slower). Each avoided re-chase is a multi-hour experiment not
re-run.

\section{The measurement doctrine, as testable claims}
\label{sec:doctrine}

The doctrine is four falsifiable rules, each earned by a specific scar.

\paragraph{Same-run ratios only.} The same \code{llama.cpp} on the same box and model reported prefill
figures of $2390.1$, $2404.1$, $2417.8$, $2441.9$, $2459$, $2465.3$, and $2486$ tok/s across the campaign's
days --- a $\pm2\%$ spread from run conditions alone. A ratio computed from our number today against a
\code{llama} number from last week is therefore uninterpretable; only both sides measured in the same
session count. Every ratio in this paper obeys this.

\paragraph{Sustained-vs-sustained.} On the gfx1151 APU, a kernel's \emph{burst} per-op time ($\sim$416 $\mu$s)
and its \emph{sustained} per-op time ($\sim$1634 $\mu$s) differ by $\sim$$4\x$ --- an intrinsic power-limiting
swing confirmed under a pinned clock. Comparing a burst measurement of one variant to a sustained
measurement of another manufactures a $4\x$ phantom. All A/Bs compare sustained to sustained.

\paragraph{Contended-timing refusal.} When a foreign 7\,GB job held the GPU at $94\%$ utilization, the
candidate-vs-baseline A/B returned ``candidate $\approx$ baseline.'' The correct report is not ``refuted''
but ``blocked'': symmetric contention does not make a masked signal fair, it makes it \emph{absent}.
Contention-independent facts (compiles, engages, produces correct output) are still reported as fact; the
timing is deferred to an idle window.

\paragraph{Warm-microbench distrust.} For a memory-bound kernel the cache-warm microbenchmark systematically
lies. The dense \code{dp4a} matvec measured $74\to255$ GB/s ($3.4\x$) with weights hot in the 32 MB
last-level cache, but only $74\to97$ GB/s ($\sim$1.3$\x$) in-engine, where decode reads each weight once,
cold, from unified memory. In the other direction, a warm microbenchmark rated the f16 coopmat GEMM at
$0.07$--$0.20\x$ of \code{dp4a} and it was written off --- yet with a coalesced access pattern the same
matrix-core path became the shipping prefill default. Benchmarks are proxies for deployment; when they
disagree, deployment governs.

\section{Operationalizing the discipline as an evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

The campaign's reasoning discipline is itself a measurable property, and we operationalized it as a
22-case multiple-choice evaluation (\code{roofline}) distilled directly from the log. Every case is a real
decision point: the evidence shown is what was on the table at that moment; the gold answer is what
hardware measurement subsequently proved; and the distractors are the \emph{actual} refuted theories from
Table~\ref{tab:refutations} --- power throttling, occupancy-maximalism, ``port what the reference does,''
warm-microbench trust, contended-timing acceptance, dispatch-count-as-identity. Six of the 22 are
\emph{traps}: the prompt itself asserts a false premise that the provided evidence contradicts, testing
whether a model verifies before it complies. Ground truth is hardware-verified and unpublished, so the set
is uncontaminated by pretraining.

Beyond accuracy, two post-hoc metrics fall out of the standard results log, whose per-item token usages are
already recorded:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{overthink index} --- mean completion tokens on \emph{correct} answers. A discipline that
trusts a decisive measurement answers in hundreds of tokens; an overthinker burns thousands and, on the
trap cases, sometimes reasons its way back \emph{into} the refuted theory.
\item \textbf{trap resistance} --- accuracy on the trap subset alone, where the prompt's own framing is
adversarial.
\end{itemize}

This turns ``measure, don't theorize'' from a slogan into a benchmark: the same skill the campaign
rewarded in engineers is scored, per model, on held-out decision points. It is the pilot instance of a
distillation pipeline (a companion paper develops the loop in full).

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

This is an observational $N{=}1$ study and we do not overclaim from it.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=2pt,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{No control group.} We did not run a parallel team practising non-refutation-driven
engineering on the same problem. We cannot attribute the $11.6\x\to1.40\x$ compression to the methodology
versus the specific people, tools, or the mere passage of time. The claim is that the recorded decisions
were sound given the evidence, not that an alternative method would have failed.
\item \textbf{Single campaign, one team.} All data come from one multi-week effort by one group against one
reference implementation. The hypothesis-class distribution (mechanistic-kernel dominant, configuration-gap
highest-yield) may not generalize beyond low-level GPU work against a mature baseline.
\item \textbf{Hardware specificity.} The mechanisms are tied to particular silicon: gfx1151's unified
memory and LDS-limited occupancy, GB10's Blackwell scheduling, the APU's utilization-slaved DVFS. A
lever's sign can flip across devices (\S on the RN4 tile; the Q4\_K coalescing reversal), so the specific
verdicts do not transfer even though the \emph{method} may.
\item \textbf{Survivorship in the log.} The log records hypotheses that were seriously evaluated. Ideas
discarded before measurement, or never conceived, leave no trace; the census is of considered hypotheses,
not of the full space.
\item \textbf{The win ladder mixes measurement bases.} Its early rungs predate the instrument fix of
\S\ref{sec:variance}; we mark this in Table~\ref{tab:ladder} rather than silently splicing two noise
regimes. The endpoints ($212$ and $1734$) are individually defensible, but the intermediate slope should be
read with the basis change in mind.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Across a multi-week campaign the reliable path to throughput was not a better idea about kernels but a
cheaper way to be wrong about them. The largest wins were configuration gaps that a profiler named in
minutes; the most expensive costs were measurement artifacts that dressed as hardware physics; and the most
durable asset produced was a log of refutations that stopped the same attractive mistakes from being bought
twice. The three procedures --- a cost-ordered gate ladder, a decisive-experiment rule, and
refutation-as-prior --- are simple, and their simplicity is the point: they make the discipline auditable
and, as \S\ref{sec:eval} shows, measurable. The companion paper closes the loop by turning the same gate
ladder into the selector of an automated search, and the same decision log into training signal.

Finally, the standing must be stated without inflation (Table~\ref{tab:scoreboard}). The campaign holds two
wins (ROCm decode $1.07\x$ over \code{llama}-ROCm; a $470\x$ margin over the fork's own upstream on Metal),
one parity (CUDA decode, $1.003\x$), and three still-open gaps (Vulkan prefill $0.71\x$, Vulkan decode
$0.55\x$, CUDA prefill $0.82\x$), with the Metal-vs-\code{llama} front blocked by an offline box. ``Beat
\code{llama} everywhere'' is the standing objective, not the achieved state, and this paper claims only what
the same-run measurements support.
