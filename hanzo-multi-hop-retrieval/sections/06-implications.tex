\section{What this licenses, and what it does not}
\label{sec:implications}

\paragraph{Ruled out, on this corpus.}
Retrieval policy over a dense index --- feedback, neighbour expansion, chained
hops --- as a fix for multi-hop conversational recall. It gained at most
\rBestDelta{} points, cost up to $7\times$ the latency, and lost ground where the
baseline was working. Also ruled out, by \S\ref{sec:diagnostic} rather than by
trial: enlarging $k$, since \rBeyondHundred{}\% of questions have evidence beyond
rank 100.

\paragraph{Not ruled out, and not tested here.}
A better embedding would raise both \textbf{any} and \textbf{all} and we have not
measured its ceiling; the diagnostic predicts the gain would be modest, because
cross-session evidence is far in \emph{any} similarity space over wordings, but
that is a prediction and not a measurement. A cross-encoder reranker over a
larger candidate pool addresses the same axis and inherits the same doubt.

\paragraph{What the diagnostic actually points at.}
The signal the retriever lacks is which turns concern the same entity across
sessions. That is a relation to be \emph{constructed} --- extract entities as
turns arrive, link mentions across sessions, and let retrieval traverse those
links rather than only the similarity graph. This is the family that
entity-and-graph-structured memory systems occupy~\cite{hipporag}, and it is the
one the measurement endorses.

We are naming a direction, not claiming a result. Building it is a larger project
than testing four policies over a cached index, and the honest position at the
end of this paper is that we have established what does not work and why, and
have not yet built what does. That is the whole content of the claim.

\paragraph{One consequence for anyone shipping today.}
Report \textbf{all}, not \textbf{any}, and report the two categories separately.
A system quoting \rManyTwenty{}\% on multi-hop questions is quoting a true number
that describes a system answering \rMallTwenty{}\% of them. The gap between those
figures is not a rounding difference; it is the difference between a question
answered and a confident wrong answer built from two-thirds of the evidence.
