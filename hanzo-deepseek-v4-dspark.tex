\begin{abstract}
We report the bring-up of DeepSeek-V4-Flash --- a 284B-parameter
mixture-of-experts model with hybrid sparse attention (DSA), four-stream
hyper-connections, and a multi-token-prediction (MTP) head --- on Hanzo
Engine, a general-purpose Rust inference stack, running a 2-bit (IQ2)
quantization on a single NVIDIA GB10 (128\,GB unified memory). To our
knowledge this is the only general-purpose engine that executes this
architecture; \texttt{llama.cpp} has no \texttt{deepseek4} support, and the
reference implementation (\texttt{antirez/ds4}) is a model-specific C
engine. We describe a \emph{layer-bisection} methodology --- differencing
per-layer residual norms against an instrumented CPU reference on a
token-identical prompt --- that localized two behavior-defining defects: a
silently-swallowed integer-width mismatch in GGUF metadata parsing that
disabled the compressed-attention architecture on 41 of 43 layers, and the
absence of decode-side compressed-KV emission, which left the model
amnesic beyond its 128-token attention window. Fixing both restored
reference behavior end-to-end (think $\rightarrow$ answer $\rightarrow$
natural EOS over 1{,}127 tokens). A fused F32 online-softmax attention
kernel for the model's 512-wide latent heads, ported from the reference
and extended to speculative verification widths $q \in [1,8]$, raised base
decode 33\% to 11.9--12.5\,tok/s --- ahead of the reference's base
(11.4) --- and made self-speculative MTP decoding net-positive. We further
integrate DeepSeek's DSpark parallel-block draft natively: the Rust port
is bit-exact against the Python reference and yields a measured
$1.70\times$ end-to-end speedup on Qwen3-4B (21.97 $\rightarrow$
37.30\,tok/s) with exact greedy outputs. Finally, we argue that
speculative drafts should be trained against the \emph{deployed quantized}
target rather than full-precision weights, and demonstrate a complete
pipeline on one workstation: hidden-state harvesting at 111.5\,tok/s
teacher-forced prefill through the serving engine, cache conversion, and
draft training with decreasing loss on real captures --- with a
native-Rust training loop (hanzo-ml autograd) closing the dump
$\rightarrow$ cache $\rightarrow$ train $\rightarrow$ deploy cycle without
leaving the stack.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

DeepSeek-V4-Flash departs substantially from its V3 lineage: attention
operates on a single 512-dimensional shared-KV latent with trailing
partial RoPE and learned attention sinks; 41 of 43 layers augment a
128-token raw sliding window with \emph{compressed KV rows} pooled at
per-layer ratios (4$\times$ or 128$\times$); the inter-layer residual is a
four-stream \emph{hyper-connection} carrier mixed by a 20-iteration
Sinkhorn normalization; MoE routing uses a $\sqrt{\mathrm{softplus}}$
gate, with the first three layers hash-routed by token id; and the model
is W8A8 quantization-aware trained, so its numerics are calibrated to
8-bit activations. A separate 3.6\,GB GGUF carries a one-block MTP draft
head.

Running this on commodity hardware today means the 80.8\,GiB IQ2
quantization on a GB10's unified memory. The reference implementation is
Salvatore Sanfilippo's \texttt{ds4}, a hand-tuned C/CUDA engine specific
to this model; it is the correctness and speed bar throughout this paper.
Our goal was strict: match its \emph{behavior} (coherent long generation
with natural termination) and its \emph{speed}, inside a general-purpose
engine, then go beyond it with speculative decoding that the reference
does not have.

Three empirical lessons recur. First, \emph{measure, don't guess}: three
plausible hypotheses (a CPU-fallback expert kernel, carrier precision as
the degeneration cause, adaptive draft depth as a win) were each
overturned by direct measurement before any fix was attempted. Second,
\emph{bisect against a reference}, never debug open-loop: every root
cause below was found by differencing against \texttt{ds4} on
token-identical inputs. Third, \emph{silent defaults are landmines}: the
single most consequential bug was an \texttt{unwrap\_or\_default()} on a
metadata read.

\section{Reference-Parity Methodology}

\subsection{Token-identical trajectories}

All comparisons run both engines on byte-identical rendered prompts. The
reference's chat template (BOS + system + user + assistant marker +
\texttt{<think>}) was verified equal to our Jinja rendering offline, and
both tokenizations produce the same 24 ids. The reference exposes
\texttt{--dump-logprobs}; we added a matching per-step top-$k$ dump.
Greedy trajectories then either agree token-for-token or pinpoint the
first divergent step --- ours diverged at step~0, immediately localizing
the problem to the prefill forward rather than accumulated decode drift.

\subsection{Per-layer bisection}

We instrumented both engines to emit, for the last prompt position, the
residual-carrier L2 norm and a four-component fingerprint after every
layer: the reference's CPU path (plain C loops) via an environment-gated
\texttt{fprintf}, and our forward via an equivalent trace. Aligning the 45
checkpoints (embedding, 43 layers, final norm) gives a per-layer
norm-ratio and direction-cosine profile. The observed signature ---
layers 0--1 essentially exact (cosine $\geq 0.9997$), first drift at
layer~2, compounding drift thereafter, collapse (cosine $0.12$) in the
final layers as expert routing bifurcates --- identified the first
compressed-attention layer as the defect site and exonerated everything
the first two layers exercise: embeddings, latent attention, per-head
norms, full-window RoPE, hash-routed MoE, hyper-connections, and the
Sinkhorn kernel.

Two null results sharpened the search. Forcing every layer onto the
full-context RoPE tables produced \emph{bit-identical} output --- proving
the compressed-rope configuration was never live. And disabling the
compressed-KV concatenation changed nothing at short context --- proving
no compressor existed to disable.

\subsection{Root cause 1: an integer-width parse}

The GGUF stores the per-layer compression schedule
(\texttt{attention.compress\_ratios}) as an \texttt{i32} array. Our
metadata reader requested \texttt{Vec<u32>}; the underlying
\texttt{Value::to\_u32} matches only the \texttt{U32} variant, so the read
erred --- and an \texttt{unwrap\_or\_default()} silently yielded an empty
schedule. Every layer therefore ran in full-attention mode: wrong RoPE
base (10{,}000 instead of 160{,}000 + YaRN on 41 layers), no compressor
construction, no sliding window. This single line explained the entire
observed pathology: greedy repetition collapse at $\sim$50 tokens, a
flattened next-token distribution at step~0 (top-1 margin 1.5 nats versus
the reference's 6.0), and its insensitivity to compute precision.
Accepting any GGUF integer width fixed step~0 outright: top-5 tokens in
reference order, top-1 at 99.9\% versus the reference's 99.7\%.

\subsection{Root cause 2: decode-side amnesia}

Correct configuration exposed a second, structural gap: the compressor
ran only during prefill. At decode, attention was clamped to the raw
128-token window, so 40 of 43 layers had \emph{no memory} of anything
older --- the model lost the question, wandered, and never emitted EOS
(the reference concludes at 784 tokens; ours looped to any cap). We
implemented decode-side compressed-KV emission: a chunked input history
with $O(1)$ appends, incremental row emission at each layer's pooling
cadence (recomputing only the affected window, honoring the ratio-4
overlap semantics), and state that self-heals under speculative rollback
by truncating to the absolute position each forward reports --- no
plumbing into the cache-rollback machinery. Beyond the window,
decode attends the raw slice concatenated with the emitted rows under an
exact per-row causal/window mask; at single-token decode the mask is
provably all-visible after pre-slicing, so the fused kernel below serves
the combined buffer directly. Result: 1{,}127 tokens of coherent
reasoning, a formatted answer, and a natural stop --- reference-class
behavior for the first time on this stack.

\section{A Fused Attention Kernel for 512-Wide Latent Heads}

Flash-attention kernels in the ecosystem template head dimensions up to
256; V4's 512-wide latent forced an eager three-pass fallback (QK$^T$,
softmax-with-sinks, $\cdot$V) whose cost grows with context. We ported
the reference's decode kernel --- all-F32 online softmax, one warp per
head, eight heads per block, Q held in registers as four
\texttt{float4}s, KV tiled four rows at a time through shared memory, the
sink folded into the softmax denominator only, and the sliding-window
clamp applied per query --- and generalized it to grouped-query layouts
and to \emph{speculative verification widths} $q \in [1,8]$ (one grid
plane per query row; per-row causal end $kv{-}q{+}j{+}1$ and per-row
window). The kernel matches a scalar CPU reference to $\sim$$10^{-7}$
across window/sink/GQA cases, and S$=1$ is byte-identical to the
single-row original.

Two effects follow. Base decode rose 33\% (8.97 $\rightarrow$
11.92\,tok/s), putting the general engine \emph{ahead of the reference's
base} (11.4). And verification forwards ($q{=}2..3$) stopped paying the
eager-path tax, flipping self-speculative MTP decoding from net-negative
(9.27) to net-positive (12.56 at short context) --- the same F32
arithmetic the parity argument wants is also the fast path.

\section{Speculative Decoding}

\subsection{Self-speculation with the MTP head}

The bundled MTP head (one V4 block with NextN entry/exit, sharing the
base embeddings and output head) drives an EAGLE-style chained draft
through a generic proposer/verifier framework with both paged and
non-paged KV backends. Depth two is the measured optimum; deeper chains
lose because each additional draft is a full block forward whose marginal
acceptance does not cover its cost. A confidence-gated adaptive depth ---
DSpark's scheduler idea without the trained predictor --- measured
\emph{neutral-to-negative} on this sequential head: the gate's own
softmax and host readback cancel its savings. The framework retains
variable-length staging (a proposer may return fewer tokens than its
maximum; verification keys off the actual staged length), which parallel
drafts require.

\subsection{Native DSpark: parallel-block drafting}

DeepSeek's DSpark drafts a block of seven tokens in a \emph{single}
forward: multi-layer target hidden states are fused into a context
memory; a mask-seeded block cross-attends that memory bidirectionally
through five Qwen3-style layers; a rank-256 Markov head sequentially
biases the per-slot logits; a confidence head predicts per-slot
acceptance. We ported the draft natively and validated it bit-exactly
against DeepSpec's Python reference on identical inputs (draft tokens
exact; corrected block logits within $6\times10^{-5}$).

measured over 256-token greedy generations:

Two integration defects --- an attach call in a build path the public
builder never executes, and a missing override that let a step-start

record them as a checklist: any new speculative attachment must verify
(a) the attach executes in the build function actually called, and (b)
the pipeline overrides the staged-clear gate.

\section{Training Drafts Against the Deployed Target}

\subsection{The distribution argument}

DeepSpec's stock pipeline loads the full-precision target (284\,GB for
V4-Flash) to harvest hidden states --- infeasible on a 128\,GB
workstation, and, we argue, subtly \emph{wrong} for deployment: a draft's
acceptance rate is its agreement with the target actually served. A
W8A8-QAT model self-corrects on its trained quantization manifold; the
quantized engine's hiddens \emph{are} the deployment distribution.
Harvesting them requires no weights beyond the 80.8\,GiB already being
served.

\subsection{The pipeline, measured}

The serving engine gains a capture mode: during teacher-forced prefill it
records the hyper-connection carrier (stream-summed) at five evenly
spaced layers plus the post-norm hidden, writing one tensor file per
sample. Measured harvest rate: \textbf{111.5\,tok/s} (300 samples,
125k tokens, 18.7 minutes) --- an order of magnitude above decode, making
a 30M-token corpus a days-scale job on one box. A converter reshapes
dumps into DeepSpec's binary cache using DeepSpec's own writers
(round-trip verified bit-exact); the draft's embedding and output head
are warm-started from the target's own (frozen, dequantized from the
GGUF); the target directory DeepSpec's trainer expects is synthesized
from the GGUF's tokenizer (encoding verified identical to the
reference's) and a draft-architecture config with V4 dimensions.

On real captures, fifty optimizer steps of the draft decreased loss
smoothly (3.03 $\rightarrow$ 2.62); the full five-layer paper
configuration trains in 27.2\,GB --- comfortable residency alongside
nothing else, schedulable opposite harvesting. Because the draft is
Qwen3-shaped with V4 dimensions, the trained checkpoint loads through the
\emph{already-shipped} native DSpark path with no engine changes.

\subsection{Closing the loop in Rust}

hanzo-ml retains its lineage's reverse-mode autograd
(\texttt{Var}, \texttt{backward()} $\rightarrow$ gradient store) and
optimizers (AdamW). We built \texttt{hanzo-train} on it: a cache reader
for the binary shard format, a trainable DSpark draft with frozen
target-copied embeddings and head, teacher-forced Markov correction, and
cross-entropy over block positions. On the real captures the loss
descends from 12.68 (within noise of $\ln|V| = 11.77$) to 0.63 in 25
optimizer steps --- monotonic, a 95\% reduction --- and the saved
checkpoints pass a key-and-shape load check against the engine's DSpark
loader. Two implementation notes generalize: CPU F16 matmul makes a
frozen 129k-vocabulary head tractable, and a surrogate-loss backward
that reproduces the exact cross-entropy gradient (verified against full
autograd to $10^{-5}$) avoids materializing the 2.1\,GB gradient the
autograd engine would otherwise form for a \emph{frozen} matmul operand
on every step --- an upstream optimization opportunity for any
freeze-training workload. With this, every stage --- harvest, cache,
train, deploy --- runs in one Rust stack. We are not aware of another
Rust inference engine with a closed speculative-draft training loop.

\section{Related Work}

\texttt{antirez/ds4} is the V4-specific reference engine and the bar for
this work. \texttt{llama.cpp} supports the \texttt{deepseek},
\texttt{deepseek2}, and \texttt{deepseek32} families but not
\texttt{deepseek4}. DeepSpec (DeepSeek, MIT) provides the DSpark, DFlash,
and Eagle3 draft-training recipes and released Qwen3/Gemma checkpoints;
SpecForge and the EAGLE line established feature-level drafting. Our
contributions are orthogonal: general-engine V4 execution, the bisection
methodology and the two root causes, verification-width kernel fusion,
the bit-exact native DSpark integration, and quantized-target draft
training on serving hardware.

\section{Conclusion}

A general-purpose Rust engine now runs DeepSeek-V4-Flash with
reference-class behavior, leads the hand-tuned reference base-for-base,
and hosts the first live parallel-block speculative decoder in the
ecosystem at a measured $1.70\times$. The remaining gaps are named and
scoped: reference-side speculation retains $\sim$6\% at short context;
deep-context assembly wants a persistent device-side buffer; prefill
wants the reference's expert-tiled MoE kernels. The V4 draft now training
against the quantized target is positioned to convert the Qwen3-measured
$1.70\times$ mechanism into a decisive V4 lead. The enduring artifacts
are methodological: token-identical trajectory diffs, layer bisection
against an instrumented reference, and a hard rule --- no silent defaults
on model metadata.
