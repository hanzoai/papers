\section{Four policies, one index}
\label{sec:policies}

If one search finds part of the evidence, the standard move is to search again
from what the first search found. We implemented four policies over the cached
index. Every policy sees identical vectors and identical scoring; the retrieval
policy is the only variable.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.5cm,style=nextline]
\item[single] One search on the question. The baseline.
\item[prf] Pseudo-relevance feedback~\cite{rocchio}: blend the question vector
  toward the centroid of its own top 3 hits ($w{=}0.4$) and search again,
  keeping the first hop's best and filling from the second.
\item[multi] Search again from each of the top 3 hits; union the results,
  scoring each neighbour by the product of the seed's confidence and its own.
\item[chain] \textbf{multi}, then one further hop from what that found --- the
  retrieve--reason--retrieve shape~\cite{ircot} with similarity standing in for
  the reasoning step.
\end{description}

\paragraph{The result is negative, and it is negative in three separate ways.}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The best case is noise.} \textbf{prf} gains \rBestDelta{}
  percentage points on multi-hop. On \rQmulti{} questions that is two questions.
  It is not a result, and reporting it as one would be indefensible.
\item \textbf{Two policies made it worse.} \textbf{multi} and \textbf{chain} ---
  the two that most directly implement ``follow the first hit'' --- both lost
  ground on multi-hop, at $5\times$ and $7\times$ the latency. Searching from a
  neighbour pulls in that neighbour's neighbourhood, which is denser in
  near-duplicates of what was already found than in the missing evidence.
\item \textbf{The one gain is paid for elsewhere.} \textbf{prf} costs
  \rWorstDelta{} points on single-hop, where the baseline was working. Blending
  the query toward its own results is a bet that the first hits are on topic;
  when a single turn is the whole answer, that bet dilutes a query that was
  already correct.
\end{enumerate}

There is no row in this table anyone should ship. The honest summary is that
policy is the wrong knob, and \S\ref{sec:diagnostic} shows it was never going to
be the right one.
