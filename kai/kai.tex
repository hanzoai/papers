\begin{abstract}
\noindent
An agent makes many small decisions for every task it performs: whether a
command may run, which model a subtask needs, which context the next turn
should see, whether the work is finished. Most agents put each of these to a
generative model and parse its prose. We describe Hanzo Decision, which moves
them to a discriminative plane. A decision is a typed question (\op{choice},
\op{score} or \op{noul}) over explicit state, and its answer is a probability
distribution over criteria the caller enumerated, computed in one encoder
forward pass with no generated text. Kai is the model family that answers
them: three checkpoints on ModernBERT and mmBERT encoders, published as
\texttt{hanzoai/kai-1}, \texttt{kai-1-multilingual} and \texttt{kai-1-agent}
and derived from the open Laya checkpoints. Decisions are grouped into
versioned Decision Programs whose thresholds are bound to a calibration and
whose output runs in shadow, advisory or enforced mode. A deterministic policy
remains the authority. Verdicts form the lattice
$\allow < \ask < \deny$ and the effective verdict is the join of the policy's
and the model's, so no model output, including one induced by adversarial
state, can loosen a policy verdict. The risk of enforcing a model is then
friction alone, and friction can be counted from shadow records before a
program is promoted. The public interface, \op{POST /v1/decisions}, is
wire-compatible with OpenRouter's Decisions API and routes by model name to
native Kai or to TypeSafe's Jev. We carry the same objects to studies: a
DecisionPackage records a trade study as typed answers over evidence, reports
which weights and which answers would flip its recommendation, and refreshes
only the answers whose evidence changed. In a recorded run of the three
bootstrap checkpoints on a command that sends a private SSH key to a remote
host, two ranked \allow{} first and none placed more than 0.25 on \deny. That
result is why the model is advisory and the policy is not.
\end{abstract}
