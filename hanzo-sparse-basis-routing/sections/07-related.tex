\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Inverted files and dynamic pruning.}
The inverted file and its evaluation strategies are the substrate of the sparse
half of this design~\cite{zobel2006inverted}. Scoring follows a
term-weighting model of the BM25 family~\cite{robertson2009bm25} or a learned
one, and top-$k$ evaluation avoids scoring every posting by keeping an upper
bound per term (MaxScore~\cite{turtle1995maxscore},
WAND~\cite{broder2003wand}) or per block of a posting list (block-max
WAND~\cite{ding2011bmw}, with variable blocks~\cite{mallia2017vbmw}). Their
guarantee is that the top-$k$ is exact, not that the work is sublinear; the
work depends on how well the bounds separate, which is a property of the data.
Impact-ordered indexes and score-at-a-time evaluation trade exactness for a
budget that can be set in advance~\cite{anh2002impact,lin2015anytime}, and
PISA~\cite{mallia2019pisa} and Lucene~\cite{lucene} are the reference
implementations. The threshold algorithm of Fagin et al.\ is the general form
of all of it, with an instance-optimality result that is the nearest thing in
this literature to the claim of \S\ref{sec:setting}~\cite{fagin2003ta}.

\paragraph{Learned sparse retrieval.}
DeepCT~\cite{dai2020deepct} and DeepImpact~\cite{mallia2021deepimpact} learn
term weights for an inverted index; COIL~\cite{gao2021coil} and
uniCOIL~\cite{lin2021unicoil} reduce contextualised matching to one weight per
term; SPLADE learns a sparse expansion over the vocabulary with non-negative
weights~\cite{formal2021splade,formal2021spladev2,formal2022spladepp}. The
efficiency consequence is documented and is the reason our protocol reports
pruning ratios: learned weights flatten impact distributions and weaken
skipping~\cite{mackenzie2021wacky,lassance2022efficiency}, which guided
traversal~\cite{mallia2022guided}, block-max pruning~\cite{mallia2024bmp} and
block summaries richer than a scalar maximum~\cite{bruch2024seismic} answer in
different ways. Our design is on the same side of that line as Seismic: a
region summary is a vector of maxima, not a single number.

\paragraph{Vocabularies that are not words.}
Explicit semantic analysis indexes Wikipedia concepts~\cite{gabrilovich2007esa};
entity-linked ranking indexes entities beside
words~\cite{dalton2014entity,xiong2017duet}; code search indexes trigrams and
symbols~\cite{cox2012trigram}; dictionary learning and sparse autoencoders
produce interpretable sparse codes over model
activations~\cite{olshausen1996sparse,cunningham2023sae}; network motifs are
the structural vocabulary of graphs~\cite{milo2002motifs}. Each is prior art
for one basis type. We are not aware of a published system that scores one
weighted inner product over all of them inside a column store's part layout,
and that arrangement is what we propose.

\paragraph{Partition-based approximate nearest neighbours.}
Clustering the collection and probing a few clusters is the oldest routing idea
in this space: the inverted file over visual words~\cite{sivic2003videogoogle},
IVF with product quantisation~\cite{jegou2011pq}, the inverted
multi-index~\cite{babenko2012imi}, Faiss as the reference
implementation~\cite{johnson2021faiss}, ScaNN's anisotropic
quantisation~\cite{guo2020scann} and SOAR's spilled
assignments~\cite{sun2023soar}. SPANN keeps centroids in memory and posting
lists on disk, and prunes the lists to search by their centroid distance
relative to the nearest one~\cite{chen2021spann}, which is the approximate
variant of our planner rule. The difference in our setting is not the rule but
what it ranges over: the partitions are parts of a table that also holds
scalars, that merge on a schedule the store controls, and that a predicate may
have already narrowed.

\paragraph{Graph indexes.}
HNSW~\cite{malkov2020hnsw} and Vamana/DiskANN~\cite{subramanya2019diskann},
with streaming updates~\cite{singh2021freshdiskann} and filter-aware
variants~\cite{gollapudi2023filtered,patel2024acorn}, are what a column store
builds per part today. Their empirical scaling is excellent and their
worst-case behaviour is not
guaranteed~\cite{prokhorenkova2020graph,indyk2023worst}; a design that claims
expected cost, as this one does, cannot borrow a guarantee from them.

\paragraph{Metric and tree indexes.}
Centroid-and-radius pruning is old and well understood: branch-and-bound
nearest neighbours over a hierarchical $k$-means
tree~\cite{fukunaga1975branch}, metric trees~\cite{uhlmann1991metric}, the
M-tree's covering radius~\cite{ciaccia1997mtree}, vocabulary
trees~\cite{nister2006vocabtree}, FLANN's randomised and hierarchical
structures~\cite{muja2014flann}, and, in the spatial setting, the
R-tree~\cite{guttman1984rtree}. The reason these are not the whole answer is
equally well documented: above modest dimension the bounds stop separating and
a scan wins~\cite{weber1998quantitative,beyer1999nn}, which is why locality
sensitive hashing offers a sublinear query-time guarantee for the approximate
problem instead~\cite{indyk1998lsh}.

\paragraph{Routing across collections.}
Selective search partitions a corpus into topical shards and consults a few per
query~\cite{kulkarni2015selective}, choosing them with collection
statistics~\cite{callan1995cori}, estimates of relevant-document
counts~\cite{si2003redde}, or the tail of a shard's score
distribution~\cite{aly2013taily}; the cluster hypothesis is the assumption that
makes it work~\cite{vanrijsbergen1979ir}. A part summary is a shard statistic
under another name, computed by the engine rather than by an offline
partitioner, and maintained by merges rather than by a repartitioning job.

\paragraph{Column stores and skipping.}
Modern column stores are surveyed in~\cite{abadi2013columnstores}; ClickHouse's
architecture, including parts, granules and the sparse primary index, is
described by its authors~\cite{schulze2024clickhouse,clickhousedocs}. Skipping
by per-block aggregates is small materialized
aggregates~\cite{moerkotte1998sma}, and choosing a layout that makes skipping
effective is fine-grained partitioning~\cite{sun2014skipping}. The
log-structured merge discipline that produces the parts is
standard~\cite{oneil1996lsm}. Vector indexes inside such engines are per
segment or per part~\cite{lucene,wang2021milvus,usearch}, which is the
condition this paper starts from.

\paragraph{Summary-first retrieval for generation.}
GraphRAG routes a global question through community
summaries~\cite{edge2024graphrag} and RAPTOR through a tree of recursive
summaries~\cite{sarthi2024raptor}. Both read summaries before rows, as we do,
and neither excludes a summary by an arithmetic bound, which is the whole of
the difference.

\paragraph{Evaluation practice.}
Recall-against-cost curves rather than single operating
points~\cite{aumuller2020annb}, published ground truth and fixed budgets at
billion scale~\cite{simhadri2022bigann}, percentile latency rather than
means~\cite{dean2013tail}, bootstrap intervals~\cite{efron1993bootstrap} and
caution about power-law fits~\cite{clauset2009powerlaw} are the practices
\S\ref{sec:method} assembles.
