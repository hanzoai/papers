\section{Kai}
\label{sec:model}

Kai is one model: a multilingual text encoder, adapters that bring other
evidence into its width, and a typed-variable decision decoder
(Figure~\ref{fig:kai}). It maps evidence $E$, a typed question and, inside a
program, other questions' answers to a distribution over the question's
options.

\subsection{Evidence and adapters}
\label{sec:evidence}

\begin{definition}[Evidence]
An evidence record is a tuple
\[
  e = (\mathit{id}, \mathit{modality}, t, \mathit{source}, \mathit{frame},
  \mathit{unit}, \mathit{payload}, \mathit{uncertainty}, \mathit{provenance})
\]
of a modality (text, JSON, time series, audio, image, video, or
$\op{Custom}(\cdot)$), a time or interval, the payload's frame and unit, the
source's error model, and the producer with its inputs. A decision's evidence
is a set of records.
\end{definition}

Sensor metadata (unit, rate, calibration, error bound, quality) is input, and
temporal summaries (instant, window, trend, event, baseline, forecast) are
records too, produced by code or a forecasting
model~\cite{das2024timesfm,ansari2024chronos}; a new sensor needs an adapter,
not a new model. Each non-text modality has one adapter into the encoder's
width $d$: one rate-aware adapter for every time series, embedding each sample
with its timestamp, rate and unit; frozen Whisper~\cite{radford2023whisper} and
SigLIP~\cite{zhai2023siglip} front ends with trained projections for audio,
images and video. Latents are keyed by content hash and computed once per
changed evidence state \proposed.

Today evidence is JSON read as text: each question's instruction and the
serialised state form one context,
\texttt{[CLS]}~$\langle\tau\rangle$~\texttt{question:}~$\iota$~\texttt{[SEP]}~$s$~\texttt{[SEP]},
cut at the context length by dropping the state's tail and encoded by
ModernBERT~\cite{modernbert} or mmBERT~\cite{mmbert} \shipped.

\subsection{Decoder: factorized choice}
\label{sec:factor}

Let $H \in \mathbb{R}^{L\times d}$ be the context's token states plus a learned
type embedding $e_\tau$, and $O_i \in \mathbb{R}^{L_i\times d}$ those of option
$i$, encoded alone as \texttt{[CLS]}~$c_i$~\texttt{[SEP]}. An option's encoding
depends on its text only, so each distinct option in a batch is encoded once
for every question that lists it \shipped, and across calls once per program
version \proposed. Two pre-norm cross-attention blocks, in which option tokens
attend to context tokens and nothing else, give
$X_i = B_2(B_1(O_i + e_\tau, H), H)$; with $v_i$ the mean of $X_i$,
\begin{equation}
  s_i = w^{\top}\,\mathrm{GELU}\bigl(W\,\mathrm{LN}(v_i) + b\bigr).
\end{equation}
The type fixes the law, at a temperature $T$ fitted per type and option-count
bucket on validation data \shipped:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item \op{choice}: $p_i = \operatorname{softmax}_i(s_i/T)$.
  \item \op{noul}: $p_{\op{true}} = \sigma\bigl((s_{\op{true}} -
    s_{\op{false}})/T\bigr)$, each side scored with its own description.
  \item \op{score}: a cumulative-link model~\cite{mccullagh1980} over levels
    $1..k$, $P(y \le j) = \sigma\bigl((\theta_j - \eta)/T\bigr)$. The location
    $\eta = a^{\top}\bar v$ reads the state-aware level vectors; the cutpoints
    read the level texts alone,
    $\theta_j = b^{\top}\bar o + \sum_{i \le j}
    \operatorname{softplus}(g^{\top}[\bar o_i; \bar o_{i+1}])$ with $\bar o_i$
    the mean of $O_i$, so they are ordered.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Symmetry]
\label{prop:symmetry}
For any weights, permuting a question's options permutes $p$ the same way, and
exchanging the texts of a \op{noul}'s sides maps $p_{\op{true}}$ to
$1 - p_{\op{true}}$ \shipped.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$s_i$ depends on $c_i$ and $H$ alone, and softmax is permutation equivariant;
the exchange swaps $s_{\op{true}}$ and $s_{\op{false}}$, and
$\sigma(-x) = 1 - \sigma(x)$.
\end{proof}

Laya has neither: its options share one sequence, in order.

\paragraph{Retrieval.}
A choice with more than $k = 32$ options is narrowed first. The context's first
token and each option's mean token are projected to unit vectors $u$, $r_i$;
$\pi = \operatorname{softmax}_i(e^{\gamma}\langle u, r_i\rangle)$ with $\gamma$
learned. The head scores the top $k$ under $\pi$, a set $S$, and the rest keep
their retrieval probability:
\begin{equation}
  p_i = \begin{cases} (1-\rho)\,\operatorname{softmax}_{j \in S}(s_j/T)_i & i \in S,\\
  \pi_i & i \notin S,\end{cases}
  \qquad \rho = \sum_{i \notin S} \pi_i ,
\end{equation}
so the mass beyond the top $k$ is kept, not renormalised away, and the head
costs $O(k)$ per question for any option count \shipped.

\paragraph{Training.}
Log loss plus Brier score against each question's target distribution, both
strictly proper~\cite{gneiting2007}, plus 0.2 times the retrieval log loss;
bf16 over f32 masters on Metal, AdamW, token-budgeted batches. Data are the
train splits of the harness's sources at pinned revisions,
minus any example sharing a content unit (the state's text, or the first 200
letters and digits of a field of 200 or more characters) with a harness
evaluation state \shipped.

\subsection{Decoder: typed-variable diffusion}
\label{sec:diffusion}

A program's questions depend on each other, and answered independently they
can break its rules: approving an invoice flagged as a duplicate.

\begin{definition}[Typed variables]
A program's questions are variables $Z = (Z_v)_{v \in V}$, each with a finite
domain $D_v$ (labels, levels, or a \op{noul}'s sides), with a dependency DAG
$G$ on $V$ and rules $R$, each on two variables:
$Z_a = i \Rightarrow Z_b = j$, or $\lnot(Z_a = i \land Z_b = j)$.
\end{definition}

Kai predicts masked slots jointly: for a masked set $M \subseteq V$,
\begin{equation}
  p_\theta(Z_M \mid E, Z_{V\setminus M}, G) = \prod_{v \in M}
  p_\theta(Z_v \mid E, Z_{V\setminus M}, G),
\end{equation}
each slot a query over the evidence latents and the visible slots, scored
against its own domain by the head above. Training draws $t \sim U(0,1)$ and
masks each variable with probability $t$: absorbing-state discrete
diffusion~\cite{austin2021d3pm} over typed variables, not tokens. $M = V$ is a
set of independent questions; $|M| = 1$ asks one with all others known
\proposed.

\paragraph{Inference.}
Passes are spent on uncertainty.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Pass 1 predicts every variable and freezes those whose top probability
    reaches $\tau$.
  \item Pass $d+1$ re-asks, once, each unfrozen variable whose parents are all
    settled (frozen, roots, or re-asked), conditioned on their answers.
  \item Each unsettled variable keeps its top $m$ candidates; a small selector
    scores assignments of the candidate lattice for coherence.
  \item A deterministic projection returns
    $\hat z = \arg\max_{z \models R} \sum_{v} \log p_v(z_v)$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Depth bounds cost]
\label{prop:depth}
Steps 1 and 2 end within $\operatorname{depth}(G) + 1$ passes, depth counted in
edges, and ask at most $2|V|$ questions.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Roots and frozen variables are settled after pass 1. If every variable of depth
below $d$ is settled after pass $d$, each of depth $d$ has all parents settled
and is re-asked by pass $d+1$. Each variable is asked in pass 1 and at most once
more.
\end{proof}

Steps 1, 2 and 4 are in the code with Kai's factorized head as the conditional:
a re-asked variable sees its parents' answers and probabilities as typed facts
added to its state, each pass is one batched call, and the projection is an
exact branch and bound bounded by the sum of per-variable maxima \shipped. The
trained slot model, the lattice and selector, and distilling the passes into
one or two are \proposed.

\subsection{One checkpoint line, provenance, runtime}
\label{sec:checkpoints}

Kai has one checkpoint line, \texttt{hanzoai/kai}, with a revision per training
stage: A, typed text decisions (the harness's train splits, MASSIVE in 51
locales, agentic, coding and skill decisions, CLINC150, XNLI, SST-5, extreme
choice, robustness); B, the evidence adapters with the encoder frozen (sensors,
time series, audio, image, video, graph and SysML); C, joint masked decoding
over whole programs. Stage A's training code is on the main branch \shipped;
the stages and their revisions are \proposed{} until each has a harness run.
The encoder is mmBERT-base~\cite{mmbert}, multilingual, at an 8,192-token
context \proposed; the model code runs any ModernBERT-family
encoder~\cite{modernbert} \shipped. The revision evaluated here is
\res{kj/kai/model} (weights \res{kj/kai/weights}).

\paragraph{Provenance.}
Kai initializes from Laya's weights~\cite{laya}, released under Apache-2.0: the
encoder and type embedding as they are, Laya's two self-attention head layers
split into the two cross-attention blocks, and its marker scorer as the option
scorer; retrieval and the ordinal head start fresh \shipped.

\paragraph{Laya, the baseline.}
Laya 0.3.20's three checkpoints are not Kai revisions: English on
ModernBERT-large at a 512-token context, multilingual on mmBERT-base at 1,024,
and Laya-TD, ModernBERT-large fine-tuned on typed
decisions~\cite{typeddecisions} at 1,024. Their joint head packs the question,
each option after a marker token, and the state into one sequence within a
192--256-token option budget, and a script detector routes non-English states
to the multilingual checkpoint. We run their byte-identical weights in Laya's
Python reference runtime and natively in Rust through a port of the joint head
\proposed, whose parity with the reference is \res{par/logit} per logit
(Section~\ref{sec:repro}).

\paragraph{Runtime.}
Rust on Hanzo's tensor library, \texttt{hanzo-ml}. The \texttt{kai} crate holds
the model, question parsing, layout, token-budget batching, decoding and
refinement, on CPU in f32 and Metal in bf16 \shipped, and on CUDA and ROCm where
the library builds them \proposed. The \texttt{decision} crate serves
\op{POST /v1/decisions} over a registry: Kai and Laya in process, Jev through
OpenRouter~\cite{openrouterdecisions} \proposed.
