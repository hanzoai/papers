% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================================
\begin{abstract}
A decentralized AI network must pay operators for work they actually performed.
A digital signature proves \emph{authorship} of a message; a plurality vote
proves \emph{agreement} among reporters; neither proves \emph{computation} ---
that the claimed model was run on the claimed input to produce the claimed
output. The cheapest robbery of such a network is therefore a one-bit modal
guess that joins the quorum and is minted alongside honest work. We present
Proof of AI (PoAI): the proof is not a separate artifact bolted onto inference,
it is emitted \emph{as a side-effect of execution by the Hanzo engine itself}.
The engine is the native inference, training, and embedding execution layer;
because $\sim$95\% of a forward pass is matrix multiplication $C = A\cdot B$,
each layer can be verified in $O(n^2)$ instead of recomputed in $O(n^3)$ using
Freivalds' algorithm over a prime field $\Fp$, $p = 2^{61}-1$, applied to the
\emph{exact} \code{int8} integer accumulator. Integer arithmetic is bit-exact
across CPU, GPU, and backends, so the check has \emph{zero} false-reject ---
the load-bearing determinism on which the whole scheme rests. We describe the
four proofs that compose over this one primitive (Proof of Embedding, Inference,
Training, and Compute), the streaming activation-trace Merkle Mountain Range
that lets an operator commit now and be challenged later, the
\code{reportData} binding that ties a proof to a specific model, prompt, and
output, and the by-tier treatment of floating point that avoids the unsoundness
of naive tolerance-based checking. We state honestly what is proven and tested
today and what remains the next slice: the verifier core and determinism
prerequisites ship and are tested; the chain enforces no-proof-no-mint;
end-to-end proof \emph{emission} from a running model is designed and merged but
not yet live.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction: what a proof of AI must prove}
\label{sec:intro}

Consider a network where operators run AI models and are minted tokens for the
work. Three properties are easy to confuse, and only one of them is the thing
that must be secured.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item A \textbf{signature} proves \emph{authorship}: this party committed to
        this message. It says nothing about whether any computation occurred.
  \item A \textbf{plurality} (a quorum, a majority vote over reported outputs)
        proves \emph{agreement}: $m$ reporters submitted the same answer. It is
        only as honest as the reporters, and it rewards consensus, not work.
  \item A \textbf{proof of computation} proves the load-bearing fact: this
        operator ran \emph{this} model on \emph{this} input and obtained
        \emph{this} output.
\end{itemize}

The gap between the first two and the third is an economic vulnerability, not a
theoretical nicety. Suppose minting requires only a signed output that matches
the quorum. The cheapest attack is then a $\sim$1-bit \emph{modal guess}: an
operator that never loads the model, predicts the most likely token (or copies
a neighbor), signs it, joins the plurality, and is paid. No matmul is performed;
the marginal cost of the ``work'' is a coin flip. Any network that settles on
signatures-plus-agreement is robbed by exactly this strategy, and the robbery is
indistinguishable from honest participation at the level of signatures and
votes.

PoAI closes the gap by making the proof a \emph{property of execution} rather
than a property of reporting. The Hanzo engine is the native execution layer
for inference, training, and embedding across the zen model family. When it runs
a model it can, at near-zero overhead, emit a commitment to the computation it
performed; a verifier later challenges a small random part of that commitment
and checks it for asymptotically less than the cost of redoing the work. A
modal-guess operator has no committed activations to open and is caught
immediately. This paper grounds that claim in the shipped engine code
(\code{hanzo-engine/src/poi.rs}) and its canonical chain-side mirror
(\code{luxfi/crypto/poi}), folds in the product-of-experts settlement material
from the earlier PoAI fragment, and is explicit about what is live versus
designed.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item The framing above: signature $\neq$ plurality $\neq$ computation, and
        the modal-guess attack that the distinction exposes
        (\S\ref{sec:intro}).
  \item The Freivalds core over the exact \code{int8} accumulator in $\Fp$, with
        zero false-reject, grounded line-for-line in \code{poi.rs}
        (\S\ref{sec:freivalds}).
  \item The four proofs (PoE/PoI/PoT/PoC) that compose over the single matmul

  \item The determinism prerequisites that make exact verification possible at
        all (\S\ref{sec:determinism}), the commit-now/verify-later
        activation-trace MMR and interactive bisection (\S\ref{sec:mmr}), and
        the \code{reportData} binding (\S\ref{sec:binding}).
  \item The by-tier floating-point treatment that avoids tolerance-corridor
        unsoundness (\S\ref{sec:fp}) and the architecture-family coverage table
        for the whole zen zoo (\S\ref{sec:coverage}).
  \item An honest implementation-status section (\S\ref{sec:status}).
\end{itemize}

\section{The Freivalds core: verifying a forward pass}
\label{sec:freivalds}

A transformer forward pass is dominated by matrix multiplication: the
attention projections, the MLP/expert GEMMs, and the output head are all of the
form $C = A\cdot B$, and together they are $\sim$95\% of the floating (here,
integer) operations. This is the lever. To \emph{recompute} a layer
$A\in\mathbb{Z}^{t\times k}$, $B\in\mathbb{Z}^{k\times n}$, giving
$C\in\mathbb{Z}^{t\times n}$, costs $O(t\,k\,n)$ --- the same work the prover
did. To \emph{verify} it, we do not need to redo it.

\begin{theorem}[Freivalds, 1977, over $\Fp$]
\label{thm:freivalds}
Let $p$ be prime, $A,B,C$ integer matrices of conforming shape reduced into
$\Fp$, and let $r \in \Fp^{\,n}$ be drawn uniformly at random. If
$C = A\cdot B$ then $A\,(B\,r) = C\,r$ \emph{always}. If $C \neq A\cdot B$ then
\[
  \Pr_{r}\!\big[A\,(B\,r) = C\,r\big] \;\le\; \frac{1}{p}.
\]
Computing $B\,r$, then $A\,(B\,r)$, and $C\,r$ costs $O(t k + k n + t n) = O(n^2)$
matrix--vector work, one order below the $O(n^3)$ of the multiply.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Let $D = C - A\cdot B \neq 0$ over $\Fp$, so some row $D_i \neq 0$. The event
$D\,r = 0$ forces $\langle D_i, r\rangle = 0$, a single non-trivial linear
constraint on $r$; its solution set is a hyperplane containing a $1/p$ fraction
of $\Fp^{\,n}$. An honest $C$ gives $D = 0$ and passes for every $r$, which is
why there is no false-reject.
\end{proof}

Running $k$ independent challenge vectors multiplies the per-vector soundness
error, giving a tampered $C$ a survival probability of at most $(1/p)^k$.

\paragraph{Why integers, and why $p = 2^{61}-1$.}
The engine's \code{int8} quantized path multiplies signed 8-bit operands into a
32-bit accumulator ($i8\cdot i8 \to i32$, the \code{f8q8} path in
\code{hanzo-quant}). That accumulator is an \emph{exact integer}; it is identical
on every backend that respects the reduction, because integer addition and
multiplication carry no rounding. Verifying over $\Fp$ with the Mersenne prime
$p = 2^{61}-1$ keeps every intermediate inside 61 bits, so a single 128-bit
multiply (\code{fmul}) and a conditional subtract (\code{fadd}) suffice and never
overflow. The result is the property the rest of PoAI depends on:
\textbf{zero false-reject across CPU/GPU/backends}. Soundness error is
$1/p \approx 2^{-61}$ per challenge vector; $k = 2$ vectors give $\le 2^{-122}$.

\paragraph{The shipped API.}
The core lives in \code{hanzo-engine/src/poi.rs}: dependency-free, backend-agnostic,
unit-tested in isolation. The surface is small and is mirrored byte-for-byte by
the Go verifier the chain consumes (\code{luxfi/crypto/poi}, with
\code{NewMat}/\code{Verify}/\code{VerifyMulti}/\code{DeriveChallenges} and the
same $p = (1\ll 61)-1$).

\begin{lstlisting}[language={}]
pub const P: u64 = (1u64 << 61) - 1;       // Mersenne prime field modulus

pub struct Mat { rows, cols, data: Vec<i64> }  // exact i32/i64 accumulators

// Verify C == A*B for ONE challenge r:  A*(B*r) == C*r over F_p, O(tk+kn+tn).
pub fn freivalds_verify(a, b, c: &Mat, r: &[u64]) -> bool;

// Verify against k challenges; honest C passes ALL, tampered C fails w.p. >= 1-(1/p)^k.
pub fn freivalds_verify_multi(a, b, c: &Mat, challenges: &[Vec<u64>]) -> bool;

// Derive k challenge vectors of length n from a seed (prod: keccak(beacon || ...)).
pub fn derive_challenges(seed: u64, n: usize, k: usize) -> Vec<Vec<u64>>;
\end{lstlisting}

The reduction \code{to\_field} uses \code{rem\_euclid} so signed \code{int8}
weights and activations map correctly into $[0,p)$. The module ships eight unit
tests covering an honest $2\times 2$ product, a single tampered entry being
caught, negative-entry reduction, deterministic challenge derivation, a
fail-closed shape mismatch, and a $16{\times}24\cdot 24{\times}16$ product where
flipping one deep entry off-by-one is still caught --- the sparse-cheat case that
motivates challenging every layer.

\paragraph{The challenge must come after the commitment.}
Soundness in Theorem~\ref{thm:freivalds} assumes $r$ is unpredictable to the
prover. If the prover knew $r$ in advance it could craft $A,B,C$ with
$D\,r = 0$ yet $C\neq A\cdot B$. The production derivation therefore draws the
seed from an on-chain beacon \emph{after} the prover has committed
(\S\ref{sec:mmr}); \code{derive\_challenges} is the reproducible expansion both
sides apply to that seed. The unit tests use a \code{splitmix64} stream for the
same reproducibility --- identical soundness, only the unpredictability source
differs.

\section{Four proofs over one primitive}
\label{sec:four}

PoAI is not four mechanisms; it is one matmul-verification primitive applied to
four \code{workloadType}s. The operands $(A, B, C)$ and the I/O commitment differ
by workload, but the verifier, the field, and the soundness argument are shared.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=0pt,style=nextline]
  \item[Proof of Embedding (PoE).] The embedding tower (and any ViT/audio
        encoder feeding it) is a stack of GEMMs producing a vector. Freivalds
        verifies the projection layers; the input commitment fixes what was
        embedded. Settlement uses the embedding as the unit of paid work.
  \item[Proof of Inference (PoI).] A decode step is the attention and MLP/expert
        GEMMs plus the output head. The transcript commits the per-layer
        operands; a challenged layer is opened and Freivalds-checked. This is the
        canonical case and the one the modal-guess attacker cannot fake.
  \item[Proof of Training (PoT).] A training step is a forward pass plus a
        backward pass; the backward pass is again matmuls (the transposed
        GEMMs of the gradient). The same primitive verifies that a claimed
        gradient update corresponds to real backpropagation, not a fabricated
        delta.
  \item[Proof of Compute (PoC).] The general case: any committed GEMM workload
        (a kernel an operator claims to have run for hire) is verifiable by the
        identical $A\,(B\,r) = C\,r$ check. PoE/PoI/PoT are PoC specialized by
        what the operands mean and what I/O is bound.
\end{description}

This is deliberate orthogonal design: one primitive at one slot, four payoffs.
A network that wants to pay for embeddings, inference, training, or raw compute
does not need four verifiers --- it needs one, parameterized by
\code{workloadType}.

\paragraph{Settlement: closed-form expert weights from PoAI (folded from the
prior fragment).} Once work is \emph{verified}, it can be \emph{weighted}. For
each expert (operator) $m$, let $q_m \in [0,1]$ be the Bayesian reliability
estimated from its historical attestations. Under a product-of-experts
combination the optimal mixing weight is closed-form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poai-weights}
  \eta_m \;\propto\; \frac{q_m}{1 - q_m},
\end{equation}
i.e. precision-weighted aggregation, with no manual tuning. At decode time this
is the product-of-experts rule
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poe-main}
  \log \pi_*(y\mid x) \;=\; \eta_0 \log \pi_0(y\mid x)
   \;+\; \sum_{i=1}^{K} \eta_i \log \phi_i(y\mid x) \;-\; \log Z,
\end{equation}
where $\pi_0 = p_\theta$ is the base model, $\{\phi_i\}$ are expert factors, and
$Z$ is the per-token partition function. PoAI supplies the reliabilities $q_m$
that make Eq.~\eqref{eq:poai-weights} trustworthy: a weight derived from
\emph{verified} work, not from self-reported agreement.

\section{Determinism is the load-bearing property}
\label{sec:determinism}

Exact Freivalds needs an exactly reproducible $C$. If two honest runs of the
same model on the same input could produce different outputs, there would be no
canonical $C$ to commit to, and an honest prover could be false-rejected. Exact
verification is therefore \emph{impossible} without bit-level determinism. Two
sources of nondeterminism are silently present in ordinary inference, and both
are closed in the shipped engine (\code{hanzoai/engine} v1.2.5).

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item \textbf{Argmax ties.} Greedy decode takes $\arg\max$ over logits; when
        two logits are equal, the winner is implementation-defined (it depends on
        reduction order on the device). The engine pins a \textbf{lowest-id
        tie-break}: among tied maxima the smallest token index wins, on every
        backend.
  \item \textbf{MoE expert selection.} A Mixture-of-Experts router picks the
        top-$k$ experts by score; ties and unstable sort order change \emph{which
        experts run}, changing $C$ entirely. The engine pins a \textbf{canonical
        top-$k$}: sort by score descending, breaking ties by expert index
        ascending (the \code{deterministic\_topk\_indices} selection).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}
Determinism is not a performance feature here; it is the precondition for
soundness. Only because the int8 accumulator and these two tie-breaks are
reproducible can the verifier assert that an honest operator's $C$ matches
bit-for-bit, and hence that \emph{any} Freivalds failure is real cheating rather
than hardware drift. Proof-bearing runs additionally set
\code{set\_moe\_proof\_deterministic(true)} and pin temperature-0 greedy decode.
\end{remark}

\section{Commit now, verify later}
\label{sec:mmr}

A forward pass over a large model at hundreds of tokens produces terabytes of
activations; retaining them is infeasible, and forcing synchronous verification
would serialize the network. PoAI instead has the operator \emph{commit} to its
activations cheaply during execution and be \emph{challenged} later on a small
random part --- the optimistic bargain: cheap in the happy path, partial
re-execution only under challenge.

\paragraph{ActivationTraceMMR.} A streaming \textbf{Merkle Mountain Range}
accumulates one leaf per $(\text{layer}, \text{token}, \text{tap})$ in execution
order and keeps only $O(\log N)$ peak hashes --- a frontier of roughly 768 bytes.
Activations are hashed as the kernel produces them and then dropped. The hash is
\code{keccak}, with leaf/node domain separation:
\begin{align*}
\text{leaf} &= \keccak(\text{\small DOMAIN\_POI\_ACT} \,\|\, \text{layer}
   \,\|\, \text{token} \,\|\, \text{tap} \,\|\, \text{dtype} \,\|\,
   \text{shape} \,\|\, \text{tensor bytes}),\\
\text{node} &= \keccak(\text{\small DOMAIN\_POI\_NODE} \,\|\, \text{left}
   \,\|\, \text{right}),
\end{align*}
where $\text{tap} \in \{\code{matmul\_in}, \code{matmul\_out},
\code{block\_out}\}$ --- exactly the operands a Freivalds check (or a non-matmul
recompute) will open, no more. Lone nodes on odd levels use \textbf{RFC-6962
promotion}, \emph{not} Bitcoin duplicate-last: the duplicate-last construction is
the CVE-2012-2459 malleability (a forged tree can collide a root), so it is
deliberately avoided. The MMR closes by binding the leaf count,
$\text{activationTraceRoot} = \keccak(\text{\small DOMAIN\_POI} \,\|\,
n_{\text{leaves}} \,\|\, \text{mmr root})$, so a subtree cannot be reinterpreted
as the whole tree.

\paragraph{Zero overhead when off.} The commitment is threaded as an
\code{Option<\&mut ProofTranscript>} through the forward context, not a boolean
checked in hot loops. When the option is \code{None}, each commit site is a
single not-taken branch --- no hashing, no allocation, no tensor copy. When it is
\code{Some}, the per-matmul hook commits the int8 activation view and the i32
accumulator by borrow (no copy); the weight $B$ is committed once at load into
the weights root, never re-hashed per token.

\paragraph{Challenge, open, bisect.} A verifier derives a layer index
$\ell$ and challenge $r$ from a beacon \emph{after} commitment, opens that
layer's $A$ (\code{matmul\_in}), $B$ (a reference under the weights root), and
$C$ (\code{matmul\_out}) with $O(\log N)$ MMR inclusion proofs, and runs
\emph{windowed} Freivalds over a random token sub-range to keep the opened
payload small. The verifier recomputes the leaf hashes, checks the inclusions
against the committed roots, re-derives $r$, and checks $A\,(B\,r) = C\,r$ ---
total cost $O(t k + k n + t n + \log N)$, about $\min(t,k,n)\times$ cheaper than
recomputing the layer. A mismatch slashes the operator. To localize a
\emph{sparse} cheat (a single bad layer among many) the protocol runs interactive
\textbf{bisection} to the first divergent layer in $O(\log L)$ rounds, so
soundness is independent of how many layers are sampled.

\section{Binding: a proof points at exactly one (model, input, output)}
\label{sec:binding}

A correct matmul check is worthless if the attestation can be replayed, spliced
across tasks, or pointed at a different model. PoAI binds the proof with two
hash chains whose constants match the chain side byte-for-byte. The challenge is
derived from immutable task context:
\begin{multline*}
\text{challenge} = \keccak\big(\text{\small DOMAIN\_CHALLENGE} \,\|\,
  \text{taskId} \,\|\, \text{intentID} \,\|\, \text{modelSpecHash} \\
  \|\; \text{promptHash} \,\|\, \text{openBlockHash} \,\|\, \text{operator}\big),
\end{multline*}
and the attested result commits to that challenge:
\begin{multline*}
\text{reportData} = \keccak\big(\text{\small DOMAIN\_REPORT} \,\|\,
  \text{challenge} \,\|\, \text{modelSpecHash} \\
  \|\; \text{promptHash} \,\|\, \text{outputHash}
  \,\|\, \text{runtimeMeasurement}\big).
\end{multline*}
The chain consumes \code{reportData}; because it folds in the challenge (which
already folds in \code{taskId}, \code{intentID}, and \code{openBlockHash}), a
transcript is task-scoped and cannot be reused.

\paragraph{\code{modelSpecHash} is measured, not labelled.} The model identity
is a \keccak{} Merkle root over the \emph{quantized weight bytes the engine
actually loaded} (the per-layer \code{QuantizedSerde} serialization, with the
quant-type discriminant in each leaf). It is not a name string. Re-quantizing the
same weights to a different scheme yields a different root; a genuinely different
model yields a different root. Combined with a \code{runtimeMeasurement} that
folds in the kernel build id, reduction mode, and sampler/seed, the decoding
regime becomes part of identity: the same weights decoded at temperature 2.0, or
under a different reduction order, are rejected. The full transcript root chains
these together,
\begin{multline*}
\text{transcriptRoot} = \keccak\big(\text{\small DOMAIN\_POI} \,\|\,
  \text{modelID} \,\|\, \text{inputCommitment} \,\|\,
  \text{quantizationSpec} \\
  \|\; \text{kernelVersion} \,\|\,
  \text{activationTraceRoot} \,\|\, \text{outputCommitment}\big),
\end{multline*}
so the binding runs challenge $\to$ model $\to$ input $\to$ output and an
attestation cannot be detached from any link in the chain.

\section{Floating point: by tier, not by tolerance}
\label{sec:fp}

The natural temptation is to run Freivalds directly on floating-point GEMMs with
a tolerance: accept if $\lVert A(Br) - Cr\rVert < \varepsilon$. This is both
\textbf{unsound} and \textbf{false-rejecting}, and PoAI rejects it.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \emph{Unsound:} a tolerance corridor of width $\varepsilon$ is an
        attacker's budget. A cheater can perturb $C$ anywhere inside the corridor
        --- a non-trivial space of fabricated outputs --- and pass. The clean
        $1/p$ soundness of Theorem~\ref{thm:freivalds} holds only in an exact
        field.
  \item \emph{False-rejecting:} floating-point reduction order is not fixed
        across hardware, so two honest runs differ in the low bits. Any
        $\varepsilon$ small enough to be sound is small enough to reject honest
        operators on a different GPU.
\end{itemize}

PoAI therefore selects the verification regime by numeric tier:

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Verification regime by numeric tier.}
\label{tab:fp-tiers}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Tier} & \textbf{Mechanism} & \textbf{Soundness basis} \\
\midrule
\code{int8} / quantized & exact Freivalds over $\Fp$ & software-only, zero false-reject \\
fp16 / bf16 / fp8       & deterministic-fp (RepOps): & bit-reproducible by pinned \\
                        & pin reduction order        & reduction; or TEE/CC attestation \\
small / niche           & zkML circuit               & succinct cryptographic proof \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For the quantized path the check is exact and software-only --- no special
hardware, the common case for the deployed zen models. For native low-precision
floating point, the fix is not a tolerance but \textbf{determinism}: pin the
reduction order (RepOps / deterministic-fp) so the GEMM is bit-reproducible, then
the exact equality check applies again; where that is impractical, fall back to a
trusted-execution / confidential-compute attestation of the kernel. For small or
unusual workloads a zkML circuit gives a succinct proof directly.

\begin{remark}
A floating-point value \emph{is} an integer --- a sign bit, an exponent, and a
mantissa. A GEMM executed in a \emph{pinned} reduction order is therefore an
exact, reproducible computation over those integers; ``deterministic-fp'' is not
an approximation but a commitment to a specific, replayable order of exact
operations. The unsoundness of naive fp-Freivalds comes entirely from
\emph{unpinned} order, not from floating point being inherently fuzzy.
\end{remark}

\section{Coverage across the zen architecture families}
\label{sec:coverage}

The matmul primitive plus two extensions covers the entire zen model zoo. The
two extensions are a \emph{routing proof} (for sparse/MoE models) and
\emph{sequential state-chaining} (for recurrent and multi-step models); every
other family is base Freivalds plus a deterministic input commitment.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Architecture-family coverage. ``Beyond base'' marks the two extensions
over plain dense-matmul Freivalds.}
\label{tab:coverage}
\begin{tabular}{p{3.4cm} p{6.2cm} p{2.4cm}}
\toprule
\textbf{Family} & \textbf{How PoAI verifies it} & \textbf{Beyond base} \\
\midrule
Dense transformer &
  Freivalds on every layer's GEMMs; activation-trace MMR. &
  --- \\
\addlinespace
Mixture-of-Experts &
  Commit router logits + selected expert indices per token; verify the
  \emph{canonical} top-$k$ chose the same experts, then Freivalds the selected
  experts' GEMMs only (sparse). &
  routing proof \\
\addlinespace
Linear-recurrent / SSM / Gated DeltaNet &
  Per-step Freivalds, with consecutive states bound in the commitment (step
  $t$'s output is step $t{+}1$'s input). &
  state-chaining \\
\addlinespace
Multimodal (ViT + audio) &
  Vision/audio towers are GEMMs $\to$ Freivalds; commit the preprocessed input
  by hash; pin preprocessing (resize/normalize; FFT$\to$mel is
  commit-and-recompute). &
  input commitment \\
\addlinespace

  Multi-step: per-step Freivalds plus binding consecutive latents in the

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

One primitive, two extensions, full coverage --- the recurrent state-chaining
mechanism serves both linear-recurrent models and diffusion, so there is no
separate diffusion verifier.

\section{Ownership: whose data, whose compute}
\label{sec:ownership}

PoAI is the technical substrate for an ownership model. Users own their data and
own the AI they run --- the proof lets them prove a computation was performed
without surrendering control of the model or the inputs to a trusted middleman.
Compute is \emph{selectable}: a user may have a trusted DAO node perform the work
(convenient, with the proof guaranteeing they were not short-changed) or run a
user-operated node themselves (sovereign, with the same proof format settling on
the same chain). The verification is identical in both cases; what differs is
only who holds the keys to the executing node. The proof, not the operator's
reputation, is what gets minted against.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A network that pays for AI must verify computation, not authorship or agreement.
The decisive observation is that a forward pass is almost entirely matmuls, that
a matmul can be \emph{verified} an order of magnitude below the cost of
\emph{performing} it, and that on the engine's exact integer accumulator this
verification is bit-exact with zero false-reject. PoAI builds directly on the
Hanzo engine: the engine is the native execution layer, and the proof is emitted
as a side-effect of that execution --- one Freivalds primitive over $\Fp$,
specialized into four proofs (embedding, inference, training, compute), made
sound by determinism, made asynchronous by a streaming Merkle Mountain Range with
interactive bisection, and made unforgeable by a measured-weights binding into
\code{reportData}. The core is proven and tested and the chain already refuses to
mint without a proof; emitting that proof end-to-end from a running zen model is
the next, designed, slice.
