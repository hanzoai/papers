\begin{abstract}
We present \emph{Datastore}, Hanzo's analytics database: a fork of the
ClickHouse columnar engine (Apache~2.0) re-engineered for a leaner resident
footprint, a smaller binary, and a thinner transport surface. We report three
results. First, applying profile-guided optimization (PGO), ThinLTO, and the
BOLT post-link optimizer yields a server whose steady-state resident set is
$49$--$53$\,MB versus $\sim\!116$\,MB for the upstream official binary --- less
than half --- while matching or exceeding its throughput on aggregation and
filter workloads. Second, we trace a long-standing failure of BOLT on AArch64
to an unexpected root cause: default-enabled XRay instrumentation emits composed
\texttt{R\_AARCH64\_PREL64} relocations that the AArch64 path of \texttt{llvm-bolt}
cannot lower, manifesting as a spurious ``signature is not valid'' on every
ephemeral runner. Disabling XRay --- correct for a release binary regardless ---
both unblocks BOLT and removes $\sim\!45$\,MB of sled sections, shrinking the
binary from $945$\,MB to $648$\,MB. Third, we replace the gRPC/ArrowFlight stack
with ZAP, a Cap'n-Proto-derived transport, and show that carrying native
columnar blocks as opaque ZAP payloads with promise pipelining flattens
request latency in chain depth, a $63\times$ improvement at depth~64 over a
request-response baseline. Resident memory is flat from $10$ to $10{,}000$
concurrent connections. We close with the implications for hosting Datastore as
a multi-tenant, provisionable offering on a native PaaS.
\end{abstract}
