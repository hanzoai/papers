\section{Decisions as programs for studies}
\label{sec:studies}

The objects of the previous sections were built for an agent's inner loop, but
nothing in them is specific to agents. A trade study, an analysis of
alternatives or a source selection is also a set of typed judgments over
evidence, aggregated into a recommendation. Today such a study usually lives in
slides and spreadsheets: the scores survive, the reasons for them are in
someone's memory, and a year later, when a cost estimate or a test result
changes, the study is redone from the beginning because nobody can say which
of its conclusions depended on the number that moved. Public programs now ask
for the alternative. The U.S.\ Army's 2026 SBIR topic ARM26BX06-NV012,
\emph{Agentic-AI, Schema-Driven Decision Management for Auditable Studies and
Acquisition Decisions}~\cite{armysbir}, asks for studies that are defined by a
schema, executed with agentic assistance, auditable after the fact, and
refreshable as evidence changes. This section describes how the objects above
provide that \proposed.

\subsection{The study as a program}

A study program fixes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item alternatives $A = \{a_1, \dots, a_n\}$;
  \item criteria $J = \{j_1, \dots, j_m\}$ with weights
    $w \in \Delta^{m-1}$, $w_j \ge 0$, $\sum_j w_j = 1$;
  \item for each criterion $j$, ordered levels $L_j$ with a description of
    each and a value $u_j(\ell) \in [0, 1]$ for each level, in the manner of
    multi-attribute utility theory~\cite{keeney1976};
  \item named assumptions $\theta$, each with a value and a declared range: a
    budget ceiling, a fielding date, a discount rate, a threat estimate;
  \item constraints, predicates $g(a; \theta)$ that an alternative must
    satisfy to be considered at all, such as fitting the budget or meeting a
    threshold requirement;
  \item for each cell $(a, j)$, an executor and its inputs. Most cells are a
    typed \op{score} question whose levels are $L_j$, answered by Kai over the
    evidence a query selects for alternative $a$. A cell that is arithmetic,
    such as a life-cycle cost from a cost model or a range from a performance
    model, is computed by that deterministic model from the evidence and
    $\theta$, and its value placed on the same scale.
\end{itemize}
It is a Decision Program in the sense of Section~\ref{sec:programs}: named,
versioned, immutable, bound to a model and a calibration. Judgments go to Kai,
arithmetic goes to deterministic code, and nothing numeric comes from
generated prose.

\paragraph{Aggregation.}
Cell $(a, j)$ returns a distribution $p_{aj}$ over $L_j$. Its expected value
and variance are
\begin{equation}
  U_{aj} = \sum_{\ell \in L_j} p_{aj}(\ell)\, u_j(\ell), \qquad
  \sigma^2_{aj} = \sum_{\ell \in L_j} p_{aj}(\ell)\, u_j(\ell)^2 - U_{aj}^2,
\end{equation}
and an alternative's value is the weighted sum
\begin{equation}
  V(a) = \sum_{j} w_j\, U_{aj}, \qquad
  \operatorname{Var} V(a) = \sum_j w_j^2\, \sigma^2_{aj},
  \label{eq:value}
\end{equation}
the variance under the assumption that cells are answered independently. A
deterministic cell has $\sigma_{aj} = 0$ unless its model reports an
uncertainty. The recommendation is the best feasible alternative,
$a^\star = \arg\max \{V(a) : g(a; \theta) \text{ holds for every constraint}\}$.

The additive form assumes the criteria are preferentially independent; where
they are not, the study must say so and use a different aggregation, and the
sensitivity results below apply only to the additive one.

\subsection{The DecisionPackage}

The unit of record for a study is a DecisionPackage: the study program and its
version; the assumptions and constraints in force; every cell's question or
deterministic model, evidence references with content hashes, model
revision, calibration and full answer distribution; every human override,
recorded beside the answer it overrides with its author and reason rather than
in place of it; the policy results for any cell a policy governs; the values
$V(a)$ with their variances; the recommendation; the sensitivity report of
Section~\ref{sec:flip}; and the approvals, recording who approved the program
before it ran and who accepted the recommendation after. A package is the audit trail: every number in the
study's conclusion resolves to a field in it, and every field resolves to an
answer and the evidence the answer read.

A package separates what people decided from what models estimated, and
each kind of work goes to the component suited to it. People decide the
alternatives, criteria, weights, level descriptions, value functions,
assumptions and constraints, and approve the program that encodes them. Kai
makes the bounded judgments. Deterministic models do the arithmetic and check
the constraints. Policy governs who may approve what. People may override any
cell. The generative plane helps with each step it does not own: it elicits
criteria and level descriptions from source documents and interviews, proposes
the evidence query for each cell, reasons through the ambiguous engineering
questions a person must then settle, and writes the narrative of the final
report. Every number the narrative states must resolve to a package
field, and a sentence that states one that does not is rejected, in the way
our assertion graph rejects an uncited claim in a generated
answer~\cite{hanzographpaper}.

\subsection{What flips the decision}
\label{sec:flip}

A recommendation is only as useful as the account of how fragile it is. A
package reports four answers to ``what would flip it?''.

\paragraph{Weights.}
For a challenger $b$, write $\delta_b = U_{a^\star} - U_b \in \mathbb{R}^m$ for
the difference of cell values across criteria, so that
$V(a^\star) - V(b) = w^{\top}\delta_b > 0$. Let
$P = I - \tfrac{1}{m}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}$ project onto the directions
that keep the weights summing to one.

\begin{proposition}[Smallest weight change]
\label{prop:weights}
The smallest change $\Delta$ in Euclidean norm with $\mathbf{1}^{\top}\Delta = 0$
that makes $b$ tie $a^\star$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta^\star_b = -\frac{w^{\top}\delta_b}{\lVert P\delta_b\rVert^2}\,
  P\delta_b, \qquad
  \lVert \Delta^\star_b \rVert = \frac{w^{\top}\delta_b}{\lVert P\delta_b\rVert},
  \label{eq:weightflip}
\end{equation}
provided $P\delta_b \ne 0$. If $w + \Delta^\star_b \ge 0$ it is the smallest
change within the simplex; otherwise it is a lower bound on that change.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Minimise $\lVert\Delta\rVert^2$ subject to $\mathbf{1}^{\top}\Delta = 0$ and
$\delta_b^{\top}\Delta = -w^{\top}\delta_b$. Since $\Delta$ lies in the range
of $P$ and $P$ is symmetric and idempotent,
$\delta_b^{\top}\Delta = (P\delta_b)^{\top}\Delta$, so this is the distance
from the origin to a hyperplane in that subspace with normal $P\delta_b$,
attained at the stated point. Adding the constraint $w + \Delta \ge 0$
shrinks the feasible set, which can only raise the minimum. If $P\delta_b = 0$,
every weight vector ranks $a^\star$ and $b$ the same way and no change of
weights flips them.
\end{proof}

The package reports $\rho_w = \min_b \lVert\Delta^\star_b\rVert$, the
challenger that attains it, and the direction: which criteria's weights must
rise and which fall. A stakeholder who disagrees with the weights can read
from it how far they would have to disagree to change the answer.

\paragraph{Answers.}
Moving one cell's value changes one alternative's $V$ by $w_j$ times the
move. For the runner-up $b$ with margin $\Delta V = V(a^\star) - V(b)$, the
recommendation flips if $U_{a^\star j}$ falls by $\Delta V / w_j$ or $U_{bj}$
rises by $\Delta V / w_j$, and likewise for every other challenger with its own
margin. We report each cell's required shift $r_{aj}$ in units of the cell's
own standard deviation, $z_{aj} = r_{aj}/\sigma_{aj}$. A cell with small
$z_{aj}$ is one where the recommendation turns on a judgment the model is
itself unsure of: that is where an analyst's time, or more evidence, is worth
the most, the question the value of information literature
asks~\cite{howard1966}. A cell whose required shift exceeds the range of $u_j$
cannot flip the recommendation alone and is reported as such.

\paragraph{Assumptions and constraints.}
Weights and answers are not the only inputs a reviewer doubts. For each
assumption $\theta_i$ the package sweeps its declared range, holding the
others fixed, recomputes the cells that depend on it, and reports the
break-even value $\theta_i^\star$, if there is one in the range, at which the
recommendation changes, whether because another alternative's value overtakes
$a^\star$'s or because a constraint admits or excludes an alternative. Where
the margin $V(a^\star; \theta) - \max_{b} V(b; \theta)$ is continuous in
$\theta_i$ and changes sign across the range, bisection finds the crossing;
otherwise a grid does, and the package says which. Only cells whose inputs
include $\theta_i$ are recomputed at each point, by the reuse rule of
Proposition~\ref{prop:refresh}, and a deterministic cell costs a function
call. ``The recommendation holds for any budget above \$41M and any fielding
date before 2029'' is the kind of sentence this produces, and each of its
numbers resolves to a sweep in the package.

\paragraph{The model's own uncertainty.}
Sampling a level for every cell from its $p_{aj}$ and recomputing $\arg\max$
estimates the probability that the recommendation would differ if each cell
were resolved, $\Pr[\arg\max_a V(a) \ne a^\star]$. The estimate is as good as
the calibration of the cells it samples, which is one more reason the program
records the calibration it was run under.

\paragraph{An illustration.}
The following is a constructed example, not data. Three alternatives are
scored on cost ($w = 0.40$), performance ($0.35$) and schedule risk ($0.25$):

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lccc|c@{}}
\toprule
 & cost & performance & schedule & $V$ \\
\midrule
A & 0.60 & 0.80 & 0.70 & 0.695 \\
B & 0.80 & 0.60 & 0.60 & 0.680 \\
C & 0.50 & 0.70 & 0.90 & 0.670 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A is recommended by 0.015 over B and 0.025 over C. For B,
$\delta_B = (-0.20, 0.20, 0.10)$ and Equation~\eqref{eq:weightflip} gives
$\Delta^\star_B = (+0.040, -0.029, -0.012)$, of norm 0.051: raising the cost
weight from 0.40 to 0.44, taken from the other two, makes B tie A. For C the
change is $(-0.042, -0.042, +0.083)$, of norm 0.102, a rise in the schedule
weight from 0.25 to 0.33. Both stay in the simplex, so both are exact, and
$\rho_w = 0.051$ toward B.

For the answers, suppose A's performance cell is the least certain,
$\sigma = 0.12$, with $\sigma = 0.03$ for B's cost, $0.04$ for the schedule
cells of A and B, and $0.05$ elsewhere. The shift that flips A against B is
$0.015/0.35 = 0.043$ on A's performance, which is $z = 0.36$; the next
smallest is A's cost, $0.015/0.40 = 0.038$ at $\sigma = 0.05$, $z = 0.75$;
no cell of C can flip the recommendation within less than $z = 1.25$. The
package would say: the recommendation rests on A's performance evidence, and a
third of a standard deviation there changes it. Taking each cell as normal
with these moments, B beats A with probability 0.39 and C beats A with
probability 0.33, and in $4 \times 10^5$ joint samples A is best in 54\%, B in
28\% and C in 19\%. The study recommends A and says, in the same document,
that the recommendation is close to a three-way tie which one more performance
measurement of A would most likely settle.

\subsection{Refresh}

A study refreshed a year later should cost in proportion to what changed. Give
each cell a key: the program version, the question, the model revision and
calibration, and the content hashes of the evidence its query selected.

\begin{proposition}[Exact reuse]
\label{prop:refresh}
If a cell's key is unchanged and inference is deterministic on the backend,
its answer distribution is unchanged, so reusing the stored answer gives the
same package as recomputing it. A refresh then asks $O(c)$ questions for $c$
changed cells, and every value, variance and sensitivity figure is recomputed
from the cells, which is arithmetic.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The distribution is a function of the question, the state and the model
revision with its calibration (Section~\ref{sec:decisions}); the state of a
cell is the evidence its key hashes. Equal keys give equal inputs.
\end{proof}

A refresh produces a new package and a diff: which cells changed and by how
much, whether $a^\star$ changed, and how $\rho_w$ and the cells' $z$ values
moved. The refresh can run on a schedule or on an event, when a new document
lands that some cell's query selects.

\subsection{Evidence and time}

A package must be able to say not only what it concluded but what was known
when it concluded it. Hanzo's temporal assertion graph~\cite{hanzographpaper}
answers every read at a valid instant and a transaction instant, and an answer
as known at an instant is unchanged by every later write except erasure. If a
study's evidence queries read the graph and the package records the
transaction instant of the run, the package can be replayed exactly as known
on the day it was decided, even after the evidence was later corrected, and the
diff between that replay and a refresh separates what changed in the world
from what changed in the record.

Engineering studies increasingly keep their alternatives, requirements and
analyses in a system model. SysML~v2~\cite{sysmlv2} gives such models a
textual notation and a standard API, which makes them addressable as evidence:
an alternative can be a reference to a part definition, a criterion to a
requirement, and a cell's state to the serialised elements its question should
read. We have not yet built this binding.
