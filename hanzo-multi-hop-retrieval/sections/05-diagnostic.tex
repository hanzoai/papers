\section{Why no number of hops helps}
\label{sec:diagnostic}

Rather than try a fifth policy we measured the evidence itself.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
\textbf{Structure of multi-hop evidence (\rQmulti{} questions)} & \\
\midrule
Gold turns per question, mean              & \rGoldAvg{} \meas{} \\
Gold turns per question, max               & \rGoldMax{} \meas{} \\
Evidence spanning more than one session    & \textbf{\rSpanSessions{}\%} \meas{} \\
\midrule
Median rank of a gold turn                 & \rMedRank{} \meas{} \\
Median rank of the \emph{worst} gold turn  & \textbf{\rMedWorst{}} \meas{} \\
Worst gold turn beyond $k{=}20$            & \rBeyondTwenty{}\% \meas{} \\
Worst gold turn beyond $k{=}100$           & \rBeyondHundred{}\% \meas{} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{The evidence a multi-hop question needs, and where a single similarity
search puts it.}
\label{tab:diagnostic}
\end{table}

Three facts in that table between them close the case.

\paragraph{The evidence is not one neighbourhood; it is several.}
\rSpanSessions{}\% of multi-hop evidence spans more than one conversation
session. These are turns separated by simulated weeks, about different immediate
topics, phrased differently, related only because they concern the same entity.
Nothing about that relation is expressed in the wording, so nothing about it is
expressed in a similarity metric over wordings. A second search in the same space
is a second look at the same neighbourhood.

\paragraph{The hard turn is not near the easy one.}
The median gold turn is at rank \rMedRank{}, comfortably retrievable. The median
\emph{worst} gold turn is at rank \rMedWorst{}. That distance is the entire
failure: a question is answered only when the last of its evidence arrives, so
recall@$k$ \textbf{all} is governed by the worst-ranked gold turn and by nothing
else. Improving the rank of turns that already rank 24 --- which is what an extra
hop from a top hit does --- moves a number the metric does not depend on.

\paragraph{No affordable $k$ rescues it.}
The worst gold turn is beyond $k{=}20$ for \rBeyondTwenty{}\% of questions and
still beyond $k{=}100$ for \rBeyondHundred{}\%. Retrieving 100 turns to answer
one question is most of a short conversation; it defeats the purpose of
retrieving and it degrades the answer by burying the evidence in context. So the
brute-force escape is closed too, and it is closed by measurement rather than by
taste.

\paragraph{Putting it together.}
The retriever finds \emph{a} relevant turn nearly always (\S\ref{sec:baseline}),
because for any multi-hop question at least one piece of evidence does resemble
the question. It misses the others because they resemble the question far less
--- they are linked to it through an entity and a timeline, not through
vocabulary. Every policy in \S\ref{sec:policies} is a way of spending more
similarity, and similarity is the axis along which the missing evidence is
\emph{far}. This is why the negative result is not a tuning failure: no setting
of a similarity-based policy has access to the signal that would fix it.
