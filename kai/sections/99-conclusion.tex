\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

An agent's small decisions are where its cost, its safety and its quality are
decided, and most of them do not need a generative model. A typed question
over explicit state, answered by an encoder in one forward pass, gives a
distribution a caller can threshold, record and replay. Putting that answer
under a deterministic policy with a join makes the safety of the system
independent of the model's, and reduces the question of when to trust the
model to a count of catches and friction that shadow mode records exactly.
Versioned programs bound to calibrations keep thresholds honest as models
change, and the same objects, applied to a study, give a recommendation with
an audit trail, an account of what would flip it, and a refresh that costs
what changed.

The bootstrap checkpoints do not yet know Hanzo's questions, and our one
recorded run says so plainly. What is built is the structure that lets them
learn safely: the wire that makes Kai and Jev interchangeable, the programs
that fix what is asked, the records that turn shadow decisions into labels, and
the policy that stays in charge while they do.
