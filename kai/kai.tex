\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Agents, controllers and engineering studies ask many small questions of
explicit state. Kai answers them as typed decisions, a \op{choice},
\op{score} or \op{noul} over options the caller lists, returned as a
calibrated distribution with no generated text. Kai is one model: a
multilingual encoder, adapters that bring sensor streams with their metadata,
audio, images and video into it, and a typed-variable decoder. Options are
encoded alone and scored against the evidence by two cross-attention blocks, so
option count, order and question count are not bounded by one sequence;
\op{noul} is label-symmetric by construction, \op{score} a cumulative-link
model, and large choices are narrowed by retrieval that keeps the mass it cuts.
A program's dependent questions are typed variables, refined in waves bounded
by dependency depth and projected onto the program's rules. Programs are
versioned DAGs whose verdicts join a deterministic policy on
$\allow < \ask < \deny$, so no model loosens policy, and each run is a hashed
package that replays, diffs and refreshes. We report capabilities existing
decision models lack, speed, and accuracy on a frozen 62-suite harness against
Laya and TypeSafe's Jev, and design a systems evaluation of Kai controlling
Enso, whose thesis is that Kai does not make Zen generate faster but makes it
generate less. Every claim carries its status, and every number is written by a
script from a kept run.
\end{abstract}
