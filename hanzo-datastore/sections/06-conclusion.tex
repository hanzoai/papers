\section{Deployment and Multi-Tenancy}

Datastore is built to be hosted as a provisionable, multi-tenant offering on a
native PaaS. Its compatibility surface makes the two analytics products Hanzo
operates --- a privacy-preserving web-analytics service and a product-analytics
platform --- migrate by configuration alone: both already isolate tenants by a
column key (\texttt{organization\_id} and \texttt{team\_id} respectively) over a
shared database and schema, so pointing them at a Datastore instance is a change
of host, not of data model. The leaner resident footprint compounds directly in
this setting: at $\sim\!50$\,MB per instance instead of $\sim\!116$\,MB, an
operator more than doubles the number of tenant instances per node at fixed
memory, which is the dimension that governs the unit economics of a managed
service.

Because the engine derives from ClickHouse under the permissive Apache~2.0
license, Datastore is freely re-brandable, hostable, and resellable --- as are
the adjacent Hanzo data products on equally permissive upstreams (PostgreSQL,
pgvector, SQLite, PocketBase, and the Valkey-derived key-value store). This
licensing posture is what makes a commercial managed-database offering built on
these components viable without copyleft entanglement.

\section{Conclusion}

We have shown that the operationally dominant costs of a columnar analytics
server --- resident memory and binary size --- yield substantially to a
disciplined build pipeline. PGO, ThinLTO, and BOLT together produce a Datastore
server at less than half the resident footprint of the upstream official binary
with equal or better read throughput, and memory that is flat from $10$ to
$10{,}000$ connections. Along the way we identified that default-enabled XRay
instrumentation was both bloating the binary by $\sim\!45$\,MB and, through
unlowerable composed \texttt{R\_AARCH64\_PREL64} relocations, silently blocking
BOLT on AArch64 --- a fix that is one disabled default. Finally, replacing gRPC
with a Cap'n-Proto-derived transport that carries native columnar blocks as
opaque payloads exposes promise pipelining to clients, flattening dependent-chain
latency by up to $63\times$ without disturbing the engine's bounded memory
behavior.

The work points to two clear next steps: promoting ZAP from a transport to the
primary, ZAP-native query path, and extending the same posture to the
coordination layer. Both are constrained by the same principle that guided this
work --- preserve compatibility, pay only for what the workload uses, and let the
measurements, not the defaults, decide what ships.
