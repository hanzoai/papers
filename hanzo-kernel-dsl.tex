\title{\textbf{Fusion Is Composition:\\
A Category-Theoretic Kernel DSL that Collapses the\\
Backends\,\x\,Ops\,\x\,Types Product}}
\author{Hanzo AI --- ML Systems}
\date{Paper 07 --- companion to Paper 06 (\emph{When Does a Kernel Win Surface?})}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A multi-backend inference engine hand-writes the same $\sim$20 operations, specialized over $\sim$22
GGUF quantization formats, once per GPU backend --- a Cartesian product of five kernel trees and
$\sim$52{,}000 lines that is not merely a maintenance cost but the breeding ground for
divergence bugs, because one operation with five implementations has five numeric behaviors. We
present \hk{}, a first-party Rust GPU-kernel DSL that collapses this product to a sum, built on one
organizing idea: \textbf{fusion is composition} --- the functor law $\mathrm{map}(g)\circ\mathrm{map}(f)
= \mathrm{map}(g\circ f)$ made mechanical, with Reduce/Scan as algebraic fences, so ``can these two
ops fuse?'' is a total function of the op's \emph{class}, not a pattern matcher or a cost model.
On this base sit three mechanisms that each attack one axis of the product: \emph{comptime-specialized
autotune} (one kernel source, its schedule exposed as compile-time knobs the tuner picks per device,
every variant byte-identical); \emph{intrinsic islands} (scoped target-gated blocks inside one kernel
whose \code{default} arm is the normative oracle); and \emph{a CPU runtime as the bit-exact oracle}
for every backend. We report the evidence: 10/10 op families bit-exact against the CPU oracle;
DSL$\to$Vulkan and DSL$\to$ROCm dispatch bit-exact on real hardware (rms\_norm on gfx1151,
$\text{max\_rel}=2.43\times10^{-7}$); a single-source \dpa{} matvec at \textbf{118\% of the hand-tuned
kernel} it replaces (196~GB/s, gfx1151); a shipped Vulkan migration where the DSL twin was
\textbf{7.1\x{} faster} than the hand-written shader \emph{and} bit-exact; and a nightly three-backend
GPU CI (self-hosted CUDA/ROCm/Metal) that enforces the bit-exactness law. We also report a
scientifically load-bearing \emph{negative} result: int8 tensor-core MMQ via the high-level WMMA API is
structurally sync-bound (the opaque accumulator fragment forces a store$\to$sync$\to$reload$\to$scale per
32-element block; a better-tiled version is \emph{slower}), so block-quantized GEMM stays hand-written
behind benchmark gates --- which is exactly what islands are designed to hold. None of Triton, XLA,
torch.compile, TVM, Halide, Burn, or Mojo models fusion as morphism composition; we state precisely
what is and is not novel.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The kernel layer of a multi-backend LLM engine is a product. Table~\ref{tab:dup} is the current
inventory in the Hanzo ML and engine repositories: five hand-written kernel trees --- CUDA C++, HIP
C++, Metal Shading Language, GLSL$\to$SPIR-V, and the engine's own CUDA --- carrying on the order of
$52{,}000$ lines of hand-written kernel arithmetic. The same $\sim$20 operations (matvec, matmul,
flash attention, RMSNorm, RoPE, softmax, the Gated-DeltaNet linear-attention ops) are re-implemented in
each, and within each they are specialized over $\sim$22 GGUF quantization formats. Three axes ---
\emph{backends}, \emph{ops}, \emph{types} --- are multiplied by hand.

That product is where the correctness bugs breed, and Paper~06 catalogs the class concretely: a
Qwen3-8B model that generates coherently on Metal collapses into token repetition under CUDA
flash-attention, byte-identical weights and sampler, because \emph{the same operation has a different
kernel per backend and therefore a different numeric behavior}. Uniform numeric behavior across
backends is not something four or five copies can be tested \emph{into}; it is a structural property of
having exactly one implementation. The engineering goal, then, is not ``write the kernels more
carefully'' but ``write each operation once and lower it everywhere,'' turning the product into a sum:
\[
\underbrace{\text{backends} \x \text{types} \x \text{ops}}_{\text{hand-written today}}
\;\longrightarrow\;
\underbrace{\text{backends} + \text{types} + \text{ops}}_{\text{one source, lowered}}.
\]

copy, machine-generated codebook tables (e.g. the i-quant grids), and vendored upstream kernels
(FlashInfer, Marlin, GPTQ) that are neither hand-written duplication nor a DSL collapse target.}

\paragraph{Thesis and contributions.}
This paper argues that the collapse is not an engineering slog of retyping kernels into a portable
syntax but follows from one algebraic idea, applied three ways. The idea is that \textbf{kernel fusion
is morphism composition}: an elementwise kernel is the lift of a scalar function to every array index,
and the functor law $\mathrm{map}(g)\circ\mathrm{map}(f)=\mathrm{map}(g\circ f)$ read right-to-left
\emph{is} fusion --- one kernel computing the composite, no intermediate array. Reduce/Scan are the
\emph{fences} where the pattern changes, decidable from the op's class, not from a pattern matcher.
Concretely, we contribute:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Fusion as composition} (\S\ref{sec:fuse}): an op algebra in which legal fusion is a total
function of op \emph{class}, one fused-interpreter kernel that lowers any composed chain to a single
launch with zero intermediates, and its generalization from a line to an arbitrary DAG --- all
bit-exact-gated on the CPU (fused one-kernel $=$ na\"ive $N$-kernel $=$ plain-Rust reference).
\item \textbf{Comptime-specialized autotune} (\S\ref{sec:tune}): the schedule of a single-source kernel
exposed as \code{\#[comptime]} knobs the engine monomorphizes over; a small variant set timed once per
$(\text{device},\text{op},\text{shape})$ and cached to XDG; every variant byte-identical, so the tuner
picks speed without touching numerics --- the ``one op, $N$ device forks'' bug class killed.
\item \textbf{Intrinsic islands} (\S\ref{sec:island}): a target-gated block \emph{inside} one kernel
whose \code{default} arm is normative and whose accelerated arms are proven byte-for-byte equal to it
on the CPU oracle --- the disciplined seam for the rare instruction that truly needs a per-backend idiom.
\item \textbf{The CPU oracle law and its CI} (\S\ref{sec:oracle}): the CPU runtime is the single
bit-exact reference for every backend, enforced nightly on self-hosted CUDA/ROCm/Metal runners.
\item \textbf{Evidence, positive and negative} (\S\ref{sec:eval}--\ref{sec:negative}): cross-backend
bit-exactness, a portable kernel that \emph{beats} the hand-tuned one, a shipped migration that improved
performance, and an honest structural negative result on int8 tensor-core MMQ.
\end{enumerate}

We adopt CubeCL as the lowering engine and wrap it in a first-party facade so that kernel source names
only \code{hanzo\_kernel::*}; the string \code{cubecl} appears in exactly one line of one
\code{Cargo.toml}. That is ``values, not places'': CubeCL is the implementation \emph{value};
\hk{} is the stable \emph{namespace} we build against, upgradable or forkable without editing a kernel.

\section{Design: the facade and the three axes}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Three orthogonal axes, one source}
The kernel code braids three independent concerns. Pulled apart, each is written once and they compose
(Table~\ref{tab:axes}). The transformation is not new in spirit --- ROCm already decomplected two of
the three axes, writing its kernels as \code{qmatvec\_core<WTYPE>}: one contraction, the quantization
type injected as a compile-time template parameter. The DSL is exactly that pattern with the
\emph{backend} as the other compile-time axis. We are generalizing a decomplect already trusted in
production, not inventing an untested one.

\subsection{A code generator, not a second runtime}
\hk{} lowers a \code{\#[kernel]} to SPIR-V for Vulkan, to CUDA/PTX for CUDA, to MSL for Metal, and
through cubecl-hip for ROCm. Crucially it is a \emph{code generator}, not a competing GPU runtime: the
engine's Vulkan path is already \code{include\_bytes!(<name>.spv)} $\to$ \code{create\_shader\_module}
$\to$ dispatch, and CubeCL emits exactly those bytes. A DSL RMSNorm dumped and inspected is a
\code{GLCompute} entry point with buffers on descriptor bindings $0,1,2$ --- byte-for-byte the shape
the engine's hand-written \code{.comp}$\to$\code{.spv} shaders already take. So the DSL plugs into each
backend's \emph{existing} dispatch machinery; there is no dual runtime and no cross-runtime copy. The
one bounded integration step is a three-line SPIR-V post-pass (rename the entry point to \code{main};
strip CubeCL's unused info-uniform at binding~3 via \code{spirv-opt --remove-unused-interface-variables};
dispatch), automated by a reusable emitter.

\subsection{The kernel-authoring surface}
Listing~\ref{lst:names} shows the facade's first-principles vocabulary: \code{\#[kernel]} says what it
is (a GPU kernel), not \code{cube} (a brand); \code{Grid} and \code{Block} name the launch shape
directly. A kernel's only import is \code{use hanzo\_kernel::prelude::*;}.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust,caption={The Q8\_0 matvec: one source, no backend in it. The decode
(\code{wd*wq}) and the contraction are the arithmetic each backend hand-writes today.},label={lst:names}]
use hanzo_kernel::prelude::*;

#[kernel(targets(cuda, metal, vulkan, webgpu, cpu), unchecked)]
pub fn matvec_q8<F: Float>(wd: &Array<F>, wq: &Array<i32>, x: &Array<F>,
                           out: &mut Array<F>, #[comptime] k: usize) {
    let row = ABSOLUTE_POS;
    if row < out.len() {
        let (nb, wbase, dbase) = (k / 32, row * k, row * (k / 32));
        let mut acc = F::new(0.0);
        for i in 0..k { acc += wd[dbase + i / 32] * F::cast_from(wq[wbase + i]) * x[i]; }
        out[row] = acc;
    }
}
\end{lstlisting}

The perf primitives the hand-tuned kernels rely on are all reachable and each lowers to the native
instruction: \code{dot} on a vectorized \code{Line<i8>} $\to$ \dpa{}\,/\,\code{OpSDotAccSat};
\code{cmma} $\to$ WMMA\,/\,cooperative-matrix\,/\,simdgroup; \code{SharedMemory}, the \code{plane\_*}
subgroup reductions, \code{Atomic}, and barriers. The DSL is a superset of what we express by hand, so
nothing about portability forces a performance downgrade --- a claim we make good on in \S\ref{sec:eval}
and qualify honestly in \S\ref{sec:negative}.

\section{Mechanism I: fusion is composition}
\label{sec:fuse}

\subsection{The functor law, read right-to-left}
An elementwise (pointwise) kernel is the lift of a scalar morphism $f:F\to F$ applied at every index.
Pointwise lifts obey the functor law
\[
\mathrm{map}(g)\circ\mathrm{map}(f) \;=\; \mathrm{map}(g\circ f).
\]
Read left-to-right this is two kernels with a materialized intermediate array between them; read
right-to-left it is \emph{one} kernel computing the composed scalar function with \emph{no}
intermediate. Kernel fusion is nothing but that law read right-to-left. It is exactly the transducer
story: \code{map(f)} and \code{map(g)} are transformations independent of the collection, so you compose
the \emph{transformations} and apply the composite to the collection once. Here the collection is a GPU
array and ``once'' is one launch. Categorically the Maps are morphisms $\mathrm{Array}\to\mathrm{Array}$
and composition of morphisms \emph{is} fusion --- there is no separate ``fusion pass'' concept, only the
associativity of $\circ$.

\subsection{The class decides fusibility}
The whole surface is a tiny op algebra, and legal fusion falls out of the op's \emph{class}, not from a
pattern matcher over concrete ops:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Map} (pointwise morphism): output element $i$ depends only on input(s) at index $i$.
Shape-preserving, index-local. Maps compose freely: any run of Maps is one Map (the functor law). This
is the fusible family --- \code{Add Sub Mul Div Neg Recip Abs Exp Rsqrt Sigmoid Tanh Silu Gelu}.
\item \textbf{Reduce\,/\,Scan} (contraction): output $i$ depends on a whole row/axis
(\code{sum}, \code{max}, \code{mean} $\to$ RMSNorm, softmax, matmul). Not index-local, so it cannot be
absorbed into an adjacent Map by the same lift; it needs cross-lane communication. A Reduce is therefore
a \emph{fence}: a preceding Map run fuses into its input \emph{read} (prologue) and a following Map run
fuses into its output \emph{write} (epilogue), but the reduction is the seam where the kernel pattern
changes.
\end{itemize}
The decision ``can these two adjacent ops fuse?'' reduces to
$\mathrm{fusible}(a,b) := \mathrm{is\_map}(a)\wedge\mathrm{is\_map}(b)$, plus prologue/epilogue
attachment at a Reduce's ports. Nothing else. The fusion pass is one fold: walk the chain, compose
adjacent Maps by substituting the previous expression for the current-value leaf (\code{compose\_cur},
which \emph{is} $g[\,\code{Cur}:=f\,]$), and cut a new region at every Reduce.

\subsection{One kernel interprets the whole chain}
The fused Map region is lowered to a small linear ``op program'' carried as a \code{\#[comptime]} value
and walked in a comptime-\emph{unrolled} loop, so each op becomes straight-line arithmetic in one kernel
body --- no runtime interpreter, no intermediate arrays, no extra launches
(Listing~\ref{lst:fused}). It is not a hand-written fixed kernel: any linear elementwise chain the
builder produces runs through this one \code{fused\_interp}, whatever its length.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust,caption={The one fused kernel. It reads the primary input once,
threads it through the comptime program (unrolled to straight-line code), writes once. Every
\code{Instr} is inlined; one launch, zero intermediates.},label={lst:fused}]
#[kernel(targets(cuda, metal, vulkan, webgpu, cpu), unchecked)]
pub fn fused_interp<F: Float>(x: &Array<F>, sides: &Sequence<Array<F>>,
                              out: &mut Array<F>, #[comptime] prog: Program) {
    let i = ABSOLUTE_POS;
    if i < out.len() {
        let mut acc = x[i];
        #[unroll]
        for step in 0..prog.instrs.len() {
            match comptime!(prog.instrs[step]) {
                Instr::Un(op)          => acc = apply_un_dev::<F>(op, acc),
                Instr::BinIn(op, slot) => acc = apply_bin_dev::<F>(op, acc, sides.index(slot)[i]),
                Instr::BinConst(op, b) => { let c = comptime!(f32::from_bits(b));
                                            acc = apply_bin_dev::<F>(op, acc, F::new(c)) }
            }
        }
        out[i] = acc;
    }
}
\end{lstlisting}

The ergonomic surface is a builder that reads like the composition it denotes:
\code{Fuse::new(a).\allowbreak{}mul(w).\allowbreak{}add(b).\allowbreak{}silu().\allowbreak{}run(c)} appends one \code{Instr} per call, compiles
them to one \code{Program}, and dispatches exactly one \code{fused\_interp}. Because the \code{Program}
rides as a comptime value, the constant leaf is stored as \code{f32::to\_bits} so the program stays
\code{Eq + Hash} --- the lowering engine keys the compiled kernel on it, one compiled kernel per distinct
fused chain.

\subsection{From a line to a DAG}
A real forward pass is not a line: a value fans out (used by several ops) and a binary fans in (reads two
independently computed values). \code{silu(a)*b + a} reuses $a$ twice and a linear chain cannot express
it in one region; a DAG can, and it still fuses to one kernel. The same algebra lifts from a path to a
graph: a \code{NodeId}-taping newtype records an arbitrary pointwise DAG as a \code{Vec<Node>} (operator
overloads push nodes instead of computing); a flood-fill partitions maximal Map-only connected regions,
cutting every edge into a fence --- the identical \code{Class} predicate applied to graph edges instead
of list-adjacency; and one generic \code{dag\_interp} kernel interprets a per-region DAG program over a
per-element slot array (fan-out $=$ a slot read by several later instructions; fan-in $=$ a binary
reading two slots), generalizing \code{fused\_interp}'s single accumulator to $n$ accumulators. The
concrete payoff is measured on the real engine pattern: the per-GDN-layer chain
\code{rms\_norm(x)*w*silu(g)} fuses from \textbf{9 launches to 5}, bit-exact
($\text{max}=2.98\times10^{-8}$) against the na\"ive per-op path --- directly draining the prefill
launch storm these hybrid models suffer.

\subsection{The bit-exact gate}
Every fused chain is checked three ways on the CPU runtime: (i) the fused one kernel, (ii) the na\"ive
$N$-kernel pipeline that materializes $N{-}1$ device intermediates, and (iii) a plain-Rust reference ---
all byte-for-byte identical. The tests exercise the two patterns the llama.cpp fusion audit named plus a
stress case: the SwiGLU tail \code{silu(a)*b} (2 kernels $\to$ 1) and \code{silu(a*w+b)} (3 $\to$ 1) are
\emph{byte-identical} fused-versus-na\"ive with reference error $\le 4.8\times10^{-7}$, and a 7-op mixed
chain (\code{gelu((|2a{-}1|+b)/sigmoid(w)).tanh()}) runs in one launch, matching its host oracle. The
device morphism table \code{apply\_un\_dev} is a line-for-line mirror of the host \code{apply\_un} ---
which is \emph{why} fused equals oracle bit-exactly, not merely within tolerance.

\paragraph{Why this is the right base.} Fusibility is a property of the op's \emph{type}, computed once,
not rediscovered by a matcher for each new op combination. Adding an op adds one algebra variant and its
device mirror; it does not add fusion rules. The reduction and matmul kernels stay hand-tuned and the
Map regions attach to them as prologue/epilogue --- the fusion composes \emph{with} the peak kernels
rather than competing with them.

\section{Mechanism II: comptime-specialized autotune}
\label{sec:tune}

\subsection{The collapse, closed on the schedule}
The DSL already gives one source lowered to every backend. Autotune closes the loop on the last place a
backend used to fork a kernel: the \emph{schedule}. Hand-tuning a schedule per device --- tile size,
vector width, unroll factor, cube dimension --- is the ``same op, $N$ forks, $N$ drifting numeric
behaviors'' bug class in a subtler dress. The fix is to expose the schedule as \code{\#[comptime]} knobs
on the \emph{one} source. Each knob value is a distinct compiled kernel (CubeCL monomorphizes on comptime
state), so a small variant set is enumerated, timed once per $(\text{device},\text{op},\text{shape})$,
and the winner cached. There are no forks: the source is one; only the chosen comptime tuple differs per
device. Crucially the knobs never change the numerics --- the per-row reduction is the same fold in the
same order for every schedule --- so \emph{every variant is byte-identical}, and the tuner buys speed
without ever risking a numeric change.

\subsection{The algebra: Variant, Tuned, Tuner}
Three orthogonal values (Listing~\ref{lst:tune}): a \code{Variant} is a named schedule --- a closure
\code{f(iters) -> (output, ms)} that both benchmarks (many iters) and executes (one iter); \code{Tuned}
is the fluent builder collecting variants for one $(\text{op},\text{shape-key})$; \code{Tuner} is the
cache, an in-memory $(\text{device},\text{op},\text{key})\to\text{winner}$ map backed by a per-device
on-disk file. The \code{Tuner} is a plain value over an explicit cache root, so it is fully unit-testable
offline --- no GPU, no env, no globals; the process-wide default roots at the XDG cache dir and the disk
format is one TSV line per key (no serde, no new dependency).

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust,caption={The production surface. One source per op, autotuned per
device, no forks. On a cache hit only the winner launches (once, to produce its output); the losers are
never run.},label={lst:tune}]
let y = Tuned::new("rms_norm", format!("rows={rows},n={n}"))
    .variant("b64_r1",  |it| rms_norm_tuned_bench(&c, x, w, rows, n, eps, 1,  64, it))
    .variant("b128_r1", |it| rms_norm_tuned_bench(&c, x, w, rows, n, eps, 1, 128, it))
    .variant("b256_r1", |it| rms_norm_tuned_bench(&c, x, w, rows, n, eps, 1, 256, it))
    .variant("b64_r2",  |it| rms_norm_tuned_bench(&c, x, w, rows, n, eps, 2,  64, it))
    .run(&c);  // times each once per (device,op,shape), caches the winner, thereafter runs it
\end{lstlisting}

\subsection{The variant tables and their proof}
Two ops ship autotuned (Table~\ref{tab:variants}). Both variant sets are proven on the CPU runtime:
every schedule is bit-identical to the oracle (rms\_norm: byte-for-byte equal, the fold order is
invariant; \dpa{}: byte-identical across variants, within $2\times10^{-3}$ f32-reorder of the
per-element oracle), and the cache mechanism is exercised directly --- a miss benchmarks all four and
records the winner, a second call is a hit that benchmarks zero, a fresh \code{Tuner} over the same
directory reloads the winner from disk and skips timing, and a changed variant set is treated as a miss
and re-tuned rather than trusting a stale name. Timing on the CPU proves the select/cache machinery; a
GPU run additionally makes the \emph{winner} meaningful.

\subsection{Why first-party}
CubeCL ships an autotuner; it was studied and declined for three concrete reasons, not NIH. It is
re-exported only from \code{cubecl\_runtime}, so adopting it would force a \emph{second} engine crate
into a facade architected so only \code{Cargo.toml} names the engine; its persistent cache lives under
\code{target/} rather than the XDG path this layer requires; and its generic-associated-type input
machinery is a large surface for ``pick the fastest of $N$ named closures.'' The concepts above are
deliberately the same as CubeCL's, sized to this crate and pointed at XDG --- a thin analog, not a new
philosophy.

\section{Mechanism III: intrinsic islands}
\label{sec:island}

\subsection{Killing the whole-kernel escape hatch}
Sometimes one inner loop needs a target-specific instruction or idiom. The wrong response is to fork a
parallel per-backend kernel --- that is the product, back again. The right response is a scoped,
target-gated \code{island!} block \emph{inside} the one DSL kernel (Listing~\ref{lst:island}). The kernel
keeps one signature, one launch contract, one CPU oracle, one test. The \code{default} arm is the
normative semantics; accelerated arms are optimizations that must equal it.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rust,caption={An island in a \dpa{} matvec. The accelerated arm issues the
hardware 4-wide int8 dot; \code{default} --- the normative oracle, taken by CPU and any unlisted target

\subsection{The mechanism: target selection at comptime}
CubeCL compiles the same target-agnostic IR for every backend; at \code{\#[cube]} expand time there is no
``which backend am I'' string, only a capability set. But the backend brand \emph{is} known on the host,
via the runtime name. So an island is selected by a \code{\#[comptime] target: Target} tag the launch
layer derives from the runtime (\code{Target::of::<R>}) and threads in; the \code{\#[kernel]} macro
rewrites \code{island!\{...\}} into a comptime \code{match target}. A comptime match over a const
scrutinee lowers \emph{only} the selected arm --- every backend compiles exactly its own arm, nothing
else. The tag is out of the authoring surface twice over: the branch site names backends, not a target,
and the launch wrapper fills the tag, so callers pass only data.

\subsection{The oracle contract, tested}
The CPU runtime is always tagged \code{Target::Cpu} --- a variant no accelerated arm claims --- so on CPU
every island resolves to \code{default}. This makes the CPU runtime the single bit-exact oracle for every
island, and the test proves it directly: run the kernel on CPU with each accelerated tag \emph{forced},
and it must equal the \code{default} result byte-for-byte. The shipped test drives all five accelerated
tags (\code{Cuda, Rocm, Metal, Vulkan, WebGpu}) through the CPU runtime and asserts each is byte-identical
to \code{default} and to the plain-Rust reference, and separately asserts the production launch (which
derives the tag from the runtime) resolves to the oracle on CPU without the caller naming a target. An
accelerated arm is thus never trusted; it is proven equivalent to the normative arm before it can ship.
This is the designed seam for the one case the rest of the paper cannot make portable ---
block-quantized tensor-core GEMM (\S\ref{sec:negative}).

\section{The CPU runtime as bit-exact oracle, and its CI}
\label{sec:oracle}

The three mechanisms share one foundation: \textbf{the CPU runtime is the single bit-exact reference for
every backend.} It is not an accelerator target --- it is the oracle. Every op ships with a plain-Rust
reference whose scalar semantics the device code mirrors line-for-line; the fusion engine's device
morphism table mirrors its host table; every island's \code{default} arm \emph{is} the CPU semantics; and
every autotune variant is checked against the oracle. Because a Map's arithmetic is index-local and the
CPU runs it in program order, the CPU result is the definition, and a GPU backend differs only by f32
re-association where a reduction reorders a sum --- a difference bounded and explained, not a bug to
hunt. The op library --- \code{rms\_norm}, \code{layer\_norm}, \code{rope} (both NeoX and GPT-J
conventions), \code{sdpa} (online/flash softmax), the GDN \code{conv1d}/\code{gating}/\code{scan}, the
quant matvecs, and the fusion chains --- is gated this way across 36 CPU tests.

This law is enforced continuously. A nightly GitHub Actions workflow
(\code{.github/\allowbreak{}workflows/\allowbreak{}gpu-tests.yml}) runs a three-backend matrix on self-hosted boxes --- one runner
label per backend: \code{cuda} $\to$ spark (NVIDIA GB10, sm\_121), \code{rocm} $\to$ evo (AMD Strix Halo
gfx1151, ROCm~7.x), \code{metal} $\to$ dbc (Apple M4~Max) --- each running
\code{cargo test -p hanzo-ml} \code{-{}-features <backend>} \code{-{}-test-threads 1} behind a
GPU-guard step. Serial
execution and the guard keep a single GPU consumer at a time per backend, which is not fastidiousness but
necessity: concurrent ROCm inits race in the kernel-fusion driver and wedge it until reboot. The
practical result is that ``the same op has the same numbers on every backend'' is a checked invariant,
not an aspiration.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We separate what is a committed, reproducible test (bit-exactness) from what is a measured benchmark on
named hardware.\footnote{Bit-exactness figures are enforced by the committed CPU/GPU tests cited.
Throughput and latency figures are session benchmarks on the named machines: evo (AMD Strix Halo,
gfx1151, RADV/Vulkan and ROCm~7.13), spark (NVIDIA GB10, sm\_121), dbc (Apple M4~Max, Metal).
Benchmarks are kernel-only (buffers created once, $N$ dispatches enqueued back-to-back, one final
sync).}

\subsection{Cross-backend bit-exactness}
The op library is bit-exact against the CPU oracle for every family it targets, and --- the part that
matters --- the DSL \emph{dispatches} bit-exact through real backend runtimes, not merely compiles
(Table~\ref{tab:bitexact}). The ROCm figure is the falsifiable end-to-end proof that the
cubecl-hip$\to$ROCm~7.13 seam works: the same \code{rms\_norm} source lowered through cubecl-hip and
dispatched on gfx1151 (device query via the R0600 bindings $+$ HIP-RTC module compile $+$ launch $+$
readback) matches the CPU oracle to $\text{max\_rel}=2.43\times10^{-7}$, well under the $2\times10^{-3}$
gate --- pure f32-reorder noise, not a decode defect.

\subsection{Portability costs nothing: the DSL matches and beats hand-tuned}
The central risk of any portable DSL is a performance downgrade. It does not materialize on the
bandwidth-bound kernels that dominate decode. A tuned single-source \dpa{} Q8 matvec ---
\code{Vector<i8,4>}$\to$\code{OpSDot}, one thread block per output row with its threads striding the
$k/4$ groups so adjacent threads read adjacent 4-byte packed weights (coalesced 256-byte transactions),
partials tree-reduced in shared memory --- measures \textbf{196~GB/s} on evo (gfx1151, Vulkan, $8192^2$,
cache-busting), which is \textbf{118\% of the $\sim$166~GB/s hand-tuned \dpa{} kernel} it would replace
and $\sim$77\% of the $\sim$256~GB/s DRAM roofline, bit-exact against the CPU oracle. The na\"ive
one-thread-per-row kernel that started at $\sim$17~GB/s was memory-\emph{uncoalesced}, not DSL-limited;
the fix was two standard decomplected steps --- coalescing and reduction --- both expressed in the DSL.
The DSL reaches the same hardware integer-dot instruction and, with standard coalescing, \emph{beats} the
hand-written code.

\subsection{A migration that improved performance}
Retirement is real, not hypothetical, and it can be a strict win. Two hand-written Vulkan kernels were
drained on \code{main}: the DSL block-per-row \code{rms\_norm} (270~GB/s) and \code{softmax} (268~GB/s)
replaced na\"ive hand-written \code{.comp} shaders, each now the production kernel with its \code{.comp}
deleted, dispatching bit-exact through the engine's real Vulkan pipeline. The sharpest example is the
fused residual-add\,+\,RMSNorm: the DSL \code{add\_rmsnorm} twin ran \textbf{7.1\x{} faster} than the
incumbent hand-written shader ($166.5\to23.4$~ms at $4096^2$ on RADV) \emph{and} was bit-exact ---
$s=x{+}\text{res}$ byte-identical, $y$ within $4.8\times10^{-7}$. A ``uniformity'' change turned out to
also be a large speedup, because the incumbent was na\"ive and the DSL block kernel is coalesced. The
migration is faster-or-equal at every step by construction: a kernel is retired only when its DSL twin is
bit-exact \emph{and} at least as fast.

\subsection{The discipline cuts both ways}
The gate also \emph{declines} migrations. Where a hand-tuned kernel is already optimal --- the Vulkan
quant matvec, Metal's \code{simd\_sum} reductions, the fused \code{rope\_norm} epilogue --- agents built
bit-exact DSL twins, measured, and correctly did \emph{not} swap: a uniformity-only change with zero
speedup and real regression risk is churn, not migration. ``One source'' is the goal; a bit-exact,
no-faster twin is kept as the portable fallback for backends that lack the tuned version, and the tuned
peak stays the specialized fast path. We only ever add coverage and correctness.

\subsection{The ROCm-7 seam, as an engineering note}
Shipping the DSL on AMD required fixing two independent upstream defects without bumping CubeCL (0.10 is
the newest published; no version escapes either). First, cubecl-hip-sys selects its FFI bindings from
\code{hipconfig --version}; ROCm~7.13 reports patch 99004, past the newest shipped binding, so upstream
emitted a feature with no bindings module and every symbol went undefined. The fix is a vendored
\code{[patch.crates-io]} fork whose \code{build.rs} clamps an unknown-newer patch to the latest shipped
binding --- ABI-safe because AMD versions every changed symbol (R0600) and 7.13 still maps
\code{hipGetDeviceProperties} to that revision. Second, cubecl-hip's server uses a \code{MultiStream}
gated behind a \code{std}-only cfg the \code{hip}/\code{rocm} features do not pull, so the ROCm feature is
declared \code{rocm = ["cubecl/hip", "cubecl/rocm", "cubecl/std"]}. Both are recorded because a portable
DSL lives or dies on the least-loved backend building cleanly.

\section{A negative result: int8 tensor-core MMQ}
\label{sec:negative}

Not everything collapses. The universal prefill gap Paper~06 documents (dense $0.6$--$0.9\x$ of
llama.cpp on every backend) is a tensor-core-MMQ gap: llama quantizes the activation to q8\_1 (int8) and
contracts block-quantized weights against it on the matrix cores. We asked whether that kernel, too, can
be written once in \hk{} and lowered to the hardware int8 matrix cores instead of hand-written four times
(CUDA \code{fast\_mmq}, Vulkan \code{mul\_mm\_q8\_mmq}, ROCm \code{qmmq}, Metal simdgroup). The answer,
honestly, is \emph{not through the portable API}, and the reason is structural rather than a missing
optimization.

\subsection{The structural cap}
CubeCL exposes two routes to the int8 matrix cores. The high-level WMMA API (\code{cmma::Matrix},
$16{\times}16{\times}16$, i8$\to$i32) loads and stores accumulator fragments automatically, one tile at a
time --- but \emph{the fragment layout is opaque}. Block-quantized GEMM needs a per-32-block f32 scale
applied to the integer accumulator, and int8 accumulation is only valid \emph{within} one constant-scale
32-block (this is inherent to the format, not a kernel choice). With an opaque fragment the only place to
apply the scale is a store$\to$sync$\to$reload round-trip through shared memory, \emph{once per block}.
That serialization is the wall. Measured on GB10/Blackwell, the high-level WMMA MMQ reaches only
$\sim$7.6~TFLOP/s, roughly one-tenth of the hand-tuned kernel, and --- the diagnostic that proves it is
sync-bound, not occupancy-bound --- a \emph{better-tiled} version (a $32{\times}64$ output tile, 8 warps,
operands staged to shared memory once per block and reused across the tile, which fixes occupancy and
operand reuse) is \emph{slower}, because the only remaining tax is exactly the per-block scale round-trip
through the opaque fragment, and better tiling adds more of them.\footnote{The structural argument (opaque
fragment $\Rightarrow$ per-block shared-memory scale round-trip; manual MMA with exposed lane map as the
higher-ceiling path) is documented in the committed \code{mmq.rs} probe. The TFLOP/s figures and the
tiled-is-slower comparison are session measurements on spark (GB10); the probe commit records CUDA
verify/bench as pending, so we report them as measured, not as a committed test.}

\subsection{The viable route is per-backend anyway}
The higher-ceiling path is manual MMA (\code{cmma::MmaDefinition}, \code{mma.sync} at
$16{\times}8{\times}32$), where the fragment element-to-lane map \emph{is} exposed, so the block scale can
be applied in register with no shared-memory round-trip. But that path is per-backend by nature ---
CubeCL 0.10's int8 MMA is CUDA-only (the probe kernels are literally \code{\#[kernel(targets(cuda))]}) ---
so writing it does not collapse the product; it writes one more backend-specific kernel, just in Rust.
The honest conclusion is therefore: \textbf{block-quantized MMQ stays hand-written behind benchmark
gates.} This is not a defeat of the thesis but a boundary the thesis anticipates. The decode matvecs,
norms, RoPE, attention, GDN, and the entire elementwise fabric --- the bulk of the ledger and the whole
of the divergence-bug surface --- collapse. The one op that resists is exactly the one for which
\S\ref{sec:island} built the seam: an island holds the hand-written tensor-core arm behind a normative,
CPU-checkable \code{default}, so even the un-collapsed kernel keeps one signature, one oracle, one test.
The negative result names the boundary and the mechanism that contains it.

\section{The collapse ledger}
\label{sec:ledger}

Table~\ref{tab:dup} is the denominator; this section is the protocol that drains it. Roughly 85\% of the
$52{,}345$ lines are a deletable target --- the hand-written arithmetic duplicated across backends, as
opposed to the genuinely peak, genuinely per-backend kernels (the MMQ GEMMs of \S\ref{sec:negative}) that
stay behind islands. Draining is never all-or-nothing and never a downgrade, because it is governed by a
\textbf{verify-then-delete protocol}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Coverage first.} The DSL fills the entire $22\,\text{types}\x5\,\text{backends}$ matrix from
one source. Cells that were missing or buggy become uniform immediately.
\item \textbf{The gate.} A hand-tuned kernel is retired for its DSL twin \emph{only} when the twin is
(a)~bit-exact against the shared CPU oracle and (b)~within performance noise on a kernel-only benchmark
--- in practice $\ge 97\%$ of the incumbent's throughput. The number decides, per type, per backend.
\item \textbf{Delete on parity.} As each twin passes, its hand-written copies are deleted and a
per-$(\text{op},\text{type},\text{backend})$ registry flips that cell to the DSL, keyed by the gate
result. The product drains to a sum one measured cell at a time, with a working, faster-or-equal engine
at every intermediate state.
\end{enumerate}
Executed instances already on \code{main}: the Vulkan \code{rms\_norm}, \code{softmax}, and
\code{add\_rmsnorm} shaders deleted in favor of bit-exact DSL twins (\S\ref{sec:eval}, the last a 7.1\x{}
win); the ROCm-7 seam opened so gfx1151 dispatches from the same source; and the DAG fusion cutting the
GDN launch storm 9$\to$5. Instances correctly \emph{not} executed: the Vulkan quant matvec and Metal
\code{simd\_sum}, where the DSL twin was bit-exact but not faster. The ledger is a scoreboard with a
gate, not a rewrite.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

Portable kernel authoring and kernel fusion are both well-trodden; the specific claim we make is narrow
and we state it precisely. \textbf{Triton} gives a tile-level Python DSL that lowers to GPU code, but
fusion is the programmer's job (you write a fused kernel) or the compiler's inside a larger stack; it has
no notion of fusion as an algebraic law over an op class. \textbf{XLA} fuses HLO graphs via
producer-consumer heuristics and a cost model; \textbf{torch.compile}/TorchInductor captures an FX graph
and fuses by a scheduler's heuristics. Both fuse by \emph{analysis over a graph}, not by a fusibility
predicate that is a function of the op's type. \textbf{Halide} and \textbf{TVM} pioneered separating the
\emph{compute} from the \emph{schedule} and autotuning the latter --- this is the nearest neighbor of our
autotune mechanism (\S\ref{sec:tune}), and we claim no novelty for schedule/compute separation itself;
what differs is that our schedule knobs are \code{\#[comptime]} monomorphizations of one kernel proven
byte-identical, so autotuning is guaranteed not to perturb numerics. \textbf{Burn}'s \code{burn-fusion}
is the closest relative to our fusion engine: it records an op stream and JITs fused elementwise kernels
over the \emph{same} CubeCL substrate. The difference is where fusibility comes from --- \code{burn-fusion}
traces Burn tensor ops and applies its own cut/epilogue policy, coupling to Burn's tensor abstraction;
ours is the functor law over an op \emph{class}, one predicate, on our own tensor type, composing with our
hand-tuned peak kernels. \textbf{Mojo} is a language for kernels, not a fusion-as-composition model.
Across all of these, we are unaware of a system that presents kernel fusion \emph{as} morphism composition
--- the functor law $\mathrm{map}(g)\circ\mathrm{map}(f)=\mathrm{map}(g\circ f)$ as the mechanical rule
and Reduce/Scan as the algebraic fences --- with a bit-exact CPU oracle as the definitional semantics.
That framing, and the three mechanisms it organizes, is the contribution; the substrate (CubeCL) and the
individual ideas (portable IR, autotuning, oracle testing) are prior art we build on and cite.

\section{Conclusion}

The kernel layer is a Cartesian product of backends, quantization types, and operations, hand-written in
five shading languages, and it is the breeding ground for the divergence bugs that cost the most to
chase. \hk{} collapses that product to a sum on one idea --- fusion is composition --- extended by three
mechanisms that each attack an axis: autotune folds the schedule fork into comptime knobs proven
byte-identical; islands hold the rare per-backend idiom behind a normative, CPU-checkable arm; and the CPU
runtime is the single bit-exact oracle every backend is measured against, nightly. The evidence is that
portability costs nothing where it counts (a single-source \dpa{} matvec at 118\% of hand-tuned, a shipped
migration 7.1\x{} faster and bit-exact), that the collapse is real and reversible (a verify-then-delete
ledger with kernels already deleted on \code{main}), and --- stated as plainly as the wins --- that one
op resists: block-quantized tensor-core MMQ is structurally sync-bound through the portable WMMA API and
stays hand-written behind exactly the island seam we built for it. What remains is a disciplined,
measured migration whose engine is faster-or-equal at every step.
