\section{Introduction}

Columnar analytics engines such as ClickHouse~\cite{clickhouse} deliver
high scan and aggregation throughput, but a production deployment is
dominated by two operational costs that the literature on query execution
rarely addresses: the resident memory of an idle-to-moderately-loaded server,
and the size and start-up cost of the binary itself. In a multi-tenant hosting
setting --- where the operator runs thousands of small-to-medium instances
rather than a handful of large ones --- these constants, not asymptotic query
complexity, determine density and therefore unit economics.

\emph{Datastore} is Hanzo's analytics database. It is a fork of ClickHouse,
which is distributed under the permissive Apache~2.0 license, re-branded and
re-engineered with three goals: (i) a substantially smaller resident footprint
and binary at equal or better query throughput; (ii) a transport layer free of
the gRPC/protobuf stack; and (iii) a build that is reproducible and
self-hosted end to end. This paper reports the engineering and the
measurements behind those goals.

Our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item A build pipeline combining profile-guided optimization (PGO),
  ThinLTO, and the BOLT~\cite{bolt} post-link optimizer that produces a
  server binary with $49$--$53$\,MB resident set --- less than half the
  $\sim\!116$\,MB of the upstream official binary (which itself ships
  PGO+LTO+BOLT) --- while matching or exceeding its throughput on
  aggregation and filter workloads (Section~\ref{sec:eval}).

  \item A root-cause analysis (Section~\ref{sec:xray}) showing that the
  decade-old difficulty of running BOLT on AArch64 ClickHouse binaries is, in
  our case, not a deficiency of \texttt{llvm-bolt} per se but a consequence of
  default-enabled XRay instrumentation. XRay emits composed
  \texttt{R\_AARCH64\_PREL64} relocations (we observe a single group of
  $62{,}692$ entries in \texttt{xray\_instr\_map}) whose lowering is
  unimplemented on the AArch64 path, surfacing downstream as an invalid OAuth
  signature on the self-hosted runner rather than as a BOLT error. Disabling
  XRay --- the correct choice for a release binary --- removes
  $\sim\!45$\,MB of sled sections \emph{and} unblocks BOLT.

  \item A transport substitution (Section~\ref{sec:arch}) that removes gRPC,
  protobuf, and ArrowFlight and carries ClickHouse-native columnar blocks as
  opaque payloads over ZAP, a Cap'n-Proto-derived RPC with promise
  pipelining. On dependent-call chains, end-to-end latency becomes flat in
  chain depth: a $63\times$ improvement at depth~64 over a request-response
  baseline at $0.5$\,ms round-trip.
\end{itemize}

A guiding constraint throughout is that Datastore must remain wire- and
SQL-compatible with ClickHouse, so that the migration of an existing
deployment is a change of image reference rather than a change of client.
Section~\ref{sec:eval} confirms apples-to-apples parity: built with identical
flags, the fork and upstream are statistically indistinguishable in memory and
throughput, isolating every reported gain to the optimization pipeline rather
than to the fork.
