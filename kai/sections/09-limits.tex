\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{The bootstrap models are not good at our questions.}
The checkpoints were trained by others on other data. Their authors report
0.362 zero-shot accuracy for the base English checkpoint on their
typed-decisions benchmark~\cite{laya}, and on our permission questions all three
were close to uniform (Table~\ref{tab:perm}). The design tolerates this,
because a model it has not promoted cannot act, but it does not hide it: until
a checkpoint is trained on Hanzo's traffic, Kai's contribution to the agent
loop is a record, not a decision.

\paragraph{Calibration is assumed, then checked.}
Every threshold, abstention rule and flip probability in this paper treats the
model's probabilities as meaning what they say. The multilingual checkpoint
ships with no fitted temperature, the English one with a fitted value the
runtime refuses to apply, and the upstream authors report a confidently wrong
English checkpoint off Latin script. Programs are bound to calibrations for
this reason, and E4 is the check.

\paragraph{The act head is not used.}
It was constant in our run. It may carry signal on the distribution it was
trained on; we have not found it.

\paragraph{Adversarial state beyond the lattice.}
Proposition~\ref{prop:tighten} protects constraints, not preferences. An
adversary who controls a state can push a routing program toward a cheaper
model, a context program toward hiding a chunk, or a completion program toward
declaring a task done early. Those programs need deterministic backstops of
their own, such as a completion that requires a passing test run in the
transcript, and we have specified them only for completion. An adversary can
also use Kai to cause friction deliberately, asking and refusing where the
policy would allow; that is a denial-of-service against the operator's
attention, bounded by the policy but not prevented by it.

\paragraph{The study model has assumptions.}
Additive aggregation requires preferentially independent criteria; the
variance formula and the normal approximation assume cells are answered
independently, which fails when two cells read the same evidence; the weight
flip is exact only when the minimiser stays in the simplex. A package states
which of these it relies on. The illustration in Section~\ref{sec:flip} is
constructed, and no part of Section~\ref{sec:studies} has yet been run on a
real study.

\paragraph{Nothing here is measured at scale.}
The recorded run has nine states. The latency, cost and accuracy figures that
would justify replacing generative calls with typed decisions in production are
the subject of Section~\ref{sec:eval-plan} and are not in this paper.
